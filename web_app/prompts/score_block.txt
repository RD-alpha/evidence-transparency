INPUT FORMAT
The <document> is not the policy document itself. It is an extract: every
sentence of the document was first classified into one aspect of the policy
argument, or discarded as belonging to none, and the sentences for this aspect
were gathered here. Sentences belonging to another aspect are scored
separately and are not shown here, so do not penalise the document for them.
A minority of sentences are discarded as belonging to no aspect, so absence from
this extract is not proof of absence from the document.

The <text> holds the sentences, one <line> each. A marker such as
"(Reference: 12)" or "(Link: 37)" means that sentence carries citation 12 or
37. The <references> block holds the footnote text of the references those
sentences cite, and the <links> block the URLs they link to. An empty
<references> and <links> block means these sentences carry no citation at all.

Score only what is shown. Do not assume supporting detail exists somewhere you
cannot see, and do not assume it is absent: treat what is not stated as not
stated. Do not raise or lower the level to hedge against what the extraction
may have missed.

HOW TO SCORE
Judge the extract as a whole, against the levels above, and give the highest
level it fully reaches. Each level also requires everything below it.

OUTPUT
Return structured JSON with two fields, in this order.
1. reasoning: roughly 100-200 words, written for a reader of the final report
   rather than for another analyst. Quote the specific lines and reference
   numbers you relied on and state the level they reach. Give the strongest
   counter-example to the level you chose, and end with what the document would
   have to add to reach the next level up.
2. score: the level 0-3, decided after the reasoning above.
