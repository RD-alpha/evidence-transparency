WHAT COUNTS AS EVIDENCE
Evidence is anything the document offers as grounds for a claim: a statistic or
figure, a research finding, a named report, survey or dataset, consultation
responses, or the experience of a comparable policy elsewhere. Citation is not
needed until level 2. Evidence that the document uses to support a claim reaches
level 1 whether or not it is cited; the missing citation is what keeps it from
level 2. This includes unnamed research, such as "research has found..." or "we
know that...": a finding is offered as grounds, so it reaches level 1, but with
no source identified it cannot reach level 2. Level 0 is for claims asserted
with no evidence at all, or evidence named with no indication of what it shows
or supports.

Properly cited, for level 2, means the claim carries a reference or link that a
reader can follow to its source. A source named only in the prose, with no
reference or link ("a 2009 report found..."), stays at level 1. So does a
generic pointer that leaves the reader to work out which item supports which
claim, such as a footnote reading "see Annex for sources".

The department's own primary evidence -- responses to its consultation, data it
collected, or its own analysis or modelling -- has no outside source to cite. It
counts as cited, and can reach level 2, when the document says how it was
obtained: for example who was consulted, how many responded and what they were
asked, or what the analysis covered. If the document gives the result but not
how it was obtained, it stays at level 1. Mark each theme that relies on primary
evidence (see OUTPUT) and say in its justification whether the method is clear.

A law or regulation that the document cites to explain why an option was chosen
or ruled out is evidence of that constraint. One cited only to define a term, or
to describe the current rules, is not. Targets, commitments, and descriptions of
what the policy will do are not evidence in themselves.

Reasoning is not evidence. An argument for a decision or a diagnosis with no
statistic, finding, source or primary evidence behind it is level 0, however
plausible. Where the document does offer evidence, clear reasoning from that
evidence to the claim is part of what makes it "linked" at level 2.

LEVELS (each level also requires everything below it)
0 - No evidence provided for the diagnosis, or evidence is named with no
    explanation at all of how it has been used.
1 - Evidence is mentioned, with some explanation of how it has been used.
2 - As in level 1, but the supporting evidence is linked to the relevant parts
    of the policy, properly cited, and you could find the source. "Linked" means
    the chain of reasoning is clear: the document shows which claim the evidence
    supports, the figure and its direction, and what it is taken to show about
    the problem -- not merely that a citation sits beside a sentence.
3 - As in level 2, but the evidence base is also assessed, and uncertainties and
    contradictory information are acknowledged. This must be the document doing
    the assessing.
