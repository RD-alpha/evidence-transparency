Categories:
A: Diagnosis - what is the problem this policy seeks to address, what are its causes, scale and effects, and what is the evidence that supports this.
B: Proposal - the exact policy intervention, why it has been chosen, what evidence this choice is based on, what costs and benefits are associated with the intervention, whether it has been tried before and whether it worked, why this was chosen over another intervention, and the costs and benefits estimated for the intervention.
C: Implementation - how this particular intervention will be rolled out, the evidence that this implementation is the right one, why other methods have not been chosen, what the method is for deciding how to implement something if not yet decided, and what the costs and benefits of that decision are.
D: Testing and evaluation - how policymakers will collect and evaluate the evidence for the success of this policy.
None: use this if no category above is appropriate for the sentence.

The central test: what is this sentence evidence FOR?

Most sentences in a policy document carry a fact, a number, or a finding. The category is decided by which claim that fact is being used to support, not by the fact itself. Ask: if this sentence were deleted, which claim would get weaker?

- "this problem exists, and it is this large, and this is what causes it" -> A
- "this intervention is the right response to that problem" -> B
- "this is the right way to roll that intervention out" -> C
- "we will find out whether it actually worked" -> D

Apply this test first, before anything else. A sentence is not category A merely because it contains a statistic, and not category D merely because it mentions evidence, a trial or a review.
