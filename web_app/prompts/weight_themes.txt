You are reading an extract from a policy document about $intervention. The
problem it is meant to address is: $problem.

This extract covers one aspect of the policy argument, $aspect. Before
the evidence behind it is scored, you are deciding which of its themes the
argument for this aspect rests on.

WHAT THE DOCUMENT SHOULD EXPLAIN FOR THIS ASPECT
$should_explain

INPUT FORMAT
The <document> is not the policy document itself. It is an extract: every
sentence of the document was classified into one aspect of the policy argument,
and the sentences for this aspect were then grouped into themes. Each <theme>
holds a <name> and its sentences in <text>, one <line> per sentence; its
sentences attribute gives how many lines it has. Sentences belonging to other
aspects are handled separately and are not shown. The document's citations,
references and links have been removed on purpose.

THE TASK
Mark each theme as core or not core.
- core: the argument for this aspect rests on the theme. It is one of the main
  supports of the case: take it away and the case for this aspect would no
  longer stand, not merely be less complete.
- not core: everything else, including themes that do bear on the problem or on
  a should-explain point but that the case could stand without, as well as
  background, procedure, legal or administrative detail, and passing remarks.

HOW TO DECIDE
Bearing on a should-explain point is not enough to make a theme core: most
themes in an aspect do. Compare the themes against each other and pick out the
ones the rest of the argument leans on. In most aspects that is one to three
themes. Mark more only when the argument genuinely rests on several separate
supports, and then say in each reason why the case could not stand without it.

Judge each theme by what it is about and the role it plays in the argument, not
by how well supported it is. You cannot see the evidence and should not try to
infer its quality: a theme of bare assertion can be core, and a theme full of
figures can be not core. Do not mark a theme not core because it reads like a
single example or because it is short, and do not mark it core only because it
is long. Treat a large share of the aspect's sentences as a sign that the
document builds its argument on a theme, but not as a substitute for judgement.

Expect at least one theme to be core unless the extract holds no argument for
this aspect at all, and say so in the reasons if that is the case. Where one
line of argument is split across two themes, judge each on its own: both can
be core.

OUTPUT
Return structured JSON with one entry per <theme>, in the order given:
- theme: the theme's name, exactly as in its <name>
- reason: one or two sentences saying what part of the argument the theme
  carries, naming the problem or the should-explain point it bears on, if any
- weight: core or not core
