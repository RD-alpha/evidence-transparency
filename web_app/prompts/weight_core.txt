You are reading an extract from a policy document about $intervention. The
problem it is meant to address is: $problem.

This extract covers one aspect of the policy argument, $aspect. Before
the evidence behind it is scored, you are deciding which lines of argument the
case for this aspect rests on.

WHAT THE DOCUMENT SHOULD EXPLAIN FOR THIS ASPECT
$should_explain

INPUT FORMAT
The <document> is not the policy document itself. It is an extract: every
sentence of the document was classified into one aspect of the policy argument,
and the sentences for this aspect are listed here in document order, one per
line, each starting with its sentence code. Sentences belonging to other aspects
are handled separately and are not shown. The document's citations, references
and links have been removed on purpose.

THE TASK
Identify the core lines of argument for this aspect, and list the sentences
that make up each one.
- A core line of argument is one of the main supports of the case: take it away
  and the case for this aspect would no longer stand, not merely be less
  complete.
- Everything else is not core, including sentences that bear on the problem or
  on a should-explain point but that the case could stand without, as well as
  background, procedure, legal or administrative detail, and passing remarks.
  Do not list these: sentences you leave out are treated as not core.

HOW TO DECIDE
Bearing on a should-explain point is not enough to make a line of argument
core: most of the extract does. Pick out what the rest of the argument leans
on. In most aspects that is one to three lines. Return more than three only when
the argument genuinely rests on more separate supports, and then say in each
reason why the case could not stand without it.

A line of argument is the claim and the sentences that make it: the statement
of the claim, and the sentences that explain, develop or support it. Include
the sentences that do this wherever they fall in the extract.

Judge by what the sentences say and the role they play in the argument, not by
how well supported they are. You cannot see the evidence and should not try to
infer its quality: bare assertion can be core, and a passage full of figures can
be not core. Do not leave a line out because it is short, and do not make it
core only because it is long.

Return at least one line of argument unless the extract holds no argument for
this aspect at all; in that case return none.

OUTPUT
Return structured JSON with one entry per core line of argument:
- name: a short name for the line of argument
- reason: one or two sentences saying why the case for this aspect rests on it,
  naming the problem or the should-explain point it bears on, if any
- ids: the sentence codes that make it up. List only codes that appear in the
  extract, each copied exactly; never list a range of codes.
