S0003 It sets out the government’s revised plans to establish a new, bespoke professional regulator for social workers that will be known as Social Work England.
S0012 Government therefore wants to establish a new specialist regulator for social work that is focused on protecting the health and wellbeing of those vulnerable children, families and adults who need social work support.
S0013 It will have a sole focus on the unique needs and role of this profession and will bring a positive emphasis to social work.
S0014 Our initial plan was to set up a new regulator as part of government - an Executive Agency of the Department for Education - but with joint governance between that Department and the Department of Health.
S0015 Concerns have been raised about this approach in the House of Lords and in our conversations with a wider range of stakeholders.
S0016 We have considered these concerns and have accepted that while the intention of setting up a bespoke regulator is the right one, our plan of having the regulator as part of government was not the best way to deliver this aim.
S0017 There is significant support for our ambition to improve the practice of social work and to raise the status of the profession.
S0018 Everyone we have spoken to recognises the pivotal role that social workers play and agrees that more needs to be done to acknowledge this.
S0019 However, we have also heard concerns about the proposed approach to deliver this.
S0020 The chief concern has been that the proposals placed the regulation of social workers too close to government, rather than providing independent oversight of the profession.
S0021 There were mixed views about whether the regulator should also have an improvement role, but on balance, there was a sense that separation between regulation and improvement was preferable and in line with practice elsewhere in the health and care professions.
S0022 We have also been asked to review the framework nature of the Bill as those we spoke to wanted greater detail about the new body set out on the face of the Bill.
S0023 In response we have tabled a number of amendments.
S0025 The scope of Part 2 of the Bill has now been refined in light of the feedback we have heard.
S0026 The role of the new body will be clearly focused on professional regulation and this will be reflected in its objectives.
S0027 The new body will be known as Social Work England and it will have a clear remit around maintaining a register of social workers, running fitness to practise hearings and setting standards for initial education and training and professional standards, including standards of proficiency and continuous professional development.
S0028 We intend that, over time, Social Work England will work to raise the minimum standards across all these aspects.
S0029 Government is keen to promote the development of post-qualification specialist practice and believes that Social Work England can play a positive role in this.
S0030 In the first instance, it will take on regulation of Best Interest Assessors and Approved Mental Health Professionals, and over time may have a role in supporting efforts to develop post-qualifying specialisms for accredited child and family practitioners.
S0031 We recognise the importance of maintaining the appropriate distance between Social Work England and government.
S0032 It has never been our intention that ministers be involved in the day to day running of the regulator or to give the government the power to make decisions about individual social workers or training courses and agree this should not be the case.
S0033 To make this clearer, the government is laying amendments to establish Social Work England on the face of the Bill.
S0034 It will be a separate legal entity in the form of a Non-Departmental Public Body (NDPB), in line with the approach in the Devolved Administrations.
S0035 The Bill now states that there will be a new body and that it will be known as Social Work England; that there will be a Chair and a Board appointed by the Secretary of State; that the Secretary of State may appoint the first Chief Executive; and that going forward this will be the responsibility of the Board with Ministers merely approving the appointment.
S0036 It is our intention that the Board will, in line with principles of good modern governance, provide strong and capable leadership.
S0037 It will consist of a majority of non-executive members and will be able to act on behalf of the public in securing public protection.
S0038 Social Work England will be able to employ the staff it needs, and have a clear set of responsibilities which it will be accountable for delivering.
S0039 The amendments also remove the option of using delegated powers to appoint another regulator in the future.
S0040 As an NDPB, there will be clear separation between the regulator and ministers and a clear role for government in holding the new regulator to account for overall delivery of its functions.
S0042 Alongside this, we are proposing that the Professional Standards Authority (PSA) take a formal oversight role of the regulator.
S0043 It will include carrying out an annual review of how the new regulator discharges its functions and will have a power to refer fitness to practice cases to the High Court where it feels that the action of Social Work England is not sufficient for the protection of the public.
S0044 This will provide important independent oversight of its work.
S0045 In essence, this maintains the PSA’s role in oversight of the professional regulation of social workers.
S0046 This makes sure that the new regulator is in line with other professional regulators in the health and care sector by providing an independent assessment of whether Social Work England is meeting its core function of the protection of the public.
S0047 The Bill now sets out the objectives of the regulator on the face of the Bill which is to ensure the protection of the public through its regulatory functions, further ensuring consistency with other health and care regulators.
S0048 Following the Law Commission report, all current health and social care regulators have a similar objective focused on protection of the public. ** (Reference: 1)
S0049 The proposed amendments to the Bill therefore reflect this.
S0051 The overarching objective of the regulator in exercising its functions is the protection of the public.
S0052 The pursuit by the regulator of its overarching object involves the pursuit of the following objectives:
S0054 To protect, promote and maintain the health, safety and wellbeing of the public;
S0056 To promote and maintain public confidence in social workers in England;
S0058 To promote and maintain proper professional standards and conduct for Social Workers in England.
S0063 In light of the concerns we have heard about the need for the new regulator to be independent, and in particular, for standards to be set objectively and with input from the sector, we are proposing to amend the Bill to reflect this.
S0064 Amendments set out that the new regulator will set professional and education and training standards, in consultation with the sector, and that Ministers will be responsible for approving them.
S0065 It will be for Social Work England to ensure that these standards are adhered to.
S0066 We have listened to concerns that there has not been enough engagement in developing proposals for the regulation of social workers.
S0067 Over the summer we have undertaken further engagement with representatives from across the sector and Peers to understand better the range of concerns and to develop the changes we are now putting forward.
S0072 As part of that investment we are committed to supporting the establishment of Social Work England and will ensure that any set-up costs will not fall on social workers themselves.
S0073 There will be a one off cost to move to the new arrangements and ongoing costs will be met through a combination of fees (as the current system does) and Government funding.
S0074 We estimate that one off costs to set up the body will be £10m and this will be met by the Government.
S0075 Government will also contribute funding to the running costs of Social Work England over the rest of this Parliament of up to £16m. We have no plans at this time to require the body to be self-financing and as with the current system Social Work England will be required to consult on any proposal to raise fees.
S0076 The costs of establishing and running the new body will be set out in full in an impact assessment that will be published alongside the secondary legislation.
