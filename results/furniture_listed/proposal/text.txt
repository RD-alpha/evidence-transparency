S2209 All of these can be of different weights and varieties.
S2211 Unless the liner is cotton then all of them will melt – the cotton will smoulder – and the liner therefore offers no protective function.
S2213 Concern was expressed that the regulations – because they apply in the UK only – could leave little incentive to innovate (i.e. in terms of reducing the need for chemicals).
S2214 It was noted that the sofas from one large retailer have different burning behaviours in different member states with the sofas sold in UK and Ireland having the highest levels of fire resistance (because of their stricter regimes).
S2215 Other Member states do have furniture regulations but they are for contract (non-domestic) furniture only.
S2217 The innovation need would be removed if the need for brominated flame retardants was reduced or removed in the regulations.
S2218 This has happened in California where due to the ban on certain FRs, innovation to develop new and safer FRs has stalled.
S2219 There has been some innovation in coating fabrics.
S2222 The panel agreed that Filling 1 could be removed from the proposal for simplification purposes, although this may not suit all manufacturers.
S2223 The removal of Filling 1 (ie testing directly over foam) makes the specification of the combustion modified foam (CM) less important as there will be an additional layer (the fibre wrap) between the cover and the foam.
S2225 The earlier testing for the technical paper which informed the 2014 consultation, showed that there would be a limited number of cover fabrics which would pass the current test and fail the new test.
S2227 The introduction of a fibre wrap would lower the amount of FRs needed for synthetic fibres.
S2228 With polycotton mixture fabrics, more FRs would be needed to pass the new test.
S2229 But there is not a linear relationship between the amount of polyester or cotton in a fabric and the degree of burning.
S2231 The current match test is generally perceived as repeatable but the introduction of a fabric wrap in testing could introduce an additional variable and it was felt more testing is needed to explore this.
S2232 The earlier testing for the technical paper used a single specification for the fibre wrap as it was focussed on reductions in chemical use in the cover, rather than the impact of the wrap itself.
S2234 The panel then talked through the draft specification proposed for the fabric wrap.
S2235 It was mentioned that the technical paper put forward a hypothesis for the purpose and effect of the fabric wrap that was not necessarily representative of what happens in real life testing and needed further evidence – such as testing different fabrics with different fabric wraps – to support it.
S2237 It was felt that ± 5mm was an appropriate level of tolerance in the specification but that this left a wide variation in the thickness of the fabric wrap that could be used in the test from 10mm to 25mmm which could have a noticeable effect on test results.
S2238 It was agreed that more testing would give a more informed view of the impact of different thicknesses and types of fibre wrap on fire resistance levels.
S2243 The panel agreed that the cigarette test could probably be removed for 100% synthetic fabrics as they would simply melt away and not smoulder.
S2246 This might also be more realistic as many smouldering fires are in fact started by other sources (e.g. overheating electrical chargers).
S2250 BIS felt this made it important to find a definition for a protective cover (or protective cover/lining combination) to allow a potential route to exempt the components underneath from the match test.
S2252 The panel agreed that hole formation was not a satisfactory method by which to determine whether a cover is protective or not.
S2253 (This was in part due to repeatability – because the fabric would not necessarily behave the same way if tested throughout the roll.)
S2254 A list of protective fabrics would be difficult to keep up to date and would need to be specific as to weight and structure of fabric; but there was also concern that not many fabrics could be said to be protective The panel discussed whether other methods were possible to determine whether the integrity of the cover fabric had been compromised.
S2255 One possibility was whether a temperature sensitive paint marker could be used underneath the cover to test whether heat had broken though the cover fabric.
S2256 Alternatively, the cover could be tested over non-combustion modified foam (NCMF) as in the current match test - but this would mean a double match test – one with the new CM foam approach and one with the NCMF (the current method) which was not attractive due to cost.
S2260 It was noted that under the previous proposal, BIS had suggested the idea of a “protective” component which in theory would allow manufacturers to wrap their flammable components in cotton or wool.
S2261 The panel thought this would introduce rather than remove risk.
S2266 2. Primary covers - a primary cover is the cover closest to the filling.
S2267 In the current regulations, pillows and cushions can be tested with primary covers to account for different types of fillings.
S2269 Synthetic materials could be defined as non-cellulose based, i.e. not plant material based.
S2270 Any material that chars, will smoulder.
S2271 The panel noted that there are a great many natural/synthetic fabric blends, e.g. polycotton, and they contain varying ratios of cellulosic to synthetic fibre, with some containing enough cellulosic fibre to smoulder.
S2274 It was suggested that a specific test for stretch covers is abolished and no difference made between them and permanent covers – any new test should apply to all covers, regardless of type.
S2276 The panel then revisited the proposal to remove the cigarette test for fabrics that passed the match test.
S2277 The match test is concerned with speed of ignitability and exposure to a high heat source.
S2278 Smouldering, as in the cigarette test, is based on long exposure to a low heat source.
S2279 These behave very differently, and therefore it doesn’t always follow that covers which pass the match test will automatically pass the cigarette test.
S2281 However, it was agreed that the less than 1% failure rate figure for the cigarette test on visible covers was accurate.
S2282 And it was suggested that the cigarette test for non-visible covers is entirely redundant as there were no known failures.
S2283 It was pointed out that this assumes that the types of covers by manufacturers will not change with the new regulations.
S2284 Different covers might become more popular which could mean that the currently low failure rate for the cigarette test could increase.
S2285 7. Removal of the cigarette test would reduce testing costs to industry.
S2286 Carrying out a match test costs about two-thirds of the cost of combined match and cigarette testing.
S2288 A new standard for a smoulder and ignition test could be developed using something other than a cigarette, for example, an electronic substitute such as cartridge heaters.
S2292 The panel discussed whether it was necessary to specify the density of the fibre wrap in the regulations.
S2293 There were mixed views as to how significant an effect this would have on the ultimate outcome (i.e. the safety of the product).
S2295 Should it be decided to specify fibre wrap then 20-25mm density would be appropriate and wrap is usually defined by weight per square metre.
S2296 If density is not specified then the outcomes of testing could be affected, particularly at the extremes.
S2297 It would be appropriate to give a range for wrap weight rather than a point estimate.
S2299 In the US fire barriers are used, e.g. fibre glass material, between the cover and the filling in order to avoid the use of flame retardants.
S2300 A fibre wrap as proposed in the test would not constitute a fire barrier.
S2302 Development of a test to work out the protectiveness of a cover would be extremely helpful.
S2304 The panel discussed some criteria of assessing when a test for protectiveness had been failed, such as charring of materials and the use of paint markers.
S2305 Fabrics that pass testing can still form small holes (dependent on the mix of synthetic and natural fibres) and split.
S2306 Presently, there is no measurement of splitting, testers just observe it.
S2307 It is not reproducible as fabrics do not always split.
S2309 The California smoulder test measures smoulder time and char rate.
S2310 The latter is easier to measure than hole formation.
S2312 The panel thought there were a number of options that could be used for testing the protectiveness of a cover including use of the existing match test for that specific function.
S2314 The logic of testing liners is that it is material close to the cover fabric and influences how the cover fabric behaves.
S2318 The work carried out for the August 2014 paper did not include liners.
S2319 The test methods used in development of the liner test had shown there was a question
S2320 about time limits for flame application and smouldering and whether these should be different or the same.
S2322 The panel suggested that generally, liners should be treated like any other material that is currently unregulated.
S2323 All internal materials (components close to the cover), including liners, should be regarded as being the same for testing purposes.
