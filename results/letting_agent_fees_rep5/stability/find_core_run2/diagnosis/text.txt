S0005 The private rented sector is an important part of our housing market.
S0006 It has almost doubled in size in the decade to 2014-15, housing 4.3 million households in England.
S0007 The sector now represents 19% of all households, up from 11% in 2004-05. 3. ** (Reference: 1)
S0008 Letting agents are engaged by private landlords to let and manage rental accommodation on their behalf.
S0009 Good agents provide a valuable service in ensuring that properties are safe, compliant and professionally managed; they help landlords comply with their legal responsibilities and help tenants secure safe and good quality homes.
S0011 The duties of letting agents might include finding tenants, collecting rent, and responding to queries from tenants (for example, in relation to repairs).
S0012 Landlords pay fees to letting agents for carrying out these duties on their behalf.
S0013 Letting agents also charge fees to tenants for a variety of reasons, including seeking references, inventory services and contract negotiations.
S0014 5. Letting agent fees to tenants vary considerably and can run into hundreds of pounds.
S0015 Tenants have little control over these fees because the agent is appointed by the landlord.
S0016 It is also not simple for tenants to understand and compare agent fees since there is significant variation in the way that agents charge for their services.
S0030 There is no overarching statutory regulation of private sector letting or managing agents in England and agents are not required to join a professional trade association, although many have done so voluntarily.
S0031 However, the Government has taken legislative action to protect the consumers in the sector.
S0033 The Government regulated in October 2014 to require letting and managing agents to be members of a Government approved redress scheme.
S0034 The three redress schemes are Ombudsman Services: Property; the Property Redress Scheme; and the Property Ombudsman.
S0035 The redress schemes are independent bodies to which tenants and landlords in the private rented sector can complain about the service that they have received from a letting or managing agent and seek a resolution.
S0037 Transparency requirements were implemented by the Consumer Rights Act 2015.
S0038 They require letting agents to publicise a full tariff of their fees, to make clear whether or not they are a member of a Client Money Protection scheme and of which redress scheme they are a member, prominently in their offices and on their website.
S0039 A fine of up to £5,000 can be levied against agents who fail to comply.
S0041 Further action will be taken over the course of this Parliament, including using powers in the Housing and Planning Act 2016 to crack down on the minority of rogue landlords and agents by introducing civil penalties of up to £30,000, banning orders and a database of rogue landlords and property agents.
S0043 The Government also took powers in the Housing and Planning Act 2016 to allow for regulations to require letting and property management agents to have Client Money Protection.
S0044 Client Money Protection, which involves client money being held in a separate, protected bank account, ensures that landlords and tenants are reimbursed in the event of a letting or managing agent going into administration or where the agent misappropriated their money whilst it was in the agent’s custody or control.
S0046 Baroness Hayter and Lord Palmer of Childs Hill conducted a review of Client Money Protection for the Government and recommended that the Government use its powers taken in the Housing and Planning Act 2016 to make Client Money Protection mandatory for all letting and managing agents that handle client money.
S0047 Mandatory membership of a Client Money Protection scheme will ensure that each and every agent is offering the same level of protection, giving tenants and landlords the financial protection that they want and deserve.

S0052 Landlords are required by law to:

S0092 Guarantor checks – for tenants who do not have a regular and steady

S0094 This guarantor will be responsible for any rental payments owed by the tenant or any damage that is inflicted to the property by the
S0095 tenant.

S0098 Reference checks – some agents may ask for references from previous
S0099 landlords or agents with whom the tenant has rented previously.
S0100 These references help to assure the landlord of the tenant’s ability to carry out their rental obligations.
S0101 • Tenancy Agreement Negotiation - once reference checks have been satisfactorily completed, the letting agent will prepare the tenancy agreement, which sets out the terms and conditions of the tenancy, including how much rent will be paid, the deposit and any specific or individual arrangements such as pets or break clauses.
S0102 The letting agent will negotiate between both the landlord and the tenant until a set of mutually agreed terms and conditions are confirmed.
S0103 • Deposit Protection – under all Assured Shorthold Tenancies, the tenant’s deposit must be protected in one of the three Government-backed Deposit Protection schemes.
S0104 Deposits must be put in a scheme within 30 days of receipt and the tenant must be provided with the details of how the deposit has been protected, including the name and contact details of the scheme, how to get the deposit back and what to do if there is any dispute over the deposit.
S0105 Ensuring that a deposit is in a protected scheme is the responsibility of a landlord or any agent acting on their behalf.
S0106 • Safety Checks – it is a legal requirement that all properties are safe and free from hazards.
S0107 This is a requirement on landlords that is often carried out on their behalf by letting agents.
S0108 Letting agents may organise gas certification, electrical safety checks and fire safety checks and provide evidence of these to the tenant.
S0109 • Moving in – letting agents will organise and coordinate a tenant’s move in date to the property, providing keys and relevant information and potentially helping to arrange utilities and council tax.
S0110 Agents will issue certain documents such as the Government’s ‘How to Rent Guide’, a copy of the latest Energy Performance Certificate and proof that smoke alarms and, where appropriate, carbon monoxide detectors have been tested.
S0111 These are requirements on a landlord, carried out by the agent on their behalf.
S0112 • Inventory - An inventory and schedule of condition of the property may be undertaken either by the agent or via a third party upon moving into the property.
S0113 A comprehensive inventory is needed for the benefit of both the landlord and the tenant; it helps resolve any issues around the return of deposits.
S0114 • Amending the tenancy agreement - a variation of the tenancy agreement may be necessary, for example if there is a change of tenant in a shared tenancy property.
S0115 This will require the letting agent to carry out the relevant reference checks on the new tenant, amend the tenancy agreement and update any deposit arrangements.
S0116 • Renewal – at the end of the initial agreed tenancy period the tenant may choose to renew their tenancy.
S0117 Subject to agreement from the landlord, the letting agent
S0118 will renegotiate the tenancy agreement, for example considering any rent increases or break clauses.
S0119 • Early termination of the tenancy – a tenant may choose to end their tenancy prior to the end of the initial agreed tenancy period.
S0120 This will require an agent to find a replacement tenant and carry out the relevant exit processes for the existing tenant.
S0121 A landlord may choose to evict a tenant from the property and may require the support of an agent with this process.
S0122 • Moving out - letting agents may coordinate a tenant’s move out from the property, collecting keys, ensuring relevant meter checks have been done and that the property has been cleaned as required.
S0123 A check out inventory will normally be undertaken before the deposit is returned with any deductions negotiated through the agent.
S0124 Once any disputes have been resolved, the agent will return the deposit to the tenant.
S0125 The agent may also be asked to provide a reference for the tenant for future rentals.
S0127 Property management services provided by agents during a tenancy may include repairs and maintenance, replacing lost keys and managing late rental payments.
S0128 These services can be prompted by a request from either the tenant or the landlord.
S0130 Fees are charged by agents to both landlords and tenants at a number of points during the tenancy.
S0131 The services provided by letting agents and their charging arrangements vary considerably.
S0134 At the outset of a tenancy, tenants are generally requested to provide a month’s rent in advance as well as a deposit.
S0135 There is currently no limit on the amount of deposit that can be requested from a tenant but it is generally equivalent to between one and two months’ rent.
