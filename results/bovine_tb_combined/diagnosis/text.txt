S0009 1.1 Bovine TB is the most pressing and costly animal health problem in the UK.
S0010 The disease threatens our cattle industry and presents a risk to other livestock and also to wildlife species (mainly badgers), pets and humans.
S0070 This is important as it is estimated that more than 20% of all breakdown herds in the HRA retain infected animals after all skin testing has been completed and restrictions have been lifted.
S0071 This substantial residual herd infection contributes to the high rate of recurrence we see in the HRA where nearly 60% of breakdowns occur in herds that have sustained a breakdown in the previous three years.
S0072 On the other hand, a substantial proportion of new herd breakdowns in the HRA are triggered by direct or indirect contact between badgers and cattle.
S0087 6.1 Tracing involves the identification of cattle that have been moved off a breakdown holding within a defined period of time.
S0088 Any animals that may have moved from a TB breakdown herd before the imposition of TB restrictions must be traced and, if still alive, TB tested to minimise the risk of disease spread to other herds.
S0089 Initial skin tests of cattle on breakdown holdings are read using severe interpretation (see below), but the cattle traced from the herds are normally tested using standard interpretation.
S0090 This is an inconsistency which the Government wishes to put right.
S0091 6.2 The test used is the comparative tuberculin skin test, which measures an animal’s immune reaction to injections of both avian and bovine tuberculin.
S0092 Depending on the degree of reaction to the test an animal is identified as: test positive (a reactor); test negative; or an inconclusive reactor (in which case a re-test is required).
S0093 Because of the limitations of the test and the nature of the response to the bovine TB bacterium we may miss 20 to 25 per cent of TB-infected cattle using the standard interpretation of the test (these animals are known as false negatives).
S0096 6.3 It is inconsistent to apply the standard interpretation to cattle traced from a herd that is undergoing testing at severe interpretation.
S0098 7.1 If an animal’s skin test result falls between the measurements for it to be classed negative or positive, the test result is deemed inconclusive and the affected animal is placed under movement restrictions, isolated and scheduled for a further skin test.
S0099 In extensive or explosive TB breakdowns IR animals not designated as reactors through
S0100 severe interpretation of the skin test can be removed at the discretion of APHA as ‘direct contacts’ without a re-test – if that happens compensation is paid.
S0101 7.2 The soonest an IR can be re-tested is 60 days after the previous test.
S0102 If the second test is negative APHA will lift the movement restrictions and the animal can re-join the herd.
S0103 If the IR is either positive or inconclusive for a second time the animal will be considered test positive and will be compulsorily slaughtered and, if it has not already happened, restrictions would be placed on the herd.

S0119 8.1 Applications for licences to allow the movement of cattle from one TB restricted herd to another are subject to a satisfactory veterinary risk assessment.
S0120 This APHA risk assessment will incorporate information known about the two herds (e.g. their TB testing history) at the time of the assessment.
S0121 However the understanding of the disease status of the herds may subsequently change and could potentially undermine the assessment.
