S0115 i. We calculate the additional funding available to spend on adult social care at a national level, combining the 2% council tax flexibility for adult social care and the additional funding for the improved Better Care Fund.
S0117 We then calculate the share of that national amount which each authority with responsibility for social care would receive if it were distributed according to the 2013 adult social care relative needs formula.

S0122 v. S0188 3.4.3 For a local authority, the measure of rates income in the rates retention scheme is its share of “non-domestic rating income” as defined in regulations and captured on the NNDR3 form.
S0189 Therefore, the objective of the revaluation adjustment is to identify and isolate the amount by which non-domestic rating income in the authority will change purely due to the revaluation.
S0190 Once identified, the relevant shares of these amounts can then be deducted or added to the tariffs or top ups to cancel out the impact of revaluation.
S0192 An authority sees its local share of non-domestic rating income grow due to revaluation from £100m to £122m.
S0193 It has a £20m top up.
S0194 With no adjustment, the £22m increase would feed through into extra revenue for the authority.
S0195 To compensate we need to deduct £22m from the £20m top up giving a tariff of £2m.
S0197 In practice we do not believe it will be possible to directly measure the changes in non-domestic rating income arising from the revaluation alone.
S0198 Instead we propose to estimate the change using a proxy (derived from gross rates payable).
S0199 The proxy will be calculated by comparing the local authority’s rateable value before and after the revaluation to quantify the impact the revaluation has on the tax base.
S0200 This effect will then be applied to the authority’s gross business rates income before the revaluation to produce a figure for the council’s income post-revaluation.
S0201 Subtracting this post-revaluation income figure from the pre-revaluation income and apportioning it according to the authority’s share of business rates income under the scheme will produce the change that needs to be made to its tariff or top up in order to ensure that it has, as far as practicable, the same income after the revaluation as it had before.
S0202 3.4.6 We propose to make this adjustment in three stages: on a provisional basis in the 2017-18 settlement; on a final basis with a reconciliation in the 2018-19 settlement; and finally, by cancelling out the reconciliation in the 2019-20 settlement.
S0206 3.4.8 The adjustment for revaluation is for those impacts discernable at the time of the revaluation only (and captured in the rateable values on 31/3/17 and 1/4/17 as we look at them on that day).
S0207 Changes to the revaluation which occur after 1 April
S0208 2017 by virtue of backdated amendments or appeals (including those backdated to 1/4/17) fall within the operation of the rates retention system in the normal way.
S0214 3.5.3 To ensure that an increase in the “local share” of business rates is fiscally neutral at the point of change, the Government and pilot areas are exploring:
S0215  ending entitlement to certain grants and other funding streams  devolving additional responsibilities to pilot areas and  adjusting existing business rate tariffs and top ups.
S0219 The calculation of the value of an additional local share will be based on the pilot areas’ Baseline Funding Levels and notional Business Rates Baselines (i.e. the 2013/14 Business Rates Baseline, uprated by subsequent changes to the small business rates multiplier).
S0220 This will preserve the integrity of the existing scheme by ensuring that the value of the additional share is exclusive of any growth (or decline) in business rates achieved by pilot authorities since 2013-14.
S0221 3.5.6 For each pilot authority, the value of funding streams and the new responsibilities rolled in to the business rates retention system (hereinafter referred to as “Grant”) will be added to the existing Baseline Funding Level to create a “new” Baseline Funding Level for the authority.
S0222 3.5.7 There are two different options on offer to pilot authorities in 2017-18.
S0224 a) the local percentage share of business rates is increased only by the value of the “Grant” rolled-in or
S0225 b) the local percentage share of business rates is increased to 100% and, to the extent that the “Grant” rolled in is not equivalent to 100% of local business rates, tariffs and top ups are adjusted appropriately.
S0226 3.5.8 Under option a) above, the additional percentage share of business rates will be equal to:
S0227 Grant / notional Business Rates Baseline x existing % share of business rates
S0228 3.5.9 This additional percentage share will be added to the existing percentage share to give the percentage to be retained in 2017/18.
S0229 3.5.10 Under option b) above, the notional Business Rates Baseline of each authority will be adjusted to reflect the increase in the local share to 100%. ** (Reference: 4)
S0230 The adjustment to the notional Business Rates Baseline is equal to:
S0231 Business Rates Baseline / existing % share of business rates x 100%
S0232 3.5.11 For option B, the difference between the new Baseline Funding Level and the new notional Business Rates Baseline will be the tariff or top up for 2017-18.

S0260 In order to ensure, as far as is practicable, the impact of the 2017 revaluation is neutralised in the rates retention scheme, DCLG propose to make the following adjustments to tariffs and top ups.
S0261 This will be in addition to the normal inflation adjustments made to tariffs and top ups.
S0263 Ideally, we would directly measure the change in non-domestic rating income between 31/3/17 and 1/4/17 (the 2 days either side of the revaluation).
S0265  being the last day of the financial year and a Friday there could be many alterations on 31 March 2017 with retrospective effect (typically appeals credited on that day).
S0266 The authority would, therefore, need to take a view as to how much of their provision they have released to fund those appeals and how much actually reduced non-domestic rates income for that day
S0267  we would need to consider whether changes to the provisions for the year should also be allocated to the income for the day and if so how
S0268  there will be other accounting adjustments in income which would need to be reflected for the day – such as bad debt.
S0270 In practice we believe this would be too complicated and too sensitive to refunds, backdated alterations and subjective allocations of accounting adjustments.
S0271 Instead the Government will use a proxy to measure the change in rates income due to the revaluation.
S0272 We will then apply that proxy (as a factor) to the non- domestic rating income for 2016/17.
S0273 That would then give an estimate of the change in in non-domestic rating income between 31/3/17 and 1/4/17.
S0275 As a proxy, we propose to adopt the change in gross rates payable before all reliefs and accounting adjustments between 31 March 2017 and 1 April 2017.
S0276 Essentially, this is just the rateable value x small business multiplier for those 2 days.
S0278 This proxy will not itself reflect changes in reliefs such as Small Business Rate Relief (SBRR).
S0279 However, the proxy will be applied to non-domestic rating income which will already reflect the degree to which local income is affected by reliefs.
S0280 So the adjustment will, by adopting non-domestic rating income, reflect the position of those authorities with a lot of SBRR.
S0282 Where the proxy could be less accurate is where reliefs in a local area change due to the revaluation out of line with the proxy for all properties in the area.
S0283 Many percentage awards of reliefs will not change due to the revaluation (e.g. charitable
S0284 relief) as their eligibility and percentage is not driven by rateable value - so for them the proxy should work as well as it does for any property.
S0285 And some have little impact (e.g. the empty property relief lower threshold) and some reliefs are insignificant (rural rate relief).
S0287 In principle, there is more scope for the revaluation factor for small properties receiving SBRR to be different to all properties.
S0289  it is not possible to say what that revaluation factor for SBRR properties would have been.
S0290 The Government has made large changes to the eligibility of SBRR for 1 April 2017.
S0291 Had the Government not done anything to SBRR we would in any case have adjusted the thresholds for the relief and the Small Business (SB) multiplier.
S0292 In the absence of the need for that decision we cannot isolate the effect of the Budget change to SBRR from the revaluation change  we believe the impact would still be marginal.
S0294 Therefore, we believe adopting a proxy based on the gross change in rateable value using the Small Business multipliers is a practical and proportionate method.
S0296 We will also separately pay section 31 for the SBRR changes in the 2016 Budget including the increase in the threshold for the SB multiplier and will consider that payment in the context of the revaluation adjustment.
S0298 We will not have actual 2016/17 non-domestic rates income or rateable values at 1 April 2017 in time for the 2017/18 settlement.
