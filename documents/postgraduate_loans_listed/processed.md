1. Executive Summary
S0054 At Budget 2015, the Government announced its intention to review how to broaden and strengthen support for postgraduate research and subsequently published a consultation on support for postgraduate study. This consultation sought views and evidence on support for postgraduate study, including on whether to offer income contingent loans for doctoral level degrees, of up to £25,000 over the lifetime of the degree. ** (Reference: 1)
S0055 The consultation generated over 400 responses and the Government published an external analysis of these responses in November 2015. ** (Reference: 2)
S0056 This analysis showed strong support for the introduction of a new loan scheme for postgraduate doctoral students where this complements existing funding mechanisms and supports excellent research.
S0057 Respondents were keen to ensure that support is maintained for existing funders that serve postgraduate research, such as through the Research Councils.
S0058 The Government response to the consultation on postgraduate study was published in November 2015. ** (Reference: 3)
S0059 The Government confirmed its commitment to introduce income contingent loans for doctoral study and its intention to offer these loans in addition to grant funding.
S0060 At Budget 2016, the Chancellor announced the Government’s intention to launch a technical consultation on the detail. ** (Reference: 4)
S0061 The key terms of the proposed loan are as follows:
S0062 • Eligible individuals would be able to borrow up to £25,000. •
S0063 The maximum course length would be six years.
S0064 •
S0065 The loan is intended as a contribution to the cost of study, rather than to specifically cover tuition fees or living costs.
S0066 •
S0067 The loan would be available to individuals studying any Level 8 doctoral qualification.
S0068 •
S0069 The loan would be available to eligible students who are not in receipt of a Research Council studentship (including fees only awards). ** (Reference: 5)
S0070 •
S0071 The loan will not be available until academic year 2018/19.
S0072 It is intended that the loan would be available to eligible UK nationals ordinarily resident in England and other students that meet the loan’s eligibility and residency requirements in place at the time.
S0073 Full details of eligibility will be published in advance of the Student Loans Company opening applications for academic year 2018/19.
S0074 • Eligible students ordinarily resident in England would be able to take their loan to any UK university offering a doctoral programme.
S0075 The Government’s objectives in introducing doctoral loans are to:
S0076 • Support take-up in doctoral study by providing access to finance to those not currently in receipt of a Research Council studentship.
S0077 •
S0078 Broaden and strengthen the UK’s research base.
S0079 •
S0080 Benefit the economy by increasing the supply of workers with the high level skills needed to meet demand from employers and the research base, and to stimulate an innovation-led economy.
S0081 This consultation seeks views on the specific details of the proposed income contingent loan for doctoral study, focusing on loan terms, eligibility and implementation.
S0082 Higher Education provision is a devolved matter for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and therefore individuals ordinarily resident in those countries would not be eligible for the loan.
S0083 However, since the loan is portable to UK HEIs we also welcome the views of interested stakeholders from around the UK.
S0084 We will consider all responses and use these to inform the design of the doctoral loans.

3. About doctoral education
3.1 The doctoral education landscape
3.1.a What is a doctoral degree?
S0119 A doctoral degree is classified as a Level 8 qualification in the Quality Assurance Agency’s Higher Education Framework for Higher Education Qualifications. ** (Reference: 6)
S0120 These include Doctor of Philosophy qualifications, usually abbreviated as PhD or DPhil, as well as professional doctorates, such as Doctor of Education (EdD) and Doctor of Business Administration (DBA).
S0121 The most common form of doctorate in the UK is the PhD or DPhil, despite the emergence of professional doctorates which have evolved in response to the needs of different professions.
S0122 The main focus of a doctoral candidate’s work is their contribution to existing knowledge in their subject discipline through original research or the original application of existing knowledge or understanding.
3.1.b What is a professional doctorate?
S0123 HEFCE research shows that professional doctorates differ from other doctoral programmes, particularly PhDs, in respect of their purpose and research focus. Professional doctorate programmes aim to develop the capacity to make a significant, original contribution to professional practice through research, and are a research based element of professional training and the development of practitioners. ** (Reference: 7)
S0124 The research within a professional doctorate programme is rooted in the professional practice of the candidate and should have a significant impact on their professional practice.
3.1.c What is the value of doctoral level skills?
S0125 The economy needs a highly skilled, research-driven workforce to enable businesses to grow and for innovation to thrive.
S0126 Employers value doctoral graduates’ deep specialist subject knowledge, excellent research and analytical skills, their capacity for forward thinking, and ability to bring fresh perspectives to problem solving.
S0127 These skills enable
S0128 doctoral graduates to innovate and develop new or improved goods, processes or ways of working.
S0129 A doctorate is the first step towards a career in research, and doctoral students are vital to the higher education sector through their contribution to original research and also as researchers of the future.
S0130 The UK’s position as a global research leader is dependent on the quality of its university-based research and UK higher education institutions are crucial contributors to the domestic research base as a whole. ** (Reference: 8)
S0131 Doctoral students provide critical mass within research groups, and are seen as engines of innovation, undertaking higher risk projects.
S0132 These students also help foster innovation through collaboration and engagement with industry.
3.1.d How does our doctoral research performance compare to other countries?
S0133 The UK has a strong foundation in research that has made it one of the best global performers in the achievement of doctoral level qualifications, and the Government is committed to increasing the strength of this world class research base.
S0134 The UK is the fourth largest producer of PhD graduates globally; of the research-intensive countries, the US, China, and Germany each produced more PhD graduates than the UK in 2011.
S0135 The growth in PhD graduates for the UK is within the range of those seen across most research-intensive countries. ** (Reference: 9)
S0136 In science and innovation, the UK is a world leader and has the most productive science base in the G7.
S0137 The UK has overtaken the US to rank first amongst comparable major research nations for Field Weighted Citations Impact (FWCI). With only 0.9% of the global population, 4.1% of researchers and 3.2% of R&D expenditure, the UK accounts for 6.4% of research articles, 11.6% of citations and 15.9% of the most highly-cited research articles. ** (Reference: 10)
S0138 The UK also has a high rate of return for our investment in science and research - at 20%.
S0139 In addition, £1 of public expenditure leverages an average of £1.36 private investment which generates even greater returns for the UK. ** (Reference: 11)
3.2 Funding doctoral study
S0140 The complexity of funding postgraduate research, including doctoral study, was highlighted in the responses to the Consultation on Postgraduate Study.
S0141 Funding for university research is currently delivered through the ‘Dual Support System’.
S0142 Dual Support combines project funding for excellent research proposals, which is forward- looking and assessed through peer review (delivered by the Research Councils), with formula based quality-related research funding that rewards performance retrospectively based on peer review and proven impact from the research (delivered by HEFCE).
S0143 The system sustains a dynamic balance between research that is strategically relevant and internationally peer reviewed, and research which is directed from within institutions.
S0144 Doctoral students can be funded by Research Councils, by their institutions, or from a number of other sources, but it is hard to be definitive on the finance options currently available to doctoral students.
S0145 In general, funding for doctoral programmes is likely to come from six main sources:
S0146 1. Research Council studentships and support; 2. University studentships, grants and support;
S0147 3. Industry supported scholarships and awards;
S0148 4. Charities and non-profit organisations; 5.
S0149 Employer support; and 6. Self-funding by the student.
3.3 Rationale for government intervention
3.3.a Addressing employer demand for high level skills
S0150 The economy needs a highly skilled, research-driven workforce to enable businesses to grow, and for the UK to be the best place to innovate.
S0151 However, the costs and length of time it takes to secure a doctorate make most employers understandably reluctant to invest upfront despite their desire to recruit doctoral students.
S0152 Employers value doctoral students’ deep specialist subject knowledge, excellent research and analytical skills, their capacity for forward thinking, and ability to bring fresh perspectives to problem solving.
S0153 These skills enable doctoral graduates to innovate, develop new or improved goods, processes or ways of working.
S0154 Research
S0155 from the Research Councils found that three quarters of employers who took part said their loss would have a business critical or significant impact on operations. ** (Reference: 12)
3.3.b Unmet student demand
S0156 The Government already invests actively in the development of the doctorate population through the Dual Support System because of their contribution to the research base and high level skills.
S0157 However, the Government is confident that even with this investment there is still unmet demand for places on doctoral programmes:
S0158 • Research Council studentships are highly contested, suggesting that more students would take up doctoral places than are currently able to do so.
S0159 •
S0160 Students meeting Research Council residency requirements are eligible for a Research Council studentship that includes a maintenance stipend.
S0161 In 2014/15, around 37% of students beginning doctorates who met the residency requirements for such a maintenance stipend received one. ** (Reference: 13)
3.3.c Addressing demand from HEIs
S0162 There is also demand for doctoral students from Higher Education Institutions.
S0163 HEIs see doctoral students as strategically important to their research capacity and output.
S0164 They provide critical mass within research groups where they support innovation and higher risk projects, and are also instrumental in building international and inter-sectoral collaborations as well as delivering industry-funded research.
S0165 Many institutions regard them as integral to, and supportive of, the culture of being a university.
S0166 Over the longer term, institutions also see postgraduate research students as important in contributing to institutional Research Excellence Framework (REF) performance and underpinning REF publications.
S0167 Because of this, institutions particularly target doctoral growth in areas of existing research excellence.
S0168 In addition, institutions recognise the importance of doctoral students as a potential pipeline of high-quality researchers for their future research capacity.
3.3.d Broadening and strengthening the research base
S0169 The Government wants to build on its existing strength in research, to broaden and strengthen the research base.
S0170 We recognise that the UK is a world leader in science and innovation and has the most productive science base in the G7; the UK has overtaken the US to rank first amongst comparable major research nations for Field Weighted Citations Impact (FWCI) A PhD is the first step towards a career in research and doctoral graduates are vital to the higher education sector through their contribution to original research and also as researchers of the future. ** (Reference: 14)
S0171 The UK’s position as a global research leader is dependent on the quality of its university-based research and UK higher education institutions are crucial contributors to the domestic research base as a whole. ** (Reference: 15)
S0172 The provision of doctoral loans, which institutions can use to create packages of support for doctoral students, will allow a broad range of institutions to strengthen their research programmes.
3.3.e Closing the finance gap for individuals
S0173 Research organisations and higher education institutions in the UK are not regulated in terms of the fees they charge for PhD programmes.
S0174 Fee levels vary across universities, but the RCUK indicative fee level for doctoral study in 2016/17 is around £4,000 per year. Variation in the total cost of a PhD or doctoral degree can come from both the course fees, which may vary by institution or subject, and the location of study. ** (Reference: 16)
S0175 The cost of living during doctoral study is strongly dependent on the location of the university.
S0176 For example, Oxford and London are at the top end of living cost, at around £14,000 per year. ** (Reference: 17,18)
S0177 The cost of living in cities such as York, Sheffield or Aberystwyth is lower, at around £11,000 per year. ** (Reference: 19,20,21)
S0178 In these cities the total cost over a three year course, assuming the indicative fee level of £4,000, would be around £45,000.
S0179 We therefore estimate that average cost of study at doctorate level could range from £45,000 (for a three year degree in lower cost location) to £73,000 (for a four year degree in a higher cost location).
S0180 The loan amount would cover around a third to a half of the total costs of a doctorate, and is intended to be a contribution towards the costs of pursuing doctoral study.
S0181 It is not intended to cover all the associated costs of studying at this level.
S0182 We consider this balances the incentives, to ensure students will continue to make considered and informed decisions about pursuing further study at this level.
S0183 We welcome the funding Higher Education Institutions, charities and employers already offer doctoral students, and would encourage them to consider how these funding packages can be used alongside doctoral loans.
S0184 The additional source of funding provided by doctoral loans also presents HEIs with an opportunity to develop new, innovative packages of funding.
4. Key principles of the new doctoral loan
S0185 The policy objectives: The Government’s objectives are to support take-up of doctoral study by providing access to finance to those not currently in receipt of a Research Council studentship; to broaden and strengthen the research base; and to benefit the economy by increasing the supply of workers with the high level skills needed to meet demand from employers and the research base and stimulate an innovation-led economy.
S0186 Affordability and value for money: The proposed terms of the loan are designed to achieve the policy objectives detailed above, whilst being affordable and offering value for money for the taxpayer.
S0187 Income contingent: As a safeguard for individuals and to ensure repayments are achievable and affordable, repayments must be based on income - similar to the principle applied in the undergraduate and master’s student loans models.
S0188 Contribution towards costs: The intention is to make finance available as a contribution to the costs of pursuing doctoral study, including fees and living costs.
S0189 The Government is not seeking to provide finance to cover all the costs associated with undertaking a doctorate.
S0190 This is similar to the approach adopted for the master’s loan.
S0191 We consider this contribution approach provides the right balance of support for students while ensuring they continue to make considered decisions about pursuing education at this level.
S0192 The Government also wishes to reduce the risk that employers will withdraw existing support from their employees wishing to study at this level.
S0193 Better than commercial rates or on better terms: The Government intends to structure the loan product so that it is exempt from consumer credit regulation.
S0194 Where the loan is made in accordance with regulations under Section 22 of the Teaching and Higher Education Act 1998, they will be exempt from regulation under the Consumer Credit Act 1974 and the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 pursuant to section 8 of the Sale of Student Loans Act 2008.
S0195 According to Section 22, the loans must have interest rates falling below those prevailing on the market, or no higher than those prevailing on the market but with more favourable terms for the borrower.
S0196 The existing undergraduate student loans are structured to fall within this exemption.
S0197 The terms for this new loan will be designed to ensure it also adheres to this exemption.
S0198 This will also secure compliance with the United Kingdom’s obligations under the Consumer Credit Directive.
S0199 Non duplicative: The proposed scheme has been designed to avoid duplicative funding - two different streams of public money being used to fund the same outcome.
S0200 The Government wishes to develop a doctoral loan model that meets all these principles.
S0201 It is against that backdrop that we are asking for respondents’ views.
S0202 The consultation seeks to provide sufficient information, through this and other sign-posted
S0203 documents referenced within it, to allow respondents to provide considered and informed views.

S0208 •

S0210 •
S0211 The loan would be available to individuals studying any Level 8 doctoral qualification.
S0212 •
S0213 The loan would be available to eligible students who are not in receipt of a Research Council studentship (including fees only awards).
S0214 •
S0215 The loan will not be available until academic year 2018/19.
S0216 It is intended that the loan would be available to eligible UK nationals ordinarily resident in England and other students that meet the loan’s eligibility and residency requirements in place at the time.
S0217 Full details of eligibility will be published in advance of the Student Loans Company opening applications for academic year 2018/19.
S0218 • Eligible students ordinarily resident in England would be able to take their loan to any UK university offering a doctoral programme.
S0219 Higher Education provision is a devolved matter for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and therefore individuals ordinarily resident in those countries would not be eligible for the loan.
S0220 •
S0221 The proposed loan would be available to individuals aged 59 and under (on the first day of the first academic year of the programme of study).
S0222 •
S0223 Our intention is that the maximum programme of study length would be six years.
S0224 •
S0225 Payments would be in tranches across the number of years of the doctoral programme, up to a maximum over the training programme of £25,000.
S0226 •
S0227 The loan interest would be calculated at RPI+3% commencing when payment is issued to the student.
S0228 •

S0230 •

S0234 •
S0235 For those students with a master’s loan, the intention is for repayment to be taken through one single postgraduate loan repayment of 6% of income above
S0236 the repayment threshold, covering both master’s and doctoral loan balances.
S0237 The repayment rate for those with only a master’s loan would remain at 6%.
S0238 •
S0239 Any outstanding doctoral loan balance would be written off 30 years after the point a borrower becomes liable to begin repaying a loan.
S0240 •
S0241 Our current intention is that the first doctoral loans would be issued to students beginning programmes of study in academic year 2018/19. •
S0242 The loan would be administered by the Student Loans Company (SLC).

5.2.b Individual eligibility
S0255 The proposed loan would be available to individuals aged 59 and under (on the first day of the first academic year of the course).
S0256 The Equality Act 2010 prohibits direct discrimination on the grounds of age (section 13 (1) EA 2010).
S0257 However, a person (A) does not discriminate against another person (B) on the grounds of age if A can show that treating B less favourably than others is a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim (section 13(2) EA 2010).
S0258 The Government is aware that some doctoral students pursue their studies purely for personal interest and without any particular intention to return to the workplace, or in some cases, to complete the programme of study.
S0259 Whilst these doctorates may be valuable in themselves, it is not the purpose of the loan to facilitate their uptake.
S0260 This may be a particular issue amongst older cohorts, both those in retirement and those approaching retirement.
S0261 We believe that this risk may be exacerbated by a loan whose repayment is income contingent.
S0262 For those who are nearing the end of their working life, they may rightly calculate that they would not have to repay any of the loan, if they expect their income to remain under £21,000.
S0263 Whilst the Government acknowledges that doctoral research has value irrespective of the age of the student who produces it, it also has a duty to ensure value for money for the taxpayer when dispensing public money.
S0264 An age restriction may therefore be important in mitigating the risk that the loan is disproportionately taken out by older students, who might be incentivised by the loan to pursue a doctorate, and are unlikely to repay the loan in full.
S0265 Current numbers of doctoral students aged 60 and above are small; in 2014/15, there were 175 England domiciled entrants to UK HEIs and EU domiciled entrants to English HEIs aged 60 or older, studying towards doctorates.
S0266 Were large numbers of older students to take out the loan and to have a low level of repayment, this would increase the amount of the loan which the Government does not expect to be repaid, and would reduce value for money for the taxpayer.
S0267 A doctoral course usually takes a minimum of three years to complete, and the proposal is for the loan to be provided for courses up to six years.
S0268 This means that older students would have a limited number of working years in which their skills would be available to the economy.
S0269 As one of the objectives of the policy is to increase high level skills for the economy, there is a value for money argument to be made for targeting the loans to those earlier in their working life.
S0270 The Government is currently of the view that making the loan available only to those aged 59 and under would reduce the risk of the loans offering low value for money for the taxpayer.
S0271 This would mirror the age eligibility criteria of the master’s loan.

5.2.d Course length
S0297 The intention is for the maximum course length to be six years, with students stating in advance how long they intend to take to complete.
S0298 Full-time doctoral courses are typically three or four years in length, but we also recognise that there is value in allowing people to study part-time over a longer period.
S0299 A six year course length restriction strikes a balance between enabling part-time study, whilst incentivising students to complete their doctorates within a set period of time, so that they take their skills into the labour market more quickly.
S0300 Undergraduate and master’s courses link eligibility for loans to intensity of study.
S0301 However, we believe that this may be difficult to measure for doctoral students, where study is independent and not course-based.
S0302 Instead of limiting loans to part-time study of a certain level of intensity, we think it would be more appropriate to set an upper boundary on the length of time that students can study for, and still receive funding.
S0303 The Government also recognises that it can be difficult for students to predict at the outset of their course how long it will take to complete.
S0304 This poses a challenge, as the Government has a duty to manage public money appropriately, by ensuring that payments to students are made in step with, rather than in advance of, need.
S0305 Doctoral study involves the submission of a thesis, which a student is usually then examined on orally, often called a viva voce.
S0306 They may then be passed without corrections, asked to make corrections, or to resubmit their thesis, or may be given an alternative lower qualification.
S0307 HEIs usually specify a maximum time within which a student must submit their thesis, but the actual completion of a doctorate comes only once the thesis has been passed, and any necessary corrections made.

5.2.f Other sources of funding

S0330 The Government believes that the responsibility for doctoral finance should be shared between the Government, individuals, HEIs and others.

5.2.h Repayment terms
S0343 The proposed repayment terms are:
S0344 • £25,000 maximum loan amount; •
S0345 The loan interest would be calculated at RPI+3% commencing when payment is issued to the student.
S0346 •
S0347 Repayment of the doctoral loan would be income contingent and in line with the repayment threshold of the master’s loans, currently set at £21,000 until April 2021.
S0348 •
S0349 The intention is for doctoral loan repayments to be calculated at 6% of income above the income threshold.
S0350 This is pending a final policy decision which will be informed by this consultation, and made on the grounds of affordability, feasibility and value for money.
S0351 Repayments would be made concurrently alongside repayment of any outstanding undergraduate loan.
S0352 •
S0353 For those students with a master’s loan, the intention is for repayment to be taken through one single postgraduate loan repayment of 6% of income above the repayment threshold, covering both master’s and doctoral loan balances.
S0354 •
S0355 Any outstanding doctoral loan balance would be written off 30 years after the point a borrower becomes liable to begin repaying a loan.
S0356 The repayment terms have been modelled and chosen based on a number of key principles, including affordability and value for money.
S0357 Another key principle is that the loan must be income contingent.
S0358 This should ensure repayments are always achievable and affordable.
S0359 Unlike Professional and Career Development Loans, repayments would be adjusted accordingly.
S0360 For example, if an individual’s earnings decreased or they stop work, repayments would be reduced or stopped as appropriate.
