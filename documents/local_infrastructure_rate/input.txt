S0001 0.1 Foreword For too long, the UK has lagged behind other advanced economies on productivity.
S0002 High-value investment in infrastructure is one way of addressing this problem.
S0003 Roads and railways make it easier to travel and to transport goods across the country.
S0004 Telecoms help us communicate with each other.
S0005 Flood defences are vital for protecting homes, businesses and farmland.
S0006 Investing in infrastructure that raises productivity could drive long-term improvements in wages and living standards.
S0007 This is crucial if we are to build an economy that works for everyone.
S0008 It is for this reason that the government has put infrastructure at the heart of its economic strategy.
S0009 We have committed to raise annual central government investment in economic infrastructure from £14 billion in 2016-17 to £22 billion in 2020-21.
S0010 We recognise, however, that central government can only do so much.
S0011 We want to collaborate with partners in local government to support further investments in infrastructure.
S0012 In many places, local authorities are best placed to recognise the needs of local residents and to identify projects that will deliver the most benefits.
S0013 Local authorities have responsibilities for transport, housing and environmental infrastructure.
S0014 In total, they spent £23 billion on capital investment last year, including £8 billion on transport and £1 billion on environmental services.
S0015 The government wants to support local areas to meet their local infrastructure needs.
S0016 We are therefore proposing to offer £1 billion of discounted lending to local authorities, available at a new Local Infrastructure Rate of gilts + 60 basis points to support local infrastructure projects that are high value for money.
S0017 Qualifying authorities would be able to access the allocation from the Public Works Loan Board, and its successor, for a period of three years to support upfront investment.
S0018 This consultation seeks views on the proposed new rate, specifically on its potential impact for borrowers and lenders in the local government debt market.
S0019 We welcome any responses to the questions outlined in this paper, which will help inform and finalise the government’s plans.
S0020 Simon Kirby MP, Economic Secretary to the Treasury 0.2 Executive summary Infrastructure contributes directly to higher productivity, which in turn leads to long-term improvements in wages and living standards.
S0021 As part of its plans to close the productivity gap, the government has pledged to increase annual central government investment in economic infrastructure from £14 billion in 2016-17 to £22 billion in 2020-21.
S0022 A key part of the government’s plan is to support local authorities to invest in local infrastructure.
S0023 In many cases, local authorities may be best placed to understand local infrastructure needs.
S0024 The government wants to support local areas to meet these needs, where projects are high value for money.
S0025 At Autumn Statement 2016, the government announced that it would consult on lending local authorities up to £1 billion at a new Local Infrastructure Rate of gilts + 60 basis points to support infrastructure projects that are high value for money.
S0026 Loans at the new rate would be available for a period of three years, with a maximum term of 50 years.
S0027 This new lending rate would be accessed through the Public Works Loan Board ( PWLB ), a statutory body that lends to local authorities and other prescribed bodies from the National Loans Fund.
S0028 The Treasury has not introduced a new PWLB interest rate since 2013 and the government would like further input from stakeholders before proceeding with this policy.
S0029 This consultation seeks views on the effect of the Local Infrastructure Rate on infrastructure investment, as well as the impacts on local authorities and lenders to local government.
S0030 1.
S0031 Introduction Infrastructure is crucial for building a more productive economy and improving people’s lives.
S0032 Every day, businesses and families depend on roads, railways, telecoms, energy and flood defences.
S0033 Investing in infrastructure will also help to address the UK’s long-standing weakness in productivity, leading to higher wages and better living standards for workers.
S0034 It is for these reasons that the government has committed to raise annual central government investment in economic infrastructure from £14 billion in 2016-17 to £22 billion in 2020-21.
S0035 At Autumn Statement 2016, the government announced that it would consult on lending local authorities up to £1 billion at discounted rates to support infrastructure projects that are high value for money.
S0036 Under the government’s proposal, local authorities would be able to borrow up to £1 billion at a new Local Infrastructure Rate which would be set at gilts + 60 basis points.
S0037 Loans at the new rate would be available for a period of three years, with a maximum term of 50 years.
S0038 The total allocation would be capped at £1 billion, and would only be awarded to infrastructure projects that demonstrate high value for money.
S0039 This consultation document sets out some more details on the proposed rate and seeks views on its potential impact.
S0040 Views are invited from a range of respondents, including individuals, local authorities and representative bodies.
S0041 The government will take all responses into account before deciding whether to introduce this rate.

S0046 2.
S0047 Local Infrastructure Rate 2.1 Public Works Loan Board The Local Infrastructure Rate would be accessed through the Public Works Loan Board ( PWLB ) or its successor body.
S0048 The PWLB is an independent statutory body that issues loans to local authorities, and other specified bodies, from the National Loans Fund.
S0049 Nearly all borrowers from the PWLB are local authorities requiring loans for capital purposes.
S0050 Local authorities are able to borrow from any willing lender in the UK or abroad without requiring government consent, but only in sterling.
S0051 Around 75% of local authority borrowing is via the PWLB , with the rest mainly coming from banks, building societies, the European Investment Bank and the issue of bonds . ** (Reference: 1)
S0052 The policy on rates and repayment terms for PWLB loans is the responsibility of the Treasury.
S0053 Monies for PWLB loans are drawn from the National Loans Fund, which is the government’s main borrowing account and is administered by the Treasury.
S0054 The interest rates on loans provided by PWLB are directly linked to gilt yields.
S0055 The PWLB currently offers three interest rates: standard rate (gilts + 100 basis points), certainty rate (gilts + 80 basis points) and project rate (gilts + 60 basis points).
S0056 Where local areas have been given allocations of borrowing at the project rate, these have been confirmed with local areas.
S0057 The government does not currently plan to allocate any further tranches of discounted lending at the project rate.
S0058 As set out in the consultation response**(Link: 2) published earlier this year, the government is planning to use its powers under the Public Bodies Act 2011 to abolish the PWLB and transfer its powers to the Treasury.
S0059 This will only affect the PWLB ’s governance arrangements and does not change any of the policy or operational aspects of lending to local authorities.
S0060 The policy on rates and repayment terms will remain the responsibility of the Treasury.
S0061 2.2 Details on Local Infrastructure Rate More details on the precise requirements for the Local Infrastructure Rate would be set out in due course.
S0062 However, for the purpose of the consultation, it may be helpful for stakeholders to have some more detail on how the government would expect to implement this policy if it did go ahead.
S0063 Bids would be considered from principal authorities, the Greater London Authority and its functional bodies, Integrated Transport Authorities and combined authorities for projects in the transport, energy, flood defences, water, waste, and digital communications sectors.
S0064 As is currently the case, ultimate responsibility for projects, including financing, assessment and delivery, remains with council members, who are democratically accountable to their electorate.
S0065 In order to allocate amounts of discounted lending for projects, the government will need a clear and transparent process to assess bids.
S0066 The government’s current proposal is that councils would be required to produce analysis and an estimate of Net Present Value for the project, signed off by the Section 151 Officer, in line with Green Book appraisal guidance.
S0067 Full details would be set out in due course, but this could include an estimate of net present value, a high level business case, a borrowing profile and a risk assessment.
S0068 Councils would be required to submit their bids to the government, and the government would approve bids that provide evidence of high value for money.
S0069 Successful bidding authorities would be given an allocation of borrowing at the Local Infrastructure Rate.
S0070 This allocation would then be accessible via the PWLB until the body is abolished, after which loans would be made by its successor.
S0071 The Local Infrastructure Rate would only be available to finance the projects for which allocations were given.
S0072 It would not be available for other non-approved projects, or to refinance existing debts.
S0073 Advances at the new rate would be available for a period of three years, with a maximum term of 50 years.
S0074 As is standard for all PWLB rates, the Treasury retains the right to make unscheduled intra-daily rate changes, or alter the formula methodology or formula for variable rates, as necessary.
S0075 2.3 Rationale for change Local authorities are responsible for delivering a sizeable share of government infrastructure investment, including but not limited to local roads, public transport and flood defences.
S0076 Last year, local authorities spent £8 billion on transport and £1 billion on environmental services.
S0077 The government believes it is important to support local authorities in delivering infrastructure investment.
S0078 In many cases, local authorities may be best placed to understand local infrastructure needs, but high costs of borrowing could prevent them from delivering projects to meet these needs.
S0079 The new rate would make it easier for authorities to finance infrastructure projects that offer high value for money.
S0080 At Autumn Statement 2016, the government announced a £2.3 billion Housing Infrastructure Fund to be allocated to local authorities on a competitive basis.
S0081 This will provide infrastructure that unlocks new private house building in areas where housing need is greatest.
S0082 The Local Infrastructure Rate would complement this, giving local authorities more financing options for infrastructure projects that support the delivery of housing.
S0083 Alongside the Housing Infrastructure Fund, the government has also committed £1.8 billion to local enterprise partnerships ( LEPs ) in England through a third round of Growth Deals.
S0084 Given the increased availability of capital funding to local authorities, we want to understand local authorities’ appetite for discounted lending to support infrastructure investments.
S0085 The local government debt market is evolving, with new trends emerging and new lenders entering the market.
S0086 It is important for the government to fully understand the impact of a new PWLB interest rate before we consider introducing it.

S0104 Annex A: Impact assessment Type of impact Effect Economic impact The government expects local authorities to take up a significant proportion of the £1 billion allocation if the new rate is introduced.
S0105 This would raise investment in infrastructure and increase the productive capacity of the UK economy . ** (Reference: 2)
S0106 Impact on business including civil society organisations Increased infrastructure investment would make it easier and cheaper to do business.
S0107 An increase in lending by central government, albeit limited to infrastructure only, may reduce demand for private sector finance.
S0108 Financial impact This policy is likely to lead some local authorities to increase expenditure financed by borrowing, or reprioritise capital funding away from lower priority areas.
S0109 Impact on individuals, households and families Additional infrastructure directly contributes to living standards.
S0110 For example, investment in local roads would reduce travel times and ease congestion.
S0111 The indirect effect of infrastructure investment is higher wages, which would benefit working families.
S0112 Operational impact We do not expect the PWLB to incur any additional costs in administering the new rate.
