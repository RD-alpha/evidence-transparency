Executive summary
S0037 1.
S0038 This consultation paper sets out proposals for the reform of the Advocates’ Graduated Fee Scheme (AGFS).
S0039 This scheme pays advocates to defend clients in the Crown Court.
S0040 The current AGFS scheme was last subject to major change in 2007 and the Government considers it needs to be modernised.
S0041 2.
S0042 The Government considers the current arrangements are in need of reform.
S0043 The AGFS relies too heavily on Pages of Prosecution Evidence (PPE), served by the Crown Prosecution Service, as a means of deciding how complex individual cases are - and therefore how much a defence advocate should be paid.
S0044 The current scheme also relies on the number of witnesses to help determine the fee to be paid.
S0045 3.
S0046 Our justice system is changing, and new forms of evidence are becoming critical features of many criminal cases.
S0047 The counting of pages, and counting of new forms of electronic evidence, converted to “pages” is no longer the most effective way of assessing how much work an advocate needs to do in an individual case, and therefore how much that advocate should be paid.
S0048 4.
S0049 Our proposed scheme reduces reliance on counting pages, and instead would introduce a more sophisticated system of classifying offences - based on the typical amount of work required in each case.
S0050 The time spent in court, conducting the advocacy upon which our justice system relies, would also become a more important driver for the fee paid.
S0051 5.
S0052 The proposed scheme is designed to be cost neutral - there is no intention to reduce or increase the overall cost envelope.
S0053 The proposed scheme would redistribute money, better reflecting work done, and modernising the system in concert with the Better Case Management (BCM) reforms that are transforming the way that our criminal courts do business.
S0054 6.
S0055 The proposed scheme is set out in detail at Annex 1.
S0056 The consultation sets out how fees in the proposed scheme would be calculated, in comparison with the current scheme - and summarises the current and proposed schemes (Section 3).
S0057 It then sets out the contents of the graduated fee bundle, again comparing current and proposed schemes for ease of reference (Section 4).
S0058 The proposed scheme “un- bundles” several elements of the graduated fee, meaning that advocates would be paid set amounts for individual elements of a case (for example, separate fees for up to six standard appearances).
S0059 This would increase certainty for all advocates about incomes, especially when multiple advocates act in the same case.
S0060 7.
S0061 The proposed system of categorising offences is explained in detail (Section 5).
S0062 This section also deals with the “relative” fees to be paid between types of advocate (QC, leading junior, led junior/junior alone) and refresher fees.
S0063 It also explains why, in drugs and fraud cases, PPE is still used to help categorise how serious the case is in certain circumstances.
S0064 8.
S0065 Proposals for the definition of guilty plea fees and cracked trial fees are then set out.
S0066 We propose to ensure that cases that crack late are remunerated fairly, to reflect work done to prepare the case for trial (Section 6).
S0067 In the proposed scheme a cracked trial fee would only be paid once the defence had filed a certificate of trial readiness.
S0068 This is the point at which they are certifying, before the court, that their case is ready to proceed to trial - so the vast majority of the case preparation should have been carried out by this point.
S0069 It is fair that the fee scheme reflects this.
S0070 9.
S0071 The final sections of the paper deal with special preparation and wasted preparation (Section 7), other matters where there are significant changes from the old scheme to the proposed scheme (Section 8) and equalities (Section 9).
Introduction
S0072 Background 1. This paper sets out proposals for the reform of the Advocates’ Graduated Fee Scheme (AGFS). ** (Reference: 1)
S0073 This is the scheme through which criminal defence advocates are paid for carrying out publicly funded work in the Crown Court.
S0074 1.2
S0075 The current scheme has been in place for around twenty years, and was last subject to significant reform in 2007, following recommendations made by Lord Carter.
S0076 1.3 In 2015, the Bar Council published proposals for a revised AGFS scheme. A joint working group consisting of representatives from across the legal profession has been jointly developing a proposal for reform over the last few months. ** (Reference: 1)
S0077 The views expressed by the working group have been carefully considered.
S0078 The Government has set out its own vision for reform in this consultation paper.
S0079 The case for intervention 1. ** (Reference: 4)
S0080 The Government considers that the AGFS requires reform for a number of reasons.
S0081 Since the scheme was last reformed, there have been considerable changes to the way criminal cases are run, and the way that evidence in them is served.
S0082 Modernising reforms such as the Crown Court Digital Case System, and an increase in the use of electronic evidence, are not effectively provided for in the current scheme.
S0083 As a result of the changing environment, the Government considers that the volume of evidence is no longer necessarily reflective of the amount of work an advocate has to undertake on an individual case.
S0084 1.5 The Government also considers that the existing arrangements are unnecessarily complicated, for advocates and administrators alike.
S0085 We understand that it can often be unclear to an advocate what their fee will be at the point of taking on a case.
S0086 The current scheme can be inflexible, especially when new offences are established.
S0087 A large miscellaneous class of offences has undermined confidence in the fairness of the current scheme.
S0088 As a result, we are proposing a simpler, clearer scheme for all Crown Court advocates.
S0089 In the proposed scheme fees would be more transparent at the outset.
S0090 The scheme would also be fully compatible with our evolving Criminal Justice System.
S0091 1.6 Better Case Management (BCM) reforms, being put in place as a result of Sir Brian Leveson’s Review of Efficiency in Criminal Proceedings are also transforming the way our criminal courts operate. ** (Reference: 2)
S0092 This should be addressed within a new, modern fee scheme, designed to complement the BCM agenda.
S0093 1.7 The working group referred to above agreed a set of guiding principles to apply to the redesign of the scheme.
S0094 As far as possible the new scheme should:
S0095 • be cost neutral (using 2014-15 as the baseline);
S0096 • minimise reliance on pages of prosecution evidence served (PPE);
S0097 • reflect, and pay for, the actual work done;
S0098 • support getting the right outcome in individual cases, and remove as far as possible any perverse incentives;
S0099 • be consistent with and, where appropriate, support wider reforms - for example, the Better Case Management programme and wider Criminal Justice System reforms; and • place no extra administrative burden on Her Majesty’s Courts and Tribunals Service (HMCTS), the Legal Aid Agency (LAA), and practitioners than the current scheme – and ideally a reduced burden.
S0100 1.8 The working group, and the Government, openly acknowledges that there are tensions between some of these principles; the proposal presented aims to find the right balance between them.
S0101 1.9 One of the additional aspirations articulated by the Bar Council in their original proposal was sustainability.
S0102 In their assessment, career progression is limited for advocates under the current scheme.
S0103 The current scheme and proposed reforms 1. The existing AGFS uses a series of proxies to categorise the likely complexity of the case, and the fee that should therefore be paid. ** (Reference: 10)
S0104 These proxies include the number of witnesses and amount of evidence.
S0105 One of the most important proxies is Pages of Prosecution Evidence (PPE) served.
S0106 Over the last few years there have been significant changes in the way that evidence is served.
S0107 Electronic evidence, including video footage, and mobile phone and hard-drive data, is increasingly served.
S0108 The current payment arrangements, whereby some of this material is “converted” into pages, no longer fully reflects the work required of advocates in the Crown Court.
S0109 1.11 The proposed scheme reflects a different approach.
S0110 It dispenses with witnesses as a proxy for complexity, and radically reduces the role of PPE.
S0111 Instead payment is graduated based on a more detailed and sophisticated breakdown of the offence that the defendant is charged with.
S0112 Currently there are eleven offence categories.
S0113 The proposed scheme features sixteen offence categories, which encompass a total of forty-two separate bands.
S0114 The category and band, designed to reflect the average amount of work required in a typical case, would become a critical factor in determining what the advocate is paid.
S0115 1.12 In tandem, the amount of time spent by an advocate performing their duties, would become an increasingly important variable in determining the fee paid.
S0116 We consider that it is right that “work done” is accounted for as fully as possible in the proposed scheme.
S0117 At its core, that “work done” is the advocacy conducted in the Crown Court.
S0118 Through the detailed revised design (see Sections 3-8 and Appendix one) the proposed scheme places more weight on this.
S0119 1.13 We recognise that the roles of litigators and advocates are very different, especially in relation to the consideration and preparation of evidence.
S0120 We consider that the balance in the AGFS should be altered for advocates, with a greater emphasis on advocacy “work done”.
S0121 1.14
S0122 The proposed AGFS scheme is designed to account for the reality of the current Crown Court process, and to be better suited to likely changes in the future.
S0123 For example, the nature and quantity of evidence served, and the boundary between used and unused evidence is likely to evolve continually over the next few years.
S0124 A fee scheme built on a more detailed split of offences, and time spent conducting advocacy, is likely to be far more durable.
S0125 1.15 There is also no intention in reforming the scheme to either increase or decrease the overall cost envelope for the AGFS. One of the principles that the members of the working group agreed was cost neutrality. ** (Reference: 3)
S0126 This is a scheme that should cost the same overall, but provide more certainty for advocates about their fees.
S0127 1.16 In light of the above, this consultation paper sets out the Government’s proposed scheme.

S0303 Graduated fee bundle 4. ** (Reference: 1)
S0304 The existing graduated fee includes payment for attendance at the Plea and Trial Preparation Hearing (PTPH), attendance at the first two days of trial, attendance at four other standard appearances, attendance at three conferences and views, fifty pages of prosecution evidence, and up to ten witnesses.
S0305 4.2
S0306 This fee is paid in all cases that qualify, including those featuring less evidence and fewer trial days, standard appearances, conference and views, and witnesses than the bundle accounts for.
S0307 Depending on the nature of the case, the advocate will, quite legitimately, undertake more work in some cases, and less work in others, yet be paid the same fee.
S0308 4.3 Additional payments are made for trial days in excess of two, for other standard appearances in excess of four (i.e. the PTPH and four other standard hearings), for in excess of three conferences and views, PPE in excess of page fifty, and for each witness in excess of the first ten. 4. ** (Reference: 4, 7)
S0309 The proposed scheme would adopt a very different approach.
S0310 Firstly, the evidence uplift has been removed: PPE no longer forms part of the basic fee calculation.
S0311 Retaining a fee system heavily predicated on PPE would not properly account for the reality of contemporary Crown Court advocacy.
S0312 Elements of the scheme still involve PPE, for reasons explained in section 5, but its role is greatly diminished.
S0313 4.5
S0314 Secondly, the witness uplift has been removed.
S0315 Separate fees would no longer be paid for cases featuring more than ten witnesses.
S0316 The number of witnesses the prosecution produce to aid their case is not necessarily the best indicator of a case’s complexity (as there may be a case with numerous witnesses that is relatively straightforward; conversely a case could have one or two witnesses who provide complex evidence that needs significant analysis).
S0317 The existing position also means that there is considerable cost uncertainty for both the individual advocate and the LAA.
S0318 We consider that a more sophisticated categorisation of offences is a better way of capturing complexity, increasing cost predictability and therefore setting payment.
S0319 4.6 Thirdly, fewer discrete elements are bundled into the basic fee. ** (Reference: 8)
S0320 The current and proposed bundle is compared below.
S0321 2 x days of trial advocacy 1 x day of trial advocacy 3 x conferences and views 3 x conferences and views 4. We consider that our proposal would create a much more transparent system. ** (Reference: 7)
S0322 We also consider that it meets the principle of paying for work done.
S0323 By splitting out elements of the graduated fee, there would be fewer cases where we are paying on the basis of an average number of hearings.
S0324 The first six standard hearings would be paid separately, so advocates would be paid additionally for these elements of a case.
S0325 4.8
S0326 Having a smaller bundled fee is also consistent with the Better Case Management Reforms.
S0327 As unnecessary hearings reduce over time, a fee scheme predicated on an average number of hearings per case would not fully reflect work done.
S0328 Splitting out the additional hearings would mean that the proposed AGFS scheme complements the BCM reforms.
S0329 The scheme would ensure separate payment for individual hearings and would be future-proofed, with payment becoming more reflective of work done.
S0330 4.9
S0331 In addition, paying individual fees provides increased cost certainty for advocates, especially when multiple advocates have conducted different elements of the same case, or the case has been picked up as a late return.
S0332 Currently the Bar operate a fee sharing protocol, which apportions the bundled fee in cases with multiple hearings and multiple advocates. ** (Reference: 10)
S0333 Although this protocol is owned by the Bar, and the Government has no role in this, some fees are “depleted” in certain cases when apportioned between the trial advocate and substitute advocates.
S0334 By splitting out more discrete elements from the bundle, all advocates would have increased certainty as to the exact fee they would end up receiving in the vast majority of cases.
S0335 4.10
S0336 Our proposals aim to protect junior advocates, by paying them separate fees for standard hearings in the vast majority of cases.
S0337 Around 96% of cases feature six or fewer standard hearings (one of which is likely to be the PTPH), and in all of these cases, hearings will be remunerated separately. ** (Reference: 11)
S0338 In addition to the PTPH attracting a separate payment, sentencing hearings would also be remunerated separately (unless they occurred on the same day that the trial concludes).

S0362 As a result forty-two different base graduated fees, dependent on the nature and seriousness of the offence, would feature in the proposed scheme.

S0368 These would be the more straightforward cases spanning a range of different, less complex offences, when compared to other offences in the Crown Court.

S0371 5.5
S0372 The relative complexity of different cases carried out by advocates, as suggested by the Bar Council, is one of the foundations of the proposed scheme.
S0373 The “simplest” case was assigned a suggested fee (i.e. the standard case category referred to above) and the most “complex” case (i.e. a difficult murder case) was assigned a suggested fee.
S0374 The most complex case was given a score of 100; the simplest case a score of 5. 5. ** (Reference: 6)
S0375 The relative complexity of all the new categories and bandings was then considered.
S0376 Each was given a score (with no case capable of scoring less than five, or more than one hundred) to indicate its relative complexity and seriousness.
S0377 In this way the relativities between cases were set.
S0378 5.7
S0379 Fees were then applied on the same proportionate basis - i.e. the most complex case in the scheme was originally proposed to attract a fee twenty times higher than the simplest case (assuming they were conducted by the same level of advocate).
S0380 Subsequently, having spoken to experts in the field, there have been adjustments to some fees, but most fees correspond closely to the original relativities derived.
S0381 The most complex cases in the scheme (e.g. a Band 1.1 murder) are proposed to be remunerated at around fifteen times the amount of the simplest case (i.e. a standard case).
S0382 The indicative fee table and equivalent chart is set out in Annex Two.
S0383 5.8 As well as relativities between offences, the scheme also builds in relativities between types of advocate: QCs would be paid double the rate of juniors, and leading juniors one and half times the junior rate.
S0384 5.9
S0385 In addition, the daily refresher rates do not feature exactly the same relativities as the corresponding brief fees for the same case.
S0386 The refresher remunerates the advocate for time spent in court, and time spent preparing for subsequent days once the trial has commenced.
S0387 The Bar have suggested slightly different relativities accordingly; these underpin the refresher rates that we are consulting on - as set out in full with an equivalent chart in Annex 2. 5. Most of the categories and bandings in the scheme are based on groupings of offences. ** (Reference: 10)
S0388 These are sometimes qualified in relation to other relevant factors, where there is a broad spectrum of severity or complexity within the same statutory offence (or group of offences).
S0389 One example of this is in drugs cases, where the weights and quantities of drugs are often critical factors in the complexity of the case.
S0390 The unique factors inherent in drugs cases, and large volumes, also explain why this category features an unusually high number of bandings (seven).
S0391 5.11
S0392 Whilst the scheme overall greatly reduces the role of PPE as a proxy for complexity, in two proposed offence categories we propose to retain page counts as one factor for differentiating seriousness within those categories.
S0393 PPE limits feature in the bandings for drugs and dishonesty cases.
S0394 In drugs cases, we consider a page count needs to be retained, because drugs may never physically be recovered in a conspiracy case.
S0395 So a category that relied exclusively on the weight or quantity of drugs recovered may not properly reflect complexity in those circumstances.
S0396 In dishonesty cases, there will be certain circumstances where the value of the loss or intended loss will not always be clear at the outset - so page counts have been retained as a proxy of complexity.

S0426 Early guilty pleas and cracked trials 6. ** (Reference: 1)
S0427 In the current scheme, the period between the date of the PTPH and the date that the first day of the trial is listed for is used to decide whether a guilty plea fee or cracked trial fee is paid.
S0428 Cases that plea in the first third of this period receive a guilty plea fee; cases that plea in the second two thirds of this period receive a cracked trial fee.
S0429 6.2
S0430 We propose to alter the definitions of an early guilty plea and a cracked trial fee so we can better meet the principle of paying for work done.
S0431 In reality, the Bar and experts in the field have suggested that the trigger point in the existing AGFS, for a guilty plea becoming a crack, is too early.
S0432 A significant amount of case preparation will often take place in the immediate run up to the trial, and it is this work that we consider should be targeted.
S0433 6.3 Currently over 90% of cases crack in the final third. The exact length of the final third will vary depending on the length of the case. ** (Reference: 13)
S0434 We think a better dividing point between the payment of a guilty plea fee and a crack trial fee is the point at which the defence files a certificate of trial readiness.
S0435 This should be served no later than 28 days before the date of the trial.
S0436 By filing this form the defence are certifying, to the judge, that their case is trial ready.
S0437 The work undertaken to prepare the case at this point should be similar to that conducted on a case which ends as an effective trial.
S0438 As such, we think the point of this certificate being filed, or the point at which it is directed to be filed, would represent a more appropriate point after which to determine whether a trial has ‘cracked’ than the current ‘thirds’ system.
S0439 This would also helpfully align with the stages in Better Case Management.
S0440 6.4
S0441 We are proposing to pay a crack fee for these cases which fairly reflects work done, but is slightly lower than the trial fee - which includes payment for the first day’s advocacy at the trial.
S0442 Given this we are proposing to set the crack fee at 85% of the relative cost of a full trial in the same offence category and band, and the same level of advocate.
S0443 Indicative fees for cracked trials, by offence category and band, and advocate type, are set out in Annex two.
S0444 6.5 Guilty pleas fee would be paid for cases that plea prior to the certificate of trial readiness being filed by the defence.
S0445 Guilty plea fees would have a relative value of around 50% compared to the full trial fee in the same offence category and band, and the same level of advocate.
S0446 This demonstrates a clear and consistent equalisation of guilty plea rates across the scheme, and would result in a more uniform system.
S0447 We consider that this strikes the right balance between supporting Better Case Management and fairly reflecting the “work done” relative to trials and late cracks.
S0448 Indicative fees for guilty pleas, by offence category and band, and advocate type, are set out in Annex two.

S0460 Cases that are currently eligible for special preparation would not require it under our proposals.
S0461 7.3
S0462 In the proposed scheme, we consider that special preparation should be reserved for outlying cases, where even the broader spectrum of complexity captured under the proposed offence categories is insufficient to remunerate a case requiring a genuinely exceptional amount of preparation.
S0463 We consider that an exceptional amount of preparation might be required in cases dealing with novel points of law or fact; or cases where an exceptionally large amount of evidence is served.

S0495 Sentencing hearings that occur on the same day as a plea hearing or trial would not be remunerated separately.

S0498 We propose that for the purposes of the new fee scheme, the first day of pre-trial cross-examination should continue to be treated as the first day of the trial.

S0618 A guilty plea payment would be made where there is a guilty plea prior to a defence certificate of trial readiness being filed.
S0619 Cracked Trials 9.
S0620 A cracked trial is one where the defendant alters their plea to guilty before the trial has commenced.
S0621 A cracked trial will be paid at a fixed rate depending on the category and band of the case and the type of advocate.
S0622 This rate will be set at 85% of the basic trial fee for that category and band of case and type of advocate.
S0623 10.
S0624 A cracked trial fee would be paid if a trial cracks after the defence has filed a certificate of trial readiness but before the trial has commenced.
S0625 Special Preparation 11.
S0626 Cases would be considered for a special preparation payment if they involved novel points of law or fact; or featured 10,000 PPE (with the exception of drugs, where 15,000 PPE would be required, and dishonesty, where 30,000 PPE would be required).
S0627 Wasted Preparation 12.
S0628 Wasted preparation provisions are proposed to remain unchanged from the existing scheme.
S0629 Other elements of the scheme (for indicative fees, where appropriate, see Annex 2)
S0630 13.
S0631 Additional defendant uplift - we propose to pay an uplift of 50% of the appropriate trial fee for each additional defendant represented by the same advocate in a case.
S0632 14.
S0633 Conference and Views - we propose that conferences with clients and views are paid a fixed fee per hour dependant on the type of advocate.
S0634 15.
S0635 Travel and subsistence - we propose that this would be paid as under the current scheme - i.e. travel expenses would be paid, provided that the advocate can satisfy the appropriate officer that they were reasonably incurred.
S0636 16.
S0637 Plea and Trial Preparation Hearing - this would be remunerated separately to the Basic Fee.
S0638 Should a guilty plea be entered at PTPH, a guilty plea fee would be payable instead of the PTPH fee.
S0639 17.
S0640 Elected cases not proceeded - these are cases which can be heard in the Magistrates Court, but where the defendant has elected to have the case heard at the Crown Court.
S0641 These cases would be remunerated at a flat fixed fee, regardless of advocate type.
S0642 18.
S0643 Standard Appearances - as detailed above, under the proposed scheme the first six standard appearances would be paid for separately.
S0644 Subsequent Standard Appearances are remunerated under the Basic Fee. 19.
S0645 Breach of Crown Court order - these would be paid a fixed fee per day, depending on the offence and advocate type.
S0646 20.
S0647 Sentencing Hearings - these would be remunerated separately under the new scheme, per day of attendance (unless they occur on the same day that the trial concludes, a plea is entered, or the trial cracks).
S0648 21.
S0649 Section 28 Proceedings - these have been introduced in sexual offences casesby the Youth Justice and Criminal Evidence Act 1999 so that the cross examination of key witnesses can occur at the beginning of proceedings.
S0650 In these cases, the first day of pre-trial cross examination by video would be considered to be the first day of trial (as per the current scheme).
S0651 22.
S0652 Hospital Orders - in any case in which a hospital order is made the sentence hearing will be remunerated at the rate of the refresher for the category of case.
S0653 23.
S0654 Hearings relating to admissibility of evidence - these would be paid a fixed fee dependent on the length of hearing and type of advocate, with a half day and full day refresher possible.
S0655 24.
S0656 Hearings relating to disclosure - these would be paid a fixed fee dependant on the length of the hearing and the type of advocate.
S0657 25.
S0658 Appeals - appeals against sentence or conviction would be paid as a fixed fee per day dependant on the type of advocate conducting the case.
S0659 26.
S0660 Appeals lasting more than one day - Magistrate appeals which last more than one day would be paid as a Standard Basic Fee (i.e. Band 16.1).
S0661 27.
S0662 Noting Brief - these would be remunerated at a fixed fee regardless of advocate type.
S0663 28.
S0664 Application to dismiss charge - these would be remunerated at a fixed fee equal to a refresher for that category of offence and advocate type.
S0665 29.
S0666 Abuse of Process Hearing - these would be paid a fixed fee dependent on the length of hearing and type of advocate.
S0667 A half or full day hearing fee is possible.
S0668 30.
S0669 Public Interest Immunity Hearing - these hearings would be paid a fixed fee dependent on the length of hearing and type of advocate.
S0670 A half or full day hearing fee is possible.
S0671 31.
S0672 Contempt proceedings - these would be remunerated at a fixed fee for each day of the hearing according to the category and advocate type.
S0673 32.
S0674 Interaction with Very High Cost Cases - we propose that the final decision about eligibility for the VHCC scheme would, as at present, rest with the Legal Aid Agency.
S0675 33.
S0676 Returned briefs - as under the existing scheme, we propose that there would not be an additional payment for advocates who accept a ‘returned brief’ part way through proceedings.
S0677 34.
S0678 Defendant as an absconder before trial – we propose to harmonise the timing of payment between litigators and advocates for these instances.
S0679 Under these proposals, advocates would be able to claim the appropriate fee after three months have elapsed from the point that the defendant absconds.
S0680 If a trial takes place in the period between three and fifteen months from the point the bench warrant is issued, a trail fee would be paid, minus the fee already claimed.
S0681 If a trial takes place in excess of fifteen months from the point that the defendant originally absconded, a trial fee would be paid (and any fees already paid would not be deducted).
S0682 35.
S0683 Any individual fee not specified to change in this consultation document, or the annexes thereof, can be assumed to be proposed to be remaining the same as under the existing provisions.

S0821 What is the problem under consideration?
S0822 Why is government intervention necessary?
S0823 The Advocates’ Graduated Fee Scheme (AGFS) is used to remunerate criminal defence advocates in the Crown Court.
S0824 The current scheme was last subject to major change in 2007, and the government considers it is in need of reform.
S0825 The AGFS uses proxies for complexity to determine the appropriate fee, and places a heavy reliance on factors that are increasingly unsuitable in terms of advocacy.
S0826 The advent of court modernisation reforms such as the Crown Court Digital Case System are changing our justice system for the better and we need to ensure the way we remunerate advocates supports this.
S0827 What are the policy objectives and the intended effects?
S0828 We have had some guiding principles when designing these reforms, detailed in the consultation document.
S0829 These include, amongst other things, cost-neutrality against 2014-15 case mix and volumes, ensuring the scheme pays for work done, and ensuring the reforms are consistent with wider reforms in the Criminal Justice System.
S0830 We acknowledge that some of the principles are in conflict with each other, and have attempted to find the right balance between them.
S0831 The intended effect is to deliver a scheme which delivers more appropriate remuneration for advocates, reflecting work done, whilst retaining cost neutrality.

S0833 The following options have been considered:

Evidence Base A. Background
S0886 1.
S0887 The Advocates’ Graduated Fee Scheme (AGFS) is used to remunerate criminal defence advocates in the Crown Court.
S0888 The current scheme has been in place for around twenty years, and was last subject to significant reform in 2007, following recommendations made by Lord Carter.
S0889 2.
S0890 The current scheme determines fees through a complex formula that is reliant on the advocate type, the offence, the length of trial, the number of pages of prosecution evidence and the number of prosecution witnesses amongst others.
S0891 This is supplemented through additional discrete fees for other work.
S0892 It also “bundles” certain hearings into the graduated brief fee, rather than paying for them individually.
S0893 The bundled fee includes 2 days of trial and 5 standard appearances.
S0894 Daily attendance fees are paid at a reduced rate between 40 and 60 days.
S0895 There is heavy reliance on pages of prosecution evidence (PPE), which represents a significant administrative burden on advocates and administrators alike.
S0896 This is becoming increasingly burdensome, given the movement towards more electronic evidence (with reforms such as the Crown Court Digital Case System), and will only become more so in the future.
S0897 Under the current scheme the client may make a contribution to their defence costs.
S0898 The amount that is contributed is based on the case cost and disposable income.
S0899 3.
S0900 The government considers that the AGFS requires reform for a number of reasons.
S0901 Since the scheme was last reformed, there have been considerable changes to the way criminal cases are run, and the way that evidence in them is served.
S0902 Due to modernising reforms such as the Crown Court Digital Case System, there has been an increase in the use of electronic evidence.
S0903 The current payment arrangements, whereby some of this material is “converted” into pages from electronic formats, no longer fully reflects the work required of advocates in the Crown Court.
S0904 4.
S0905 The government also considers that the existing arrangements are unnecessarily complex and complicated for advocates and administrators alike.
S0906 We understand that it can often be unclear to an advocate what their fee will be at the point of taking on a case, and the current arrangements compound the issue.
S0907 Indeed, the current scheme can be inflexible and rigid, especially in instances when new offences are established, and they have to be incorporated in the scheme.
S0908 A large miscellaneous class of offences undermines confidence in the accuracy of payments for new offence types and may not accurately reflect the actual work done by an advocate.
S0909 As such, we are proposing to implement a simpler, clearer scheme for all advocates, in which a fee can be easily calculated at the outset, and which will allow for flexible adaptation to a modern Criminal Justice System.
S0910 5.
S0911 Better Case Management (BCM) Reforms, being put in place as a result of Sir Brian Leveson’s Review of Efficiency in Criminal Proceedings, are also transforming the way our criminal courts operate.
S0912 The volume of overall Crown Court cases, and the distribution of cases between offences, have all changed since the scheme was last refreshed.
S0913 These factors can and should be addressed within a new, modern fee scheme, designed to complement the BCM agenda.
B. Policy Rationale and Objectives
S0914 6.
S0915 In 2015, the Bar Council published proposals for a revised AGFS.
S0916 An AGFS working group consisting of representatives from the professions has been jointly developing a proposal for reform over the last few months.
S0917 The results of this collaborative working are presented in full in the consultation paper.
S0918 7.
S0919 The proposed reforms should deliver a clearer, less complex fee scheme for advocates, and the department alike.
S0920 Remuneration under the scheme should more accurately reflect the work done.
S0921 The scheme should support and promote wider reforms and support a modernised Criminal Justice System.
S0922 The proposed scheme should retain the principle of cost-neutrality against 2014-15 case mix and fund spend.
S0923 8.
S0924 To address these issues and in developing our proposals for AGFS reform, we have focussed on the following guiding policy objectives.
S0925 These are for the proposed reforms to:
S0926  be cost neutral based against 2014-15 case mix and volumes;
S0927  minimise reliance on pages of prosecution evidence served (PPE);
S0928  reflect, and pay for, the actual work done;
S0929  support getting the right outcome in individual cases, and remove as far as possible any perverse incentives;
S0930  be consistent with and, where appropriate, support wider reforms - for example, the BCM programme and wider Criminal Justice System reforms; and  place no extra administrative burden on Her Majesty’s Courts and Tribunals Service (HMCTS), the Legal Aid Agency (LAA), and practitioners than the current scheme – and, ideally, lead to a reduced burden.
S0931 9.
S0932 The AGFS working group, and the government, openly acknowledges that there are tensions between some of these principles; the proposal presented aims to find the right balance between them.

D. Description of Options Considered
S0937 11.
S0938 To meet the above policy objectives, the following two options are considered in this IA:
S0939  Option 0 / ‘Do nothing’:
S0940 Retain existing arrangements for the AGFS  Option 1: Reform the AGFS Option 0 / ‘Do nothing’: Retain existing arrangements for the AGFS 12.
S0941 The current provisions for the AGFS are established in regulations.
S0942 Should the ‘do nothing’ option be pursued, the existing arrangements will remain in place, and the system would operate as it does at present.
S0943 However, it would fail to address all of the policy objectives outlined above.
S0944 Option 1: Reform the AGFS 13.
S0945 Under this option the AGFS would be fundamentally reformed to address some of its existing shortcomings and ensure it is coherent with a modernised criminal justice system.
S0946 14.
S0947 The total fee paid to advocates would continue to be made up of two components: the graduated fee, and any additional fixed fees.
S0948 However, we propose that the graduated fee would be made up of only two key elements: the basic fee (based on offence category and banding, and category of advocate), and a daily attendance fee for each trial day after the first day (determined by case category and banding, and category of advocate).
S0949 15.
S0950 The ‘basic’ fee would be dependent on the classification of the offence, determined by its nature and severity, and the advocate type.
S0951 This fee would also include a “bundled” payment for attendance at day 1 of a trial; and standard appearances in excess of the first six standard appearances.
S0952 We propose to remove the uplifts for evidence and witnesses from the graduated fee.
S0953 16.
S0954 The daily attendance fee is dependent on the classification of the offence; the category of the advocate; and the number of trial days upon which the advocate attends at court (there would no longer be a reduction in the rate after 40 days).
S0955 17.
S0956 We propose to pay fixed fees for other individual appearances and standard appearances (up to the first six).
S0957 The level of the individual fees would continue to be dependent on the length of the appearance, the nature of the appearance, and the category of advocate.
S0958 18.
S0959 Payments for guilty pleas and cracked trials would be dependent on the classification of the offence, the category of the advocate and when a guilty plea is entered.
S0960 Cracked trials under the proposed scheme are defined as a case where the defendant pleads guilty, or the case collapses for another reason, following the defence’s completion (and courts’ acceptance) of the certificate of trial readiness but before the start of the trial.
S0961 Any case in which the defendant pleads guilty, or the case collapses for another reason, before this stage of trial readiness is paid as a ‘guilty plea’.
S0962 The brief fee for a guilty plea would be 50% of a trial fee, and a cracked trial fee 85% of a trial fee.
S0963 We are also proposing to maintain the facility for other payments under the scheme dependent on the nature of the case (i.e. an additional uplift for additional defendants/indictments, travel expenses etc.).
S0964 19.
S0965 Payments would differ depending on advocate type.
S0966 A QC would receive double a junior alone fee, and a leading junior would receive 50% more than a junior alone.
S0967 Led juniors would be paid as junior alones.
S0968 20.
S0969 Option 1 is the preferred option as it best meets the policy objectives outlined above.

F. Assumptions and Risks
S1068 44.
S1069 The key assumptions and risks are highlighted below.
S1070 More detail on the risks are explored in the sensitivity analysis.
S1071 Assumptions Risks 2014-15 data It has been assumed that the volumes and case mix in 2014-15 will continue.
S1072 No changes to any inputs have been made in the main analysis (i.e. each case with the exact trial length, offence, advocate type has been modelled under the proposed scheme).
S1073 In the aggregate, a change in volumes (holding case mix constant) would still result in a cost neutral scheme, i.e. if all offences increased in volume by 1%, the proposed scheme would still be cost neutral.
S1074 However, it is more likely that the case mix profile of offences would increase or decrease in future, which would affect the overall expenditure.
S1075 The fee used for comparison is the actual fee paid if the case was paid under the current scheme (scheme 9) of the AGFS, or is the modelled fee if on an earlier scheme.
S1076 In 2014-15 there were fewer than 1,500 cases paid on a scheme other than 9 and this falls in subsequent years.
S1077 Fee scheme incentives It is implicitly assumed, when using 2014-15 data, that there are no new behavioural impacts arising from these proposals and the fee scheme incentives would remain the same.
S1078 The proposed fee scheme could change incentives which would affect the overall cost to the LAA and HMCTS operational costs.
S1079 The extent of the change would depend on the extent that the advocate is able to influence the following factors.
S1080 For instance:
S1081 - unbundling the trial days and hearings could result in an increase in trials lasting 2 days or more than 40 days, and an increase in the number of hearings.
S1082 This could increase the cost of the scheme.
S1083 - including payment for standard appearances after the 6th appearance in the brief fee could reduce the number of cases with more than 6 hearings.
S1084 This could reduce HMCTS operational costs.
S1085 LAA offence categorisation The Crown Court Remuneration (CCR) offence description has been used to categorise cases between the schemes.
S1086 This is assumed to be a true reflection of the offences listed on the indictment and claimed under the current scheme.
S1087 The CCR may not contain sufficient information to accurately categorise cases to the proposed scheme.
S1088 This could lead to higher or lower costs than estimated under the proposed scheme, depending on the specific offence.

S1090 It is assumed that the offence which was chosen under the previous scheme would be the same as under the proposed scheme.
S1091 The relativity of fee payments between offences would change under the proposed scheme.
S1092 This would affect cases involving multiple offences as the offence charged for under the current scheme may change under the proposed scheme.

S1411 Mitigation 30.
S1412 The junior end of the advocacy profession tend to do many of the ancillary hearings, particularly in more complex cases.
