S0011 Climate Change Agreements Target Review 2016 and Buy-out Price Review: Discussion Paper
S0012 Introduction
S0013 1.
S0014 The Government set out its initial proposals for the Climate Change Agreements (CCA) Target Review 2016 in the Discussion Paper and Call for Evidence. **(Link: 5) This included a proposal for a staged process to review targets, including a sift and select process to examine the evidence where changes in realistic potential have occurred since targets were agreed in 2012. ** (Reference: 1)
S0015 This would inform decisions on selecting sectors for detailed review.
S0016 The Discussion Paper also stated that:
S0017 ‘The Government response to the Consultation on simplification, 2011, said “the buy-out price will be reviewed at the target review scheduled for 2016”.
S0018 We have no plans to adjust the buy-out price for the first two reporting periods of the Scheme i.e. before the end of 2016.
S0019 As part of the target review we will examine the extent of use of buy-out in Target Period 1, once this data becomes available, to inform any review of buy-out for Target Periods 3 and 4.’
S0020 2.
S0021 The Government Response to the CCA Target Review 2016: Discussion Paper and **(Link: 6) Call for Evidence **(Link: 7) confirmed that the Government would initiate preparatory work on sift and select, in line with the process for the target review that had been set out in the Discussion Paper and Call for Evidence and also that the target review would include a review of the buy-out price for Target Periods 3 and 4 (TP3 and TP4). ** (Reference: 2)
S0022 3.
S0023 The Government’s Response **(Link: 8) to the Business Energy Efficiency Tax Landscape Review announced that the CCA Target Review, including the review of the buy-out price for Target Periods 3 and 4 (2017-18 and 2019-20), which had been on hold pending the outcome of the Business Energy Efficiency Tax Landscape Review, would recommence later this year. ** (Reference: 3)
S0024 CCA Target Review 2016 – Summary of work undertaken and conclusion
S0025 4.
S0026 The Discussion Paper and Call for Evidence **(Link: 9) in 2014 sought views on changes to abatement measures and techniques since targets were agreed in 2012.
S0027 The Government Response to the CCA Target Review 2016: Discussion Paper and Call for **(Link: 10) Evidence **(Link: 11) reported on the outcome of the Call for Evidence, including stating that:
S0028 ‘of 30 responses received…a significant number of respondents signalled their view that there had been no change in the realistic potential available in their sectors since targets were set in 2012 and therefore no justification for revisiting the 2020 sector commitments’.
S0029 5.
S0030 The Department has carried out further analysis of the evidence on changes since target-setting in 2012, including analysis based on responses to the Call for Evidence.
S0031 The analysis has reflected the reduced window for deployment of measures before the start of Target Periods 3 and 4, and also takes account of the potential timetable for negotiations/cascade to Target Unit level.
S0032 On the basis of this, the Department has concluded there is limited evidence of changes relating to the realistic potential for energy efficiency or carbon savings since 2012.
S0033 Accordingly, the Department has decided not to take sectors forward for detailed target review.
S0034 Separate arrangements will apply to sawmilling and data centres as new sectors to the CCA scheme, joining in 2014.
S0035 Review of the Buy-out Price
S0036 6.
S0037 When CCA targets were agreed with sectors in 2012 the aim was to set these at a level that was challenging but achievable, with use of buy-out being available as an alternative to meeting targets for the purpose of remaining compliant with the Scheme.
S0038 The buy-out price for TP1 and TP2 was set in 2012 at £12/tCO2ei.
S0039 7.
S0040 The CCA Biennial Progress Report, **(Link: 12) covering 2013 and 2014, indicated that in Target Period 1 just over half of the target units (51%) met their target and that just under half of the target units (49%) used buy-out. ** (Reference: 4)
S0041 The total surplus – i.e. the amount by which target units delivered above their targets - for those target units that met their target was 4,088 kilotonnes of CO2e.
S0042 This was approximately twice the amount of total of CO2e that reflected underperformance from the target units that did not achieve their target (2,024 kilotonnes of CO2e).
S0043 Of the target units that did not achieve their target, 227 (14%) of these chose not to pay the buy-out price, but rather to leave the CCA scheme.
S0044 8.
S0045 While we have used the data from TP1 to inform the options on any future buy-out price, we note that these data relate only to the first of four target periods and the results for subsequent target periods may not be similar.
S0046 In particular, since there may be a time-lag before newly deployed abatement measures deliver their full energy savings, it is reasonable to expect that the use of buy-out would be greater in TP1 than in subsequent target periods.
S0047 9. We also note the views that were supplied in response to the 2014 Discussion Paper, including that 16 of 20 respondents were ‘not in favour of a future change to the price.
S0048 A number of reasons were cited.
S0049 Respondents said that: the … price of £12 per tonne has brought certainty to businesses; any changes would deter future investments; and incentives for participation would be eroded’.
S0050 Review of the Buy-out Price: Options
S0051 10.
S0052 The buy-out price for the first two target periods (2013-14 and 2015-16) was announced in the Government Response **(Link: 13) to the Consultation on Simplifying the CCA Scheme in January 2012.
S0053 This stated that the objective in setting the buy-out price was
S0054 to strike a balance between having a strong incentive to abate (i.e. to introduce measures to meet energy or carbon saving targets) and the financial impact, including any competitiveness issues, on participants.
S0055 This Response set the buy-out price for TP1 and TP2 at £12/tCO2e.
S0056 11.
S0057 Consistent with the Government’s objective to reduce carbon emissions cost-
S0058 effectively, we would now like to understand whether a change to the buy-out price would increase carbon savings under the CCA scheme.
S0059 A higher price would likely provide a sharper financial incentive to meet targets, however it may affect the right balance between an incentive to meet targets and avoiding competitiveness impacts.
S0060 In relation to this issue, we note that participants who remain compliant with the Scheme, will continue to benefit from CCA discounts until the present CCA scheme ends on 31 March 2023.
S0061 We also note that, since the Climate Change Levy (CCL) rates, which have increased since 2012, will continue to increase annually in line with the Retail Price Index until 2018/19 and will increase further in subsequent years to reflect the announced closure of the CRC Energy Efficiency Scheme (CRC). ** (Reference: 5)
S0062 The value of the CCL discount available to CCA participants will therefore have increased significantly since 2012.
S0063 The CRC allowance price has also increased since 2012 and will continue to increase in line with the Retail Price Index (RPI) until that scheme ends in 2019. ** (Reference: 6)
S0064 The Department is seeking views and evidence to inform a decision on whether the buy-out price should be adjusted for TP3 and TP4.
S0065 12.
S0066 The Department is considering a number of options for changing the buy-out price.
S0067 Of these, we are minded to increase the buy-out price in line with the rise in average CCL rate across fuels.
S0068 CCL rates increase in line with the Retail Price Index (Option 2).
S0069 However, we would welcome views, with evidence that underpins any views, on all the options.
S0070 Option 1 – Increase the buy-out price to £17/tCO2e for TP3 and TP4
S0071 13.
S0072 An increase in the buy-out price to £17/tCO2e would broadly align it with the published 2017/18 CRC allowance prices.
S0073 This would bring the price paid for carbon emissions associated with a shortfall in meeting targets broadly to the carbon price under the CRC.
S0074 Part of the rationale for setting the buy-out price at £12/tCO2e in 2012 was that this was the same as the CRC Phase 1 allowance price.
S0075 However, having regard to the
S0076 competitiveness impacts of this size of increase in the buy-out price, we have not proposed an increase to £17/tCO2e as our preferred option.
S0077 We are also mindful of the fact that the potential link between the buy-out price and CRC allowance price would only be relevant up to and including CRC compliance year 2018/19, based on the Budget 2016 announcement **(Link: 14) that participants will not be required to purchase CRC allowances to cover energy used after 31 March 2019.
S0078 Option 2 – Increase the buy-out price broadly in line with the RPI to £14/tCO2e for TP3 and TP4
S0079 14.
S0080 An increase in the buy-out price from £12/tCO2e, the level at which it was set in 2012, to £14/tCO2e is broadly in line with RPI and so ensures that it remains the same in real terms as when it was set initially.
S0081 This is our preferred option.
S0082 An increase in line with RPI that has regard to the need to strike a balance between providing a strong incentive to abate and the financial impact on participants.
S0083 The value of the CCL discount has increased in real terms since the start of the CCA scheme in 2013 and will continue to increase in value through the remaining years of the scheme.
S0084 It therefore seems reasonable that the value of the buy-out fee should also be increased for TP3 and TP4.
S0085 An increase in line with RPI would also be consistent with the approach taken to increasing the CRC allowance price.
S0086 Option 3 – Retain the present buy-out price of £12/tCO2e for TP3 and TP4
S0087 15.
S0088 We recognise the competitiveness issue associated with the buy-out price and will consider evidence that is submitted relating to this.
S0089 However, as set out above, the CCL has increased since 2012 and will continue to do so to 2018/19.
S0090 Furthermore, the increase of CCL in 2019 to reflect the ending of the requirement to purchase CRC allowances will further increase the value of the CCL discount to 2023.
S0091 This, coupled with the high level of use of buy-out in TP1, where 49% of target units used buy-out, means we have not chosen this as our preferred option.
