Ministerial foreword
S0010 This government is committed to building an economy that works fairly for everyone.
S0011 An important element of this is our continued commitment to improve transparency of company ownership.
S0012 Greater transparency of ownership brings many benefits.
S0013 Corporate Responsibility We know that market transparency fosters confidence and trust.
S0014 Transactions are improved and the market has greater confidence when people know who they are doing business with.
S0015 Transparency also plays a vital role in helping combat corruption and money laundering.
S0016 The UK is a world leader in corporate transparency.
S0017 In 2016 we became the first country in the G20 to introduce a register of company ownership, allowing the public to access a central record of information about who really owns and controls UK companies.
S0018 At the International Anti-Corruption Summit held in London in May 2016, we committed to go further, by creating a new register showing the beneficial owners of overseas companies that own or want to buy property in the UK, and of overseas companies involved in central government contracts.
S0019 The UK property market attracts a great deal of inward investment to the UK.
S0020 This investment benefits the UK economy and supports the construction industry and a host of connected trades across the country.
S0021 It is something that the UK government is committed to protecting.
S0022 Therefore it is important to ensure the integrity and reputation of the UK property market.
S0023 The UK property market should be seen as fair, transparent and clean in order to attract the right investors and owners.
S0024 Honest business should not fear that they are supporting criminal enterprise when investing in UK property.
S0025 A higher level of transparency will boost investor confidence.
S0026 These requirements will also extend to entities bidding for government contracts.
S0027 This will ensure that we, as government, know who we are dealing with, and that we meet the standards we expect of others.
S0028 This register will be the first of its kind in the world.
S0029 That is good news for the UK, enhancing our already strong reputation as an honest and trusted place to do business.
S0030 But it also means we need to proceed with care.
S0031 Our open economy benefits greatly from foreign companies looking to do business in Britain.
S0032 We need to make sure the new requirements are workable and proportionate, such that the UK remains an attractive place for foreign investment.
S0033 This call for evidence will help us test and refine our proposals to strike the right balance.
S0034 The government, working with the Scottish, Welsh and Northern Irish administrations, will ensure the UK remains a great place to do honest business, but that it is not a safe haven for illicit finance.
S0035 We look forward to engaging with interested parties to help us achieve this.

S0121 Introduction
Introduction
The importance of transparency of company ownership
S0122 1.
S0123 The creation of an independent vehicle through which people can join their interests
S0124 together to carry out business has been a remarkably successful and useful construct for over 150 years in the UK.
S0125 Well regulated company law is vital to the UK and supports the growth of a dynamic, innovative economy.
S0126 2.
S0127 In the UK there are a wide range of checks and balances covering the activities of
S0128 companies.
S0129 On incorporation and annually companies must provide or confirm information about their directors and their shareholders.
S0130 Most of this information is made available to the public through Companies House.
S0131 3.
S0132 This information is very important but although the shareholders are the legal owners of
S0133 the company, they may not be the ultimate owners or controllers of the company.
S0134 Companies may be owned through a chain of companies with an ultimate controller at the top, or individuals may appoint a nominee to hold their shares for them so that they don’t appear on a shareholder register.
S0135 These people are the beneficial owners of the company.
S0136 If they have a high level of interest or control, they can materially influence the way the company is run.
S0137 4.
S0138 So although the use of companies is a vital and important part of the UK economy,
S0139 complex company structures can create avenues for corruption, terrorist financing and money laundering, and in some instances can enable tax evasion.
S0140 A lack of transparency can also inhibit investment.
S0141 Investors generally choose to invest in less corrupt, more transparent economies. ** (Reference: 2)
What the government has done so far
S0142 5.
S0143 The UK is leading the world on improving corporate transparency.
S0144 In June 2016 we
S0145 became one of the first countries to introduce a central, publicly accessible, register of beneficial ownership called the people with significant control register (the PSC register).
S0146 Introduction
S0147 6.
S0148 Since the establishment of the PSC register, all companies incorporated in the UK must
S0149 give information about their people with significant control to Companies House with their annual confirmation statement.
S0150 Most of the information on the register is publicly available, with some necessary exceptions to protect information about individuals at risk.
S0151 7.
S0152 The PSC register is being populated over the course of a year, as and when UK
S0153 companies are required to file their annual confirmation statement.
S0154 By July this year it will be fully populated.
Rationale for further action
S0155 8.
S0156 The UK is the number one destination for foreign direct investment in Europe.
S0157 It is a
S0158 great strength of the economy that so many overseas companies want to invest and do business in the UK.
S0159 The overwhelming majority of companies that do so contribute productively and abide by the law.
S0160 9.
S0161 However, the government is concerned about the potential for illegal activity to take
S0162 place through overseas companies investing in the property sector.
S0163 Some properties are owned through off-shore companies in order to obscure their true owners.
S0164 This can make it difficult for regulators, legitimate businesses and the general public to know who the true owners are and can make it very difficult for law enforcement agencies to carry out effective investigations.
S0165 10.
S0166 The use of offshore corporate vehicles to obscure the true owners of UK property has
S0167 attracted those who wish to hide illicit funds and launder the proceeds of crime.
S0168 Between 2004 - 2014, over £180m worth of property in the UK has been investigated as suspected proceeds of corruption. In January 2016 the National Crime Agency reported the conviction of a money launderer who had used offshore companies to launder £12m stolen from Commerzbank through council properties in London. ** (Reference: 3)
S0169 The secrecy that these offshore companies provided made it difficult for investigators to identify who owned the properties.
S0170 Greater transparency of property ownership will make the job of enforcement agencies easier and will discourage criminals and the corrupt from choosing the UK to hide or launder their money.
S0171 11.
S0172 In addition to these benefits, greater transparency should also support a better
S0173 functioning property market.
S0174 There are many cases where tenants do not know who truly owns the property they are renting, where neighbours struggle to identify who is
S0175 Introduction
S0176 responsible for work on adjoining properties that has affected them, and where it is difficult to trace the people who control vacant property.
S0177 12.
S0178 The PSC register makes it possible to trace the true owners of registered properties
S0179 owned by companies incorporated in the UK.
S0180 This can be done by finding out the name of the UK company on the title register to the property (held at the relevant Land Registry) and then looking at that company’s PSC information held by Companies House.
S0181 However, the requirements of the PSC register, as set out in Part 21A of the Companies Act 2006, do not apply to companies incorporated outside the UK.
S0182 13.
S0183 For these reasons in March 2016 the government published a discussion paper looking ** (Reference: 4)
S0184 for views on how to enhance the transparency of beneficial ownership information for overseas companies investing in UK property.
S0185 A summary of the responses to this paper is included in annex A.
S0186 14.
S0187 Included within that discussion paper were parallel proposals to enhance transparency
S0188 for overseas companies engaging in UK government procurement.
S0189 The government is keen to ensure that where it contracts directly with overseas companies, it knows who it is dealing with.
S0190 Public authorities are accountable for the public money they spend.
S0191 We cannot allow corrupt individuals to profit from doing business with the UK government.
S0192 The UK government would also like to use its purchasing power to raise the global level of transparency.
S0193 15.
S0194 Following the discussion paper, the government announced that it intended to introduce
S0195 a register of the beneficial owners of overseas companies owning UK property or engaging in UK government procurement.
S0196 This was a key announcement of the International Anti-Corruption Summit in London in May 2016.
S0197 This policy is an important element of the forthcoming new anti-corruption strategy.
S0198 It will give assurance that the UK is a hostile environment for hiding the proceeds of corruption or laundering money.
This call for evidence
S0199 16.
S0200 The new register will be the first of its kind in the world, and builds upon the UK’s global
S0201 leadership in tackling corruption.
S0202 The downside of demonstrating such leadership is that we do not have a model to work from.
S0203 We will therefore proceed cautiously, striking the right balance between improving transparency and minimising burdens on legitimate commercial activity.
S0204 The government is keen to receive views and evidence on how best to achieve our objectives in an effective and efficient manner.

Scope
S0281 The scope of the new register
S0282 25.
S0283 There are a number of different types of legal entities that can hold property in the UK
S0284 and jurisdictions around the world provide a number of routes for people to create these entities.
S0285 These entities have different purposes and structures and are designed to enable businesses to operate in the best way for their business model.
S0286 Even though most jurisdictions around the world have a corporate vehicle equivalent to the UK’s company limited by shares, the way these are defined in law can vary considerably, and change over time.
S0287 26.
S0288 The government had initially conceived that the scope of the new register should be
S0289 focused on companies limited by shares.
S0290 However, further work and consultation has shown this approach would have significant drawbacks.
S0291 Firstly, it is often not clear from land registry records what type of entity holds a title, which would make it difficult to ensure compliance with a register that only included some of those entities.
S0292 Secondly, restricting the register purely to companies limited by shares may encourage the use of other less transparent entities as a means of owning property in the UK.
S0293 27.
S0294 For these reasons we propose including all legal entities that can hold properties or bid
S0295 on central government procurement contracts in the scope of the new register’s requirements.
S0296 28.
S0297 Some overseas companies that open a UK establishment already supply information to
S0298 Companies House as required under the Overseas Companies Regulations 2009.

S0303 Leasehold
S0304 29.
S0305 The government intends that entities that hold properties through certain types of lease
S0306 arrangements (leases that last over a certain number of years that are registered with the land registers) will also need to comply with the requirements of the register.
S0307 30.
S0308 The land registries have different arrangements for recording leaseholds, based on the
S0309 length of the lease.
S0310 Leases over 7 years are required to be registered in England and Wales, leases over 20 years are required to be registered in Scotland and leases over 21 years are required to be registered in Northern Ireland.
S0311 We propose that any overseas entities that are leaseholders of property where the lease is required to be registered and the original term is for more than 21 years should register their beneficial ownership information with Companies House.
S0312 This is because we aim only to capture leaseholds that are analogous to freeholds (for example, where a premium has been paid for the property up front).
S0313 These leaseholds tend to be longer in duration, and we believe that by including leases that are initially for a term of more than 21 years the register will only hold information on significant leases.

S0316 Defining a beneficial owner
S0317 31.
S0318 It is important that we identify who benefits from the legal entity and who exercises
S0319 control over it and the asset that it holds.
S0320 Whilst we have considered alternative definitions of beneficial ownership, we intend to adopt the existing definition that underpins the UK’s PSC register.
S0321 This definition was based on international best practice developed by the Financial Action Task Force and subsequently adopted in EU anti-money laundering legislation.
S0322 Using the same definition will avoid mismatches with information on UK companies and prevent manipulation of holding structures.
S0323 32.
S0324 The current definition is set out in Part 1 of Schedule 1A to the Companies Act 2006.
S0325 This states that a person is a PSC if they meet one or more of the following conditions in respect of a UK company:
S0326 i. Directly or indirectly holds more than 25% of the shares in the company.
S0327 Objectives, scope and impacts
S0328 ii.
S0329 Directly or indirectly holds more than 25% of the voting rights in the company.
S0330 iii.
S0331 Directly or indirectly holds the power to appoint or remove a majority of the board of directors of the company.
S0332 iv.
S0333 Otherwise has the right to exercise or actually exercises significant influence or control over the company.
S0334 (The meaning of ‘significant control’ is set out in the statutory guidance.) v. Has the right to exercise or actually exercises significant influence or control over a trust or firm that is not a legal entity, which meets one or more of conditions (i) to (iv). ** (Reference: 7)
S0335 (Further detail on this is also set out in the statutory guidance mentioned above.)
S0336 33.
S0337 Schedule 1A of the Companies Act 2006 also provides for situations where individuals
S0338 hold rights or shares collectively or in a joint arrangement.
S0339 This ensures that a beneficial owner cannot obfuscate their control or ownership of a company by diluting their shareholding amongst family members or other trusted individuals.
S0340 We propose that these provisions are also applied to the definition of a beneficial owner for the new overseas register.

S0342 Adaptations to definition of people with significant control
S0343 34.
S0344 The existing PSC legislation sets out some adaptations to the above definition to allow
S0345 for different types of entity.
S0346 These adaptations provide an interpretation of the conditions (i) to (v) above, including in circumstances where legal entities do not have share capital, or where they do not have general meetings at which voting rights can be exercised, or where they do not have a board of directors.
S0347 The adaptations require the conditions (i) to (v) to be interpreted in the following ways:
S0348 • Where legal entities do not have share capital – instead consider rights to a share of the capital or profits of the entity.
S0349 • Where legal entities do not have general meetings at which voting rights can be exercised – instead consider voting rights in relation to the entity that are equivalent to voting rights in a company.
S0350 • Where legal entities do not have a board of directors – instead consider rights to appoint or remove a majority of the equivalent management body of that entity.

Protection regime
S0836 This section looks at what protections might be needed to ensure that no individuals are put at risk because information about them is made public.
Protecting individuals at risk
S0837 112.
S0838 The new overseas register will be publicly accessible to ensure transparency and increase ease of scrutiny by law enforcement bodies and transparency groups.
S0839 There are, however, some situations where making information about an individual public would put that individual at risk of harm or would create a wider public safety risk.
S0840 This builds on a principle in the UK PSC register.
S0841 The PSC register has a protection regime which allows a company or individual to apply to have information about an individual with significant control suppressed if the individual is at risk of violence or intimidation as a result of that information being made public.
S0842 113.
S0843 We propose to provide for a beneficial owner or managing officer to apply to have their information suppressed on the new register in similar circumstances.
S0844 However we also consider that because the new overseas register will relate to individual properties the risk of harm to an individual or others may be increased by the individual’s association with the property being known.
S0845 We wish to consult on whether a more extensive regime may be appropriate for the new overseas register as it may include individuals’ residential addresses.
S0846 114.
S0847 For the new register, we propose enabling individuals to apply for their information to be suppressed based on four criteria:
S0848 1.
S0849 The individual is at risk of violence or intimidation due to the activities of the entity, or the way the property is being used.
S0850 2.
S0851 Certain characteristics or attributes of that individual when associated with that entity or property could put them or someone who lives with them at risk of violence or intimidation.
S0852 3. Publicly linking the property or entity to the individual will lead to an elevated public safety risk.
S0853 4. Protection for minors or people with diminished capacity.
S0854 We believe this may be necessary as a minor or someone with diminished capacity might be unable to make an informed decision about the purchase of the property, and therefore the inclusion of their name in the new register.
S0855 Protection regime
S0856 115.

S0869 118.
