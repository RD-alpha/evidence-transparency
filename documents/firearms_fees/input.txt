S0014 Introduction The Government administers a range of firearms licences covering: weapons which are prohibited under section 5 of the Firearms Act 1968; museum collections; and approved rifle clubs and muzzle-loading pistol clubs.
S0015 This is to ensure that firearms are only possessed subject to stringent controls and the highest standards of public safety are maintained.
S0016 Licences are issued free of charge, or, in the case of museums and shooting clubs, for a fee well below the cost of providing the service.
S0017 Currently, the cost to the taxpayer of firearms licences administered by the Government is estimated at around £700,000 per year.
S0018 The Government considers that, in common with many other licensing regimes, the costs of the firearms licensing regime should properly be attributed to the organisations benefiting from the licence, and not the taxpayer.
S0019 Charging in this way ensures the real economic cost of safeguarding high risk activities is understood by licence holders.
S0020 The Government introduced new charging powers for the firearms licences it issues through the Policing and Crime Bill, which is currently before Parliament.
S0021 This consultation sets out the Government’s proposed fee levels for prohibited weapons, museums and approved shooting clubs.
S0022 The proposed fees aim to recover in full the cost to the taxpayer, so that the licence holder pays for the cost of the service.
S0023 Once responses to the consultation have been considered, and subject to the enabling powers in the Policing and Crime Bill being passed by Parliament, fees will be set in secondary legislation.
S0024 It is anticipated that new fees will come into force in 2017.
S0025 Background In England, Wales and Scotland the police are responsible for issuing certificates for civilian firearms such as shotguns used by farmers or those used in recreational shooting.
S0026 Fees for the certificates administered by the police were increased in April 2015 following a public consultation. Separately, the Home Office (and in Scotland, the Scottish Government) issue three broad categories of firearms licence covering higher risk activities: ** (Reference: 1)
S0027 • prohibited weapons licences, authorising trade or possession of non-civilian weapons;
S0028 • museum licences, for museums with firearms collections which may include prohibited weapons;
S0029 • approved shooting clubs, allowing members without the relevant personal firearms certificate to shoot using club firearms.
S0030 The Home Office and Scottish Government administer these licences to provide an extra level of assurance, national consistency in decision-making and national oversight of the licensing regime, to reflect the increased risk associated with prohibited weapons and un-certificated shooters and ensure that the highest standards of public safety are maintained. ** (Reference: 2)
S0031 Currently there is no power to charge for prohibited firearms licences and, as a result, the cost of those licences is entirely subsidised by the public.
S0032 Museum and shooting club licences are subject to fees that were last reviewed in 1995 and do not cover the cost of the service.

S0038 The police and military do not require a licence.

S0042 These include private maritime security companies supplying armed protection from piracy to UK ships in the high-risk area, forensic service providers, gunsmiths that carry out deactivation of prohibited weapons or trade in pistols used for humane slaughter, and Olympic pistol shooters.
S0043 Applicants are subject to thorough checks by the Home Office or Scottish Government and the police before an authority is issued, including background checks, an assessment of suitability and proof of genuine business need to possess prohibited firearms.
S0044 These checks are repeated on renewal, which generally takes place every 3 years.

S0047 Introduction of new fees The Policing and Crime Bill was introduced to Parliament in February 2016. ** (Reference: 5)
S0048 The Bill provides for a new power enabling fees to be charged for prohibited firearms licences.
S0049 The Bill also amends existing charging powers for museum and shooting club licences under the Firearms (Amendment) Act 1988, ensuring consistency with the new prohibited weapons power so that all enabling powers provide flexibility to set fees at different rates for different types of application.
S0050 This consultation sets out the Government’s proposed fee levels for prohibited weapons, museums and approved shooting clubs.
S0051 The level proposed has been set with the aim of recovering the cost to the taxpayer in full, so the licence holder pays for the cost of the service.
S0052 Once responses to the consultation have been considered, and subject to the enabling powers in the Policing and Crime Bill being passed by Parliament, fees will be set in secondary legislation.
S0053 It is anticipated that new fees will come into force in 2017.
S0054 The enabling powers, the proposed fees in this consultation and any fees set subsequently through secondary legislation, will apply equally to England, Wales and Scotland.

S0070 This figure is for all activities associated with the administration, decision-making and oversight of approving licence applications.

S0072 The cost comprises staff salaries (including employer pension and national insurance contributions), staff training costs and overheads such as accommodation, IT and HR services.
S0073 It also includes development costs and annual running costs for a new online application system expected to be introduced before fees are implemented.

S0077 Police costs In order to assess each licence application, the Home Office and Scottish Government commission police checks which may include background checks on named individuals, interviews, site visits and stock checks.

S0195 Why is government intervention necessary?
S0196 Currently the Home Office and the Scottish Government issue licences for prohibited firearms (under s5 of the Firearms Act 1968) to relevant businesses, such as arms manufacturers, free of charge.
S0197 Providing this licensing service imposes a cost on the Home Office, the Scottish government and the police.
S0198 This cost is currently fully subsidised by the taxpayer.
S0199 Government intervention is needed to introduce fees to recover licensing costs.
S0200 At the same time, the Government wants to increase existing fees for museum and shooting club firearms licences to full cost recovery level.
S0201 The level of the fees for each licensee group will be set in secondary legislation at a later date.

S0273 A. Groups affected Police forces in England, Wales and Scotland ** (Reference: 11)
S0274 The police undertake significant work to assess applicants for Home Office firearms licences.
S0275 The Home Office commissions police checks which may include background checks on named individuals, interviews, site visits and stock checks.
S0276 The police therefore incur a cost for the work undertaken on the Home Office’s behalf and currently meet this cost from their own budget.
S0277 Police Scotland carry out the same work on behalf of the Scottish Government for licences issued there, again meeting the cost from their own budget.
S0278 Section 5 firearms dealers The largest user of the prohibited firearms licensing service is section 5 firearms dealers.
S0279 This covers any person who is registered in Great Britain under the 1968 Firearms Act who, by way of trade or business, manufactures, sells, transfers, repairs, tests or proves prohibited (s5) firearms or ammunition.
S0280 For example dealers may manufacture or supply arms, components or services to the police or Ministry of Defence, or to military and police forces abroad.
S0281 S5 firearms includes missiles, rockets and other projectiles, noxious gases, and vehicles and aircraft incorporating a ‘firearm’.
S0282 Some firearms dealers will deactivate and trade in collector’s items or act as forensic experts.
S0283 Foreign defence companies exhibiting at the London Defence and Security Equipment International exhibition (DSEI) may also require a Home Office licence and have been included under this category.
S0284 These examples are not exhaustive.
S0285 Dealers in non-prohibited firearms such as shotguns only require a Registered Firearms Dealer certificate from the police and do not need a Home Office or Scottish Government licence.
S0286 As at 31 March 2015 there were 3,422 police-registered firearms dealers in England, Wales and Scotland. ** (Reference: 13)
S0287 As at 31 December 2015 there were an estimated 587 section 5 authorised dealers in prohibited firearms. ** (Reference: 14)
S0288 DSEI firearms dealers Foreign defence and security companies exhibiting at the biannual Defence and Security Equipment International exhibition (DSEI) in London may also require a Home Office licence if they need to possess prohibited weapons in the UK as part of the exhibition.
S0289 In 2015 temporary prohibited weapons licences were granted to 36 companies for the purpose of exhibiting at the DSEI.
S0290 Section 5 firearms carriers Some companies are authorised only for the transport of s5 firearms.
S0291 These include airlines, shipping companies and freight / logistics companies.
S0292 Because they do not store or trade in weapons the licensing process is less onerous.
S0293 As at 31 December 2015 there were an estimated 321 s5 authorised carriers. ** (Reference: 15)
S0294 Private Maritime Security Companies Private Maritime Security Companies (PMSCs) are authorised by the Home Office to enable their guards to be armed with prohibited firearms to protect UK ships from piracy in the designated ‘high risk’ areas.
S0295 Guards are authorised under the company’s licence and not as individuals.
S0296 All applications, including those by Scottish companies, are managed by the Home Office.
S0297 As at 31 December 2015 there were 12 authorised PMSCs. ** (Reference: 16)
S0298 Employees of dealers, carriers and PMSCs Employees of s5 authorised companies will need to be vetted if they have access to firearms.
S0299 Armed guards working for PMSCs are vetted.
S0300 However, because vetting of employees is only undertaken as part of an organisation’s application, the individual is not (in most circumstances) required to apply for a licence and will not be charged any fee.
S0301 Employees are therefore unlikely to be affected directly by the introduction of fees.
S0302 As at 31 December 2015, there were 322 authorised maritime armed guards. ** (Reference: 17)
S0303 The Government does not cap the number of employees working for other licence holders and therefore the number is not recorded centrally.
S0304 Museums Museums may have collections of non-prohibited or prohibited firearms, either on display or in storage.
S0305 The museums licence covers all firearm types.
S0306 As at 31 December 2015 there were 164 authorised museums. ** (Reference: 18)
S0307 Five of the museums currently licensed are national museums.
S0308 National museums are part funded by a direct grant from the Government.
S0309 Shooting clubs Shooting clubs may make non-prohibited firearms available to their members, or provide facilities for members to use their own weapons.
S0310 Members must acquire an individual firearms certificate from the police for any weapons they own, but do not need a certificate to shoot using firearms provided by a approved club.
S0311 Schools with shooting clubs or military cadet schemes also come under this category and operate in the same way.
S0312 As at 31 December 2015, there were 434 approved shooting clubs. ** (Reference: 19)
S0313 Other There is nothing in law defining who can acquire a prohibited (s5) firearms licence – it is a matter of Government policy.
S0314 The categories listed at figure 1 are the most common.
S0315 ‘Other’ comprises the GB Olympic and Commonwealth shooting teams and historical weapons found and kept as trophies of war.
S0316 It is possible further groups could emerge in the future, for example granting of licences to maritime security companies was only introduced following a decision by the Prime Minister in 2011.
S0317 The Government proposes using the enabling powers under the Policing and Crime bill (once enacted) to introduce fees for prohibited firearms licences issued by the Home Office and the Scottish Government.
S0318 The public consultation sets out proposed fees with a view to licence holders paying the full cost of the service.
S0319 This is in line with the position set out in HM Treasury’s ‘Managing Public Money’ guidance. Prohibited firearms licences are currently free of charge, and therefore fully subsidised by the taxpayer, because there was previously no power in primary legislation to charge a fee. ** (Reference: 20)
S0320 Introducing fees for prohibited firearms licences will also eliminate inconsistency and cross-subsidies across the licensing regime.
S0321 Following a 2010 recommendation by the Home Affairs Select Committee and a public consultation in 2014, fees for non-prohibited firearms licences (administered by the police) were increased in April 2015.
S0322 Museums and shooting clubs (administered by the Home Offic and Scottish Government) also already pay a fee for their licences.
S0323 Meanwhile the prohibited firearms licensing service is fully subsidised by the public.
S0324 There are already powers under the Firearms (Amendment) Act 1988 to charge for museum and shooting club licences.
S0325 Fees are charged as set out in Figure 1 although these were last reviewed in 1995 and do not cover the full cost of the service.
S0326 The Policing and Crime Bill amends existing enabling powers so that they provide enough flexibility to set fees at different rates for different services.
S0327 These amendments bring existing powers in line with the new s5 power to give a consistent set of charging powers.
S0328 The consultation document, based on the new unified charging powers, sets out a detailed proposed fee regime which ensures applicants only pay the cost of what they use.
S0329 Fees will be implemented at a later date through secondary legislation.
S0330 The Home Office firearms licensing service, including prohibited firearms, museums and shooting clubs, will cost an estimated £596,000 in 2016-17, plus an estimated £69,000 in cost to the police (for their role in carrying out checks on applicants). ** (Reference: 21, 22)
S0331 We estimate the respective costs to the Scottish Government and Police Scotland to be an additional 8% each. This creates an overall cost of £712,000. ** (Reference: 23)
S0332 Government intervention is needed to allow some or all of this cost to the taxpayer to be recovered once fees are implemented through secondary legislation, expected to be in 2017.

S0334 In common with many other licensing regimes, the Government considers that the costs of the firearms licensing regime should properly be attributed to the organisations benefiting from the licence, and not the taxpayer.
S0335 Charging will ensure the real economic cost of safeguarding high risk activities is considered by licence holders.
S0336 Fees will enable us to fund the cost of providing an improved service once the proposed online application system is introduced.
S0337 The lack of the necessary enabling powers has previously prevented this change being made.

S0342 The taxpayer continues to subsidise the full cost of the Home Office and Scottish prohibited firearms licensing services.
S0343 Museum and shooting club fee levels remain at current levels with the vast majority of the cost subsidised by the taxpayer.

S0547 Licence holders The Home Office plans to introduce a new online application system before fees are implemented.

S0549 The online system may also produce efficiency savings for the Home Office.
S0550 These savings would be passed on to applicants through reduced fees.

S0559 Option 1: Do nothing There has been media and Parliamentary criticism of the public subsidising firearms licences administered by the police, at a time when public spending needs to be reduced.
S0560 These fees were increased to full cost recovery in April 2015.
S0561 Without a power to charge for prohibited firearms licences, the taxpayer and existing fee payers will subsidise this aspect of licensing to an increasing degree as costs increase over time.
S0562 Option 2: Introduce a new set of enabling powers to allow fees to be charged There is a risk that small companies whose trade involving prohibited weapons is low value could stop this aspect of business activity if profit is not high enough to justify the cost of the licence.
S0563 Equally, museums and shooting clubs whose activities requiring a firearms licence are of low value to the public or club members may stop those activities because the cost of the licence is not justified.
