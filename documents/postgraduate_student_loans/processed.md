Introduction
S0016 1.
S0017 A doctorate qualification is important for an academic career and increasingly also for many highly skilled professions in the economy.
S0018 Doctoral students make a vital contribution to the UK’s world class research base and, through the ideas and skills that they develop, to British industrial performance and to improved economic productivity.
S0019 The Government fully recognises the importance of PhDs to the country’s economic success.
S0020 2.
S0021 The Government’s 2015 Manifesto included a commitment to introduce a new loan for doctoral students, alongside the introduction of a new master’s loan.
S0022 The master’s loan launched successfully in Academic Year 2016/17 and in Budget 2016 the Government announced its intention to launch the doctoral loan.
S0023 At that time, the Government indicated that the new loan would provide up to £25,000 to eligible students starting their studies from Academic Year 2018/19 onwards.
S0024 We also said that we would consult on the technical detail.
S0025 3.
S0026 The new doctoral loan is intended to take a similar form to the new postgraduate master’s loan, in that the loan will:
S0027 • provide a contribution to the costs of study rather than covering the full fees and living costs of a student,
S0028 • be paid directly to the student rather than to the student’s institution,
S0029 • involve a single repayment of 6% above the income threshold (currently £21,000) to cover the combined balance of any postgraduate masters and postgraduate doctoral loan.
S0030 4.
S0031 In November 2016, the Department for Education launched its public consultation on technical details of the postgraduate doctoral loan.
S0032 The consultation sought views on specific features of the proposed loan, including who should be eligible, what courses should be covered, other terms of the loan and practical issues to support the new loan’s implementation.
S0033 5.
S0034 The consultation closed in December.
S0035 We received over 300 responses, from sector and student representative bodies, individual Higher Education Institutions, charities, students, and other members of the public.
S0036 We passed all the consultation responses securely to an independent reviewer, Pye Tait, for their analysis and consideration.
S0037 The report Pye Tait produced is published alongside this response.
S0038 In addition to this report, we have sought to ensure that all responses to the consultation have been considered in development of the final product.
Why introduce doctoral loans
S0039 6.
S0040 The Government’s consultation sought views on technical aspects of the design of the new loan product.
S0041 The rationale for a doctoral loan was not the subject of the consultation, but the responses have provided further insight to support the case for doctoral loans.
S0042 The past year has also seen increased focus on the role of the UK’s research base in general and doctoral students in particular for Britain’s longer term prosperity.
S0043 7.
S0044 A strong cadre of talented researchers is vital to sustain the excellence of UK research and innovation and to deliver our future ambitions.
S0045 The Government investment in Research and Development (R&D) announced at Autumn Statement 2016 will support excellent research and focus on areas that support the UK’s Industrial Strategy and our research priorities.
S0046 The doctoral loan will complement this investment, making a further contribution to expanded doctoral study.
S0047 8.
S0048 The new loan will be the first direct Government funding for doctoral study available to students on an open to all basis, i.e. available to all eligible students who have gained a place on an eligible programme of study.
S0049 Other central support is competitively awarded, and focused on particular areas of the research base.
S0050 The new doctoral loan has the potential to reach a wider range of students with doctoral potential, and support new research areas and activities.
S0051 The loan will:
S0052 • Help overcome financial barriers to undertaking the long commitment that is a doctoral programme of study and therefore enable more students to achieve this level of qualification.
S0053 • Help students reduce the time taken to complete their study – completion is a known challenge in later years for all students and the availability of the loan will help eligible students find the time (for example, by cutting back on working commitments) to complete their thesis.
S0054 • Help particular Higher Education Institutions who may currently benefit less than their peers from Research Council funding to expand their research programmes.
S0055 • Help the Research Base overall by providing a further form of support for the doctoral students that form such a key part of the academic research community.
S0056 9.
S0057 Doctoral students are crucial to support innovation in Higher Education institutions through the commitment, fresh perspective and detailed work they do to contribute to original research within institutions.
S0058 They are an important asset for collaboration, internationally, with industry, and with other research institutions and Universities.
S0059 10.
S0060 Doctoral students are also in high demand from employers and industry: 92% leaving full-time PhD study were in employment or further study six months later compared to 89% for first degree graduates. Doctoral graduates play a key role in sectors outside Higher Education: 7-9 years after graduation 50% of doctorate holders are working in sectors outside Higher Education, with 20% working in manufacturing, engineering and R&D. ** (Reference: 1)
S0061 For 75% of industry employers, the loss of their doctoral graduates would have either a business critical or significant impact on operations. ** (Reference: 2)
S0062 11.
S0063 The OECD has also found that knowledge-based capital is a key driver of economic growth in advanced economies and is increasingly the largest form of business investment. The UK is one such economy whose comparative advantage is disproportionately derived from R&D and innovation intensive sectors. ** (Reference: 3,4)
S0064 In this context, ensuring that there is a sufficient supply of highly skilled researchers is increasingly important and a key component of the government’s overall strategy.
S0065 The Government therefore considers that the benefits of providing a loan for doctoral study will outweigh the cost of public subsidy involved in this new student finance product.
S0066 Given the novel nature of the loan, the Government intends to review the product within 5 years.
S0067 12.
S0068 By contributing to overcoming barriers associated with a lack of finance, the doctoral loan will benefit individuals, employers and the UK research base, and indirectly the wider population and economy.
S0069 13.
S0070 The rest of this document focuses on the decisions we have made following the public consultation on detail of the design of the new loan.
The existing doctoral landscape
S0071 A doctoral degree is classified in England as a Level 8 qualification in the Quality Assurance Agency’s Higher Education Framework for Higher Education Qualifications.
S0072 This qualification category includes Doctor of Philosophy qualifications (PhDs or DPhils) as well as professional doctorates (such as Doctor of Education or Doctor of Business Administration).
S0073 The main objective of a doctoral qualification is for students to make an original contribution to the existing knowledge of their subject discipline, either through undertaking new research, or by applying existing research and knowledge in a new way.
S0074 Doctoral students have until now received funding from a number of different sources, including:
S0075 • Research Council studentships and support;
S0076 • University studentships, grants and support;
S0077 • Industry and charity supported scholarships and awards;
S0078 • Employer support; and
S0079 • Self-funding by the student.
S0080 Public funding for research in English higher education is currently administered under a 'dual support’ system.
S0081 This system combines:
S0082 • Project funding for excellent research proposals that are forward-looking and assessed through peer review.
S0083 This funding stream is managed by the Research Councils.
S0084 This provides funding to select HEIs on a competitive basis, and includes the provision of a limited number of full research council studentships which HEIs award to their doctoral students.
S0085 These take the form of an award to cover fees, and a maintenance stipend depending on eligibility.
S0086 Such funding is provided to the individual student.
S0087 • Formula based quality-related funding, that rewards HEI performance based on peer review and proven impact from the research.
S0088 This funding stream is routed through the Higher Education Funding Council for England (HEFCE)).
S0089 Funding is provided to HEIs under this stream.

Main findings from the consultation
S0095 15.
S0096 The full findings of the consultation are available in the independent Analysis of Consultation Responses published alongside this response.
S0097 This section sets out the conclusions from the external consultation analysis in the key areas on which the Government sought views.
S0098 The conclusions of the external analysis are as follows:
S0099 1.
S0100 The age restriction of ‘59 and under’ strikes an appropriate balance between enabling the broadest possible access to doctoral study and mitigating the risk of non-repayment;
S0101 2.
S0102 The doctoral loan is unlikely to incentivise large numbers of students to study purely for personal benefit;
S0103 3. There is little support for an alternative proposal of capping the number of doctoral loan places per institution;
S0104 4.
S0105 The proposed maximum course length of six years would benefit students who need to study part time.
S0106 Additional flexibility may be needed for some students and, according to more than 30 universities, the Government should consider extending the loan length to seven or eight years;
S0107 5.
S0108 To ensure timely completion of doctoral programmes, universities are generally favourable to the idea of reporting students’ progress as a trigger for loan payments, however, the frequency of progress reporting currently varies between institutions;
S0109 6. There is strong support among respondents for the loan to be available for all Level 8 doctoral degrees;
S0110 7.
S0111 The proposed approach of basing loan eligibility on a student’s qualification intentions appears to be sensible, but would need clear guidance and FAQs for applicants;
S0112 8.
S0113 The loan ought to complement other forms of public and private funding for doctoral programmes, in the interests of ensuring choice for students.
Question analysis
Individual eligibility
S0114 Do you think that the proposed age eligibility restriction of 59 and under (on the first day of the first academic year of the course) is a proportionate way of mitigating the risk of low value for money?
S0115 16.
S0116 Respondents were invited to express their views on whether there should be an age eligibility restriction on this product, and if so, what it should be.
S0117 The consultation proposed that the loan would only be available to students 59 and under, and we asked a number of questions around this and other age restrictions.
S0118 This restriction was proposed as a way to ensure value for money to the public, due to the risk associated with offering loans to older students who were unlikely to be able to fully repay the loan.
S0119 17.
S0120 Almost two thirds of consultation respondents, including the majority of responding universities, agreed that some form of age restriction would be appropriate to mitigate this risk (201 respondents representing 65% of total respondents).
S0121 Of the respondents who supported this feature, the proposed age cap of 59 was the most popular, also receiving support from the National Union of Students (NUS), Universities UK (UUK) and the Russell Group (112 respondents, representing 38% of total respondents).
S0122 Among the respondents not in favour of any age restriction, common views were that education and lifelong learning should be accessible to everyone, and that an age cap would be inherently “discriminatory”.
Government response
S0123 18.
S0124 The Government confirms its intention to make the loan available to individuals 59 and under on the first day of the first academic year of their course.
S0125 The Government sees this age restriction as justified to mitigate the risks to the value for money of the product.
S0126 Setting the restriction at this age is appropriate and broadly supported by the evidence from the consultation.
S0127 It strikes the best balance between access and affordability, excluding only the oldest cohort of students, who are the least likely to make significant or any repayments prior to retirement.
S0128 The majority of consultation respondents, including universities, student representative groups, and both current and prospective students, supported this reasoning that an age restriction was a proportionate way of mitigating the risk of low value for money.
Possible additional eligibility requirements
S0129 Do you have any views on an alternative to a demand led product, which would make the loan available only at institutions that had been allocated a capped
S0130 number of doctoral loan places?
S0131 The number of loans allocated to HEIs would be made according to an existing metric (e.g. HEFCE’s allocation of Research Degree Programme supervision funding).
S0132 19.
S0133 Respondents were asked about their views on introducing a capped number of doctoral loan places with these allocated to Higher Education Institutions on the basis of an existing metric.
S0134 This was an alternative suggestion to ensure the affordability of the product.
S0135 The consultation suggested that the number of loan places per Institute could be allocated using HEFCE’s allocation of Research Degree Programme (RDP) supervision funding.
S0136 This idea was proposed as a way of guaranteeing that public money was focused on centres of excellence, which could choose the most suitable students to access the loan, thereby introducing a competitive element into the scheme.
S0137 20.
S0138 Organisational respondents disagreed strongly with the proposal to cap the number of loan places available.
S0139 The reasons that were given included objections to the principle of capping places and objections to the metric suggested for allocating them to HEIs.
S0140 It was felt that this could limit opportunities for widening participation in postgraduate education and stifle smaller and more specialised institutions.
S0141 There was also concern that such a system would be difficult to implement, especially for institutions in the devolved administrations.
S0142 The small minority of organisations in favour of such a measure referred to existing evidence that pointed to postgraduate training being most effective and of the highest quality where it is concentrated in the highest quality research environments.
S0143 These respondents felt that a competitive system was more likely to deliver quality.
Government response
S0144 21.
S0145 The Government has decided to introduce the loan product on a demand led basis.
S0146 The doctoral loan will be available to all eligible students in relation to all eligible courses.
S0147 Applicants will apply directly to the Student Loans Company for the loan, and there will be no institutional cap on number of loan places.
S0148 The Government wants to ensure that the doctoral loan product is available to a broad range of institutions and individuals, to broaden and strengthen the research base.
S0149 The Government is also keen to avoid unnecessary administrative burdens for institutions.
S0150 The Government acknowledges the important place that institutions with research excellence have within the research landscape, as is already reflected in the allocation of existing Research Council funding.
S0151 The Government wants doctoral loans to play a complementary role to existing funding streams by providing a source of finance for all eligible doctoral programmes and students.
Course length
S0152 Does making the loan available for courses of up to six years give a sufficient amount of time to enable part-time study, whilst also incentivising students to complete their doctorates within a set period?
S0153 22.
S0154 The Government consulted on the basis that the maximum course length eligible to receive the loan would be six years.
S0155 Six years was initially chosen as a suitable compromise between allowing all students, including those who are part-time, to complete their studies to the best of their ability, while also ensuring that students were suitably encouraged to complete their doctorates within a set timeframe.
S0156 The Government did not want students who may have originally completed their doctorate within three or four years to be incentivised to take longer due to the maximum length of the loan.
S0157 23.
S0158 While the majority of respondents (222 respondents, representing 77% of total respondents) agreed with the Government’s proposal, views varied considerably between respondent groups, with half of universities responding disagreeing with the proposed length.
S0159 The majority of student bodies and research-focused organisations also did not believe that six years would be sufficient for all students, suggesting instead a maximum of either seven or eight years.
S0160 Organisations pointed out that many universities have existing four-year full time courses, for which they allow part time completion within eight years.
S0161 They also noted that the Research Councils UK also set an eight-year maximum.
S0162 They argued that to restrict eligible courses to six years would make a number of part time courses ineligible, where their full-time equivalents would be four-year courses.
Government response
S0163 24.
S0164 The Government has decided that the loan will be available for doctoral programmes up to eight years in duration.
S0165 We want the loan product to widen participation in doctoral study.
S0166 This includes for students studying on a part-time basis, either by choice or due to necessity, who might have struggled to access financial support before.
S0167 This change will allow four-year programmes to be completed at 50% intensity, and cover courses in which a part-time doctorate includes a six-year period of research with an additional writing up year.
S0168 By extending to eight years, the new loan will now cover all but a small minority of full- and part-time doctoral courses available in the sector.
S0169 The maximum programme length within which the HEI requires the student to submit their thesis will determine course length.
Course eligibility
S0170 Do you have any views on the proposed loan being available to all Level 8 doctoral degrees, including professional doctorates (e.g. DBA etc.)?
S0171 25.
S0172 The Government intends for the loan to be used to widen access and participation in doctoral study, across a wide range of HEIs and research fields.
S0173 To ensure this happens, the loan was intended to be available to all Level 8 degrees, including professional doctorates as well as the traditional specialist knowledge doctorates (i.e. PhDs).
S0174 This would ensure that the new loan product supports the widest range of students, and allows for benefits to be felt in all subjects and fields of research.
S0175 26.
S0176 There was strong support for the proposal to make the loan available to all Level 8 doctoral programmes of research.
S0177 Almost all consultation respondents strongly agreed with the Government’s intention; the majority of respondents felt that this would create a level playing field between subject areas, allowing for development across the entire postgraduate research base.
S0178 From an operational perspective, respondents also felt this would make the loan much easier for institutions to manage and maintain, and be simpler for students to understand.
S0179 A small minority of respondents raised some issues over whether PhD by published work qualifications should be eligible for loan funding considering that the loan could be paid retroactively.
S0180 A small number of respondents also questioned whether professional doctorates, which attract high levels of funding from other sources, such as the NHS, should be eligible for the loan product.
Government response
S0181 27.
S0182 The Government can confirm that the loan will be available for all Level 8 doctoral programmes of research, as recognised by the QAA.
S0183 However, to avoid inappropriate use of public money, PhD by published work will be ineligible for the loan.
S0184 Students whose course aim is a research qualification awarded primarily on the basis of published works will be excluded from student finance loans on the basis that they are not actively studying.
S0185 This is in line with HEFCE guidance on eligibility for receiving research funding from HEFCE, which requires that students are actively supervised by an academic member of staff employed by a higher education provider.
S0186 As such, PhD by published work, where individuals are not enrolled within an active programme of study, are not eligible courses.
S0187 28.
S0188 In accordance with this rationale, Higher Doctorates (beyond the established Level 8 QAA rating) will not be eligible for the loan.
S0189 These qualifications are awarded sparingly and represent significant contributions to the subject area.
S0190 They are therefore mostly awarded as honourary degrees, after the work in question has been published or completed, rather than when the individual is enrolled at a Higher Education Institute and undertaking an active doctoral course.
S0191 29.
S0192 As is explained below in the section on duplicative funding, any student receiving an NHS bursary for doctoral study will not be eligible to take out the doctoral loan.
S0193 This follows the original rationale for not making students in receipt of research council funding eligible for the loan: that direct Government funding is already available to such students.
S0194 30.
S0195 Full details of course eligibility will be published in advance of applications opening for AY2018/19.
Other sources of funding
S0196 How might HEIs and others provide new and innovative packages of support to help students meet the costs of study not covered by the loan?
S0197 31.
S0198 The loan of up to £25,000 is intended as a contribution to the cost of doctoral study; while it can be of considerable help to individuals as they fund their studies, the amount being offered means that students will have to find alternative sources of funding for the rest of their doctorate costs.
S0199 In light of this, respondents to the consultation were asked how institutions and other organisations might provide new and innovative packages of support to help meet those costs of study that are not covered by the loan.
S0200 32.
S0201 The Universities who responded generally suggested that the new loan would encourage them to consider how to combine funding approaches and to look at new partnership opportunities.
S0202 A small number emphasised that existing modes of financial support that they offer, such as scholarships, would continue unchanged.
S0203 Individuals also suggested that Universities would be able to offer new arrangements to students in light of the loan; however, many strongly emphasised that existing grant funding from Universities and the rest of the HE sector should not be limited.
Government response
S0204 33.
S0205 The Government welcomes the views of Universities and other Higher Education Providers that the loan will encourage them to develop new funding packages for students.
S0206 The Government encourages the HEIs and other sponsors to incorporate the proposed product into their own finance packages, to help support doctoral students in creative and innovative ways.
Duplicative funding
S0207 The proposed loan would only be available to students who do not receive a Research Council studentship (this includes fees-only awards).
S0208 Apart from Research Council funding, how should other sources of public funding be treated when considering whether to offer the loan?
S0209 34.
S0210 The Government proposed that any student who is in receipt of a Research Councils studentship, including a fees-only award, would not be eligible to receive the loan.
S0211 The consultation sought views from respondents on how other sources of public
S0212 funding should be treated in respect of the loan, to avoid two streams of government money being directly used to fund the same individual.
S0213 35.
S0214 A wide range of views were expressed in response to this question.
S0215 While there was general support for public money not being spent twice, some Universities raised concerns about how to administratively determine and monitor which students have received other types of student funding.
S0216 A small proportion of individual respondents felt that only students who were financially disadvantaged should have access to the loan.
S0217 36.
S0218 The exclusion of Research Council funding received mixed feedback within the consultation, with half of the universities responding to the question expressing concern around the proposed exclusion.
S0219 These respondents suggested that fees- only students might be “disadvantaged” when compared to those obtaining other grants or bursaries from other sources.
S0220 A small minority of respondents noted that ‘public money’ could be defined to include funding from wider public sector employers, university scholarships, and charities, and yet that there was no intention to exclude recipients of such funding from the scope of the proposed loan.
Government response
S0221 37.
S0222 The Government can confirm that students in receipt of either Research Council studentships, whether full or fees only, or other direct forms of Government support for students maintenance and fee costs, including any salary contributions or fees provided by NHS for the purposes of doctoral study, will be ineligible for the new loan.
S0223 These funds are provided from direct Government funding and it is both feasible and appropriate to limit a student to just one source of such public finance.
S0224 The Government has considered the position of Research Council funded students who are only eligible for a fees-only award.
S0225 However, as eligibility for a full studentship including stipend is based on residency criteria, the Government believes that the product consulted on is appropriate.
S0226 Final details of any specific exclusions and eligibility criteria will be published in advance of the loan opening for applications in AY18/19.
Repayment terms
S0227 The repayment terms have been chosen based on a number of key principles, including affordability and value for money.
S0228 Are there any potential impacts of students taking out the loan that we may not have considered?
S0229 38.
S0230 The Government has proposed that the doctoral loan product would have the same repayment arrangements as the existing master’s loan.
S0231 The loan would therefore be combined with any outstanding balance the individual already has from the postgraduate master’s loan.
S0232 It would be income contingent and repaid only when the
S0233 individual’s income exceeds the set repayment threshold, currently £21,000 annually.
S0234 This threshold may change over time.
S0235 Repayments would be made through a single 6% of the borrower’s above-threshold income.
S0236 39.
S0237 These repayment terms were generally described by respondents as simple, fair and appropriate, particularly the approach of combining master’s and doctoral loans into a single payment for calculating repayments.
S0238 A small minority of respondents raised concerns about the combined effect of multiple loans (undergraduate, postgraduate and doctoral) and increased levels of indebtedness of graduates.
Government response
S0239 40.
S0240 The Government can confirm that the doctoral loan will be repaid as 6% of eligible income above the repayment threshold for such loans, currently set at £21,000.
S0241 For students with an existing master’s loan, the doctoral and master’s loans will be treated as a single balance, subject to one combined repayment.
S0242 Any outstanding balance on the doctoral loan will be written off 30 yearsafter the loan becomes subject to repayment.
S0243 The Government recognises the long-term financial commitment involved in taking on the new doctoral product.
S0244 The combination of the loan with other postgraduate loans will ensure that graduates in repayment with an undergraduate loan continue to face a maximum 15% contribution from above threshold income.
S0245 As all student loan repayment is income contingent, repayment will always be affordable for students.
Equality
S0246 Are there other issues the Government should be aware of, which could impact on the take-up of the proposed loan by those who possess a protected characteristic?
S0247 41.
S0248 The Government thanks respondents for their views on the potential effects of the loan on protected groups.
S0249 The Government has completed an Equality Assessment (EA) to assess, in compliance with its Public Sector Equality Duty, the likely impact of the new loan product on people with protected characteristics.
S0250 The features of the loan have been specifically designed to mitigate,where possible, any negative impact felt by individuals with protected characteristics.
S0251 42.
S0252 The issues raised by respondents have been taken into account in preparing the EA and in reaching decisions on individual elements of the new loan’s design.
S0253 The main points made by respondents regarding the potential impact of the product on groups with protected characteristics have already been set out in the relevant sections of this Response.
S0254 The EA is published alongside this Response.

Annex B: List of final features of the doctoral loan product
S0325 This list includes the key features of the doctoral loan, which will be launched for the 2018/19 academic year.
S0326 A complete list of the product’s terms and conditions and eligibility of students and courses for it will be made available in the SLC’s product guidance to students and HEIs.
S0327 This will be available before the launch of the loan.
Loan Amount
S0328 1. Eligible individuals will be able to borrow up to £25,000 for the purpose of completing an eligible postgraduate doctoral qualification.
Purpose
S0329 2.
S0330 The loan is intended to be a contribution to the cost of doctoral study.
S0331 The student can use the loan as they wish towards the cost of the qualification, whether on tuition, maintenance, or any other costs associated with study.
Individual Eligibility Criteria
S0332 3. Higher Education provision is a devolved matter for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland and therefore UK nationals, or individuals with settled status in the UK, ordinarily resident in the Devolved Administrations will not be eligible for the loan.
S0333 The eligibility of EU students for the doctoral loan will be announced by the Government ahead of the 2018/19 academic year.
S0334 4.
S0335 The loan will be available for students ordinarily resident in England who wish to study in the Devolved Administrations.
S0336 5. Individuals will only be eligible for the loan if they are aged 59 or under on the first day of the first academic year of their course.
S0337 6.
S0338 Individual eligibility will not be based on a means test.
S0339 7. Individuals already holding a doctoral qualification, an equivalent level qualification or a higher level qualification will not be eligible for the loan.
S0340 Qualifications obtained outside the UK will be taken into account in determining an individual’s eligibility for the doctoral loan.
S0341 8.
S0342 The loan will only be available to those starting an eligible level 8 qualification from Academic Year 2018/19.
Course Eligibility Criteria
S0343 9.
S0344 The loan will be available for most Level 8 qualifications provided by a HEI with recognised degree awarding powers where these involve an active programme of study, in accordance with the QAA.
S0345 Doctorates by retrospective publication are ineligible for the loan.
S0346 Full details of course eligibility, and guidance for HEIs, will be published in advance of applications opening for the loan.
S0347 10.
S0348 Students will only be eligible for the loan if the programme of study is a minimum of 3 years and a maximum of 8 years.
Institutional Eligibility Criteria
S0349 11.
S0350 All Higher Education Institutes in the UK with QAA recognised Research Degree Awarding Powers are eligible for students to access the loan.
S0351 12.
S0352 Alternative providers are defined as providers of higher education which do not receive annual funding from HEFCE, or any other direct annual public funding.
S0353 Only alternative providers who have been reviewed by QAA and received recognised Research Degree Awarding powers will be counted as eligible institutions for the purposes of the loan.
Repayment Terms
S0354 13.
S0355 The loan interest will be calculated at RPI+3% and interest will accrue from the date the first loan instalment is paid by SLC to the borrower.
S0356 14.
S0357 Repayments of the doctoral loan will be contingent upon and commence once the borrower has an annual income of £21,000 or more.
S0358 15.
S0359 The £21,000 annual income threshold will be initially frozen until 2021 and subject to review.
S0360 16.
S0361 Repayments of the doctoral loan will be made through the existing category for postgraduate loans in the student finance system.
S0362 The loan will be added to the master’s loan if the borrower has also previously taken this loan out.
S0363 The loan repayment will be calculated at 6% of income above the income threshold.
S0364 These repayments will occur alongside any outstanding undergraduate student loan repayments.
S0365 17.
S0366 The Statutory Repayment Due Date (SRDD) for doctoral loan balances for courses up to 4 years in length will be 6 April following the earlier of the course end date, or the withdrawal date when the student withdraws from the course.
S0367 Full details of repayment terms will be published in advance of the loans opening for applications, including the SRDD for students on courses over 4 years in duration.
S0368 18.
S0369 Any outstanding doctoral loan balance will be written off 30 years after the date the borrower’s loan balance becomes due for repayment.
S0370 19.
S0371 The doctoral loan will attract better than commercial interest rates; or match commercial interest rates with better terms.
S0372 The repayment terms will be monitored and be subject to review.
Administration of the loan
S0373 20.
S0374 The Student Loans Company will issue the doctoral loan, directly to the student.
S0375 21.
S0376 A student will not be entitled to receive subsequent tranches of the loan if they change to a course that is not eligible, or withdraw from study.
S0377 HEIs will be expected to inform the SLC if the student is no longer in attendance and when the student submits their thesis.
S0378 The student will be liable for the doctoral loan and must make repayments if they withdraw or do not complete their course.
S0379 22.
S0380 The loan will be recovered by HMRC for students who are in the UK tax system and by the SLC for individuals residing overseas.
