Introduction
S0011 The Department of Health has published for consultation a draft Statutory Instrument titled: The National Health Service Pension Scheme and Additional Voluntary Contributions (Amendment) Regulations 2017.
S0012 This instrument proposes amendments to the regulations that provide the rules for the NHS Pension Schemes in England & Wales.
S0013 There are two NHS Pension Schemes: the new reformed 2015 scheme and the older, closed scheme which is divided into the 1995 and 2008 sections.
S0014 Accordingly there are three sets of regulations under which entitlement to pension and other benefits are calculated:
S0015  The National Health Service Pension Scheme Regulations 1995 (SI 1995/300)
S0016  The National Health Service Pension Scheme Regulations 2008 (SI 2008/653)
S0017  The National Health Service Pension Scheme Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/94)
S0018 The National Health Service Pension Scheme (Transitional and Consequential Provisions) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/95) put in place transitional arrangements for members of the new 2015 scheme who have pension rights accrued in either the 1995 or 2008 section of the old NHS pension scheme.
S0019 The transitional regulations make provision for the treatment and payment of old scheme benefits during or following a period of membership of the new scheme.
S0020 They also include protections permitting members close to normal pension age to remain in the old scheme.
S0021 In summary, the draft instrument amends the scheme regulations for the following main purposes:
S0022  to support the development and adoption of new care models as articulated in the Five Year Forward View by  ensuring income from new types of contracts relating to new ways of delivering primary and secondary care are pensionable;  enabling independent providers of clinical health care services for the NHS who only hold an NHS standard sub-contract or a Multispecialty Community Provider standard sub-contract to apply to become NHS Pension Scheme employing authorities;
S0023  to make technical corrections and refinements to improve the operation of scheme regulations.
S0024 This document explains the purpose and effect of the provisions set out in the draft instrument.
S0025 It should be read in conjunction with the draft Statutory Instrument which is available at www.gov.uk/dh **(Link: 3) (policy area: National Health Service).
S0026 The Department welcomes any comments or views on the proposals set out in this document and the draft regulations.

1. New Care Models
S0037 1.1.
S0038 NHS England’s Five Year Forward View (5YFV), published in October 2014, outlined new models of delivering care services which transcend traditional boundaries, in particular to integrate out-of-hospital care. ** (Reference: 2)
S0039 1.2.
S0040 The development of differing forms of care delivery and the changes in contracting arrangements emerging from new models of care has led to the identification of specific issues surrounding access to the NHS pension scheme.
S0041 In order to ensure that work that is currently pensionable remains so for those delivering NHS services, amendments need to be made to scheme regulations to recognise new forms of contracts which will be in use from April 2017.
S0042 Multispecialty Community Providers 1.3.
S0043 NHS England published in July 2016 a document entitled “The Multispecialty Community Provider (MCP) emerging care model and contract framework”. That set out the vision of MCPs being about integration; redesigning care around the health of the population and breaking down the historical partitions between primary, community, mental health and social care and acute services. ** (Reference: 3)
S0044 1.4.
S0045 Key to delivering MCPs is the introduction of a new form of contract which will be used to commission a mix of health and care services.
S0046 At the time of commencing this consultation NHS England have not yet published the draft contract for consultation, so it is not clear what the name of the new contract will be.
S0047 For the purposes of this consultation, we have used the term “MCP standard contract” in the draft Statutory Instrument to demonstrate how and where this new contract will be introduced to the regulations.
S0048 1.5.
S0049 The intention is that the MCP contract becomes a contract which is pensionable.
S0050 It is therefore reflected in the sections of the regulations which relate to groups of staff whose eligibility for the scheme arises from the work they do and the services they deliver in relation to certain contracts.
S0051 These include GP practice staff, practitioners (GPs) and independent providers.
S0052 Amending regulations 3, 6, 10, 18, 19, 20(2) and (5), 38, 42(4) and 44 add the MCP contract to the interpretation/definitions sections of the three sets of regulations respectively.
S0053 1.6.
S0054 The MCP contract is also introduced to the independent provider (IP) sections of the regulations as a new form of “qualifying contract” via amending regulations 3(5), 10(6) and 35(2).
S0055 This adds a further contract to the three existing contracts which have been deemed “qualifying contracts” since the introduction of the IP concept to the NHS Pension Scheme regulations in April 2014.
S0056 This means that new MCPs holding an MCP contract will, from 1 April 2017, be able to apply to become an NHS pension scheme IP – which confers employing authority (EA) status and allows the organisation to offer the NHS pension scheme to its eligible employees.
S0057 1.7.
S0058 A change to scheme regulations last April, which was retrospective to 1 April 2015, allows IP status to be granted from a retrospective date, meaning that there should be no gap in pensionable service for those employees of the MCP who are eligible for scheme membership once IP status is granted.
S0059 Sub-contracting 1.8.
S0060 An amendment to scheme regulations last April permitted income from a specified sub- contract to be pensioned by those EAs that qualify via the contracts that they hold (such as IPs and GP practices).
S0061 That sub-contract was the NHS standard sub- contract. For income to be pensionable, it was also a requirement that the holder of the main NHS standard contract (from which the sub-contract flowed) was an EA. ** (Reference: 4)
S0062 1.9.
S0063 Special Health Authorities, NHS Trusts, Foundation Trusts and Clinical Commissioning Groups, for example, qualify as EAs because they are qualifying statutory bodies, which means that the nature of their contracting arrangements are not relevant to their status as EAs.
S0064 1.10.
S0065 The Department continues to monitor the fact that sub-contracting is becoming more prevalent as new models of delivering care emerge.
S0066 It is the Department and NHS England’s intention that the MCP contract is supplemented by a standard sub-contract, in the same way that the NHS standard contract has been.
S0067 For the same reasons established in the 2015 consultation on introducing the NHS standard sub-contract into scheme regulations, the Department intends that an MCP standard sub-contract is introduced. ** (Reference: 5)
S0068 1.11.
S0069 It is crucial that, when extending scheme membership in this way, appropriate checks and controls are implemented in order to limit additional scheme liabilities.
S0070 A form of sub-contract that complies with wider contracting requirements is such an appropriate control.
S0071 It also allows the scheme administrator to reliably validate eligible sub- contracted pensionable earnings when necessary and ensures consistency and legal certainty.
S0072 1.12.
S0073 Requiring use of the standard sub-contract ensures that only NHS work is pensioned, and scheme liabilities are appropriately controlled.
S0074 This sub-contract will give providers access to a sub-contract that follows the format of the MCP standard contract.
S0075 This allows for a clear line of sight for the funding of NHS clinical services to flow through the MCP standard contract down to the sub-contract.
S0076 1.13.
S0077 Ensuring that the holder of the main contract is also an EA allows checks and oversight of sub-contracting to be undertaken by the scheme administrator (NHS Business Services Authority – NHS BSA) via the annual returns that practitioners and IPs must complete and return, by law, to NHS BSA after the end of each pension scheme year.
S0078 1.14.
S0079 Amending regulation 7(4) provides for a consequential amendment to the 1995 section regulations which set out the requirements for annual returns to be provided by IPs.
S0080 This is needed as a result of allowing sub-contracts to be pensionable, and extends the requirement of the annual return to include recognised sub-contracts and to identify the sub-contractor.
S0081 Draft amending regulations 17 and 37 make the same required addition to the 2008 and 2015 regulations respectively.
S0082 Sub-contracts as qualifying contracts for IPs 1.15.
S0083 In a significant change to NHS pension scheme policy, recognising that sub-contracting is becoming more prevalent amongst providers of clinical NHS services, it has been decided to permit two mandated types of sub-contracts to be “qualifying contracts” in relation to independent provider access to the scheme.
S0084 1.16.
S0085 Since the introduction of IP access to the scheme from 1 April 2014 there have been three forms of qualifying contract upon which EA status could be granted.
S0086 The IP regulations will be amended from 1 April 2017 to also include two sub-contracts to the list of qualifying contracts.
S0087 1.17.
S0088 The two new qualifying contracts will be the NHS standard sub-contract and the MCP standard sub-contract.
S0089 This amendment will permit organisations not already EAs who only hold one (or both) types of sub-contracts to apply to become an IP.
S0090 All of the existing regulations around IPs will apply equally to the new IPs which are solely sub- contractors (including, for example, the wholly or mainly requirement and that only a maximum of 75% of the contract value should be pensionable).
S0091 1.18.
S0092 Following the access review conducted throughout 2015, taking account of the first year of IP access to the NHS pension scheme, it became very clear that NHS clinical service sub-contractors were keen to offer the NHS pension scheme to their eligible employees.
S0093 It was felt that not being able to offer scheme membership was a continuing factor in a playing field that remained unequal to all.
S0094 1.19.
S0095 The Department listened carefully to the arguments and, in the context of growing sub- contracting as a result of new care models, proposes a limited extension of IP access to sub-contractors.
S0096 This is on the basis of continuing the checks and balances that had been introduced from April 2016 when sub-contracts were first included in the regulations.
S0097 1.20.
S0098 Those checks and balances include only permitting prescribed forms of sub-contracts; ensuring that the holder of the main contract is also a scheme EA; and also requiring declaration of all relevant sub-contracts in IP end of year returns.
2. National Minimum Wage
S0099 2.1.
S0100 Regulation D3 “Further Contributions by employing authorities in respect of excessive pay increases” was inserted into the NHS Pension Scheme 1995 section regulations from 1 April 2014.
S0101 2.2.
S0102 It was designed to levy charges on employers awarding excessive pay increases to members of the 1995 Section within the final pay period used for the calculation of benefits (in the 1995 section, the best of the final 3 years’ pay).
S0103 2.3.
S0104 It has been brought to the Department’s attention that recent legislative changes relating to the national minimum wage have the potential to engage the provisions of regulation D3.
S0105 This is clearly not within the spirit or intent of the rule.
S0106 2.4.
S0107 The Department has therefore decided to specifically exclude pay increases which could be deemed excessive where the sole reason for that pay increase relate to changes to the national minimum wage.
S0108 2.5.
S0109 Amending regulation 4 inserts a new paragraph into regulation D3 to achieve this.
S0110 This amendment is retrospective to 1 April 2016 to ensure any recent changes relating to the national minimum wage are ignored for the purposes of regulation D3.

PART 7
S0554 Miscellaneous Option to persons detrimentally affected by these Regulations 53.—(1)
S0555 This regulation applies in relation to any benefit which is being paid or may become payable under the regulations amended by these Regulations to or in respect of a person who— (a) served in an employment or office which qualified the person to participate in the benefits provided under the regulations amended by these Regulations, and (b) ceased to serve in that employment or office before these Regulations came into force.
S0556 (2) Where, in a case to which this regulation applies, any provision of these Regulations would operate in relation to any person so as to place that person in a worse position than that person would have been if that provision had not applied, that person may elect that the provision shall not apply by giving notice in accordance with paragraph (3).
S0557 (3) A notice given pursuant to paragraph (2) shall be in writing and shall be delivered to the Secretary of State within six months of the coming into force of these Regulations or such longer period as the Secretary of State may allow.
S0558 (4) An election pursuant to paragraph (2) shall have effect in relation to the benefit referred to in paragraph (1) only to the extent that such benefit has accrued by virtue of periods of service rendered prior to the cessation referred to in paragraph (1) (or, if there has been more than one such cessation, the last of them before the coming into force of these Regulations) and in determining entitlement to, and the amount of, the benefit to that extent, such person shall be treated as if that person had never recommenced service at any time after that cessation (or, as the case may be, the last such cessation).
