Foreword
S0011 Britain’s roads are among the safest in the world and ensuring that they remain so is a key objective in the Government’s approach to regulating road transport.
S0012 Safe vehicles are an essential part of achieving that goal.
S0013 Identifying and fixing defective tyres, brakes or lighting can make the difference between life and death on our roads.
S0014 MOT testing is a key part of maintaining safer vehicles.
S0015 It is important that our MOT testing regime is well designed to keep dangerous vehicles off the road.
S0016 It is also important to recognise that there are cases when mandating such testing is inappropriate, for example in the case of vehicles of historical interest (also known as classic vehicles).
S0017 A number of people in Great Britain gain significant enjoyment from driving older vehicles and such vehicles add to the diversity on our roads.
S0018 It is important that they can continue to enjoy this activity.
S0019 It is also important that these vehicles are safe and appropriately maintained, especially as many older vehicles lack appropriate safety features, to ensure the risk to road safety is reduced.
S0020 Under current GB and EU law all vehicles that were manufactured before 1960 are exempted from compulsory annual roadworthiness testing.
S0021 A new European Roadworthiness Directive makes changes to the existing vehicle testing scheme, including the provisions for exempting vehicles of historical interest.
S0022 Implementing these requirements will allow Great Britain to reduce regulation in this area.
S0023 The Directive still enables exemptions from regular testing, and in respect of vehicles of historical interest, it applies to vehicles manufactured or registered for the first time at least 30 years ago, which are no longer in production and have not been substantially changed.
S0024 Our preference is to exempt vehicles manufactured or registered at least 40 years ago.
S0025 The 40 year old vehicle option is also in line with the current rolling 40 year exemptions from Vehicle Excise Duty so this will ensure greater consistency.
S0026 Throughout the proposals in this document the Government has been keen to avoid placing unnecessary burdens on businesses as well as individuals who want to make use of these vehicles.
S0027 We believe the proposals are sensible for keeping our roads safe for all users and strike the right balance.
S0028 On 23 June, the EU referendum took place and the people of the United Kingdom voted to leave the European Union.
S0029 Until exit negotiations are concluded, the UK remains a full member of the European Union and all the rights and obligations of EU membership remain in force.
S0030 During this period the Government will continue to negotiate, implement and apply EU legislation.
S0031 The outcome of these negotiations will determine what arrangements apply in relation to EU legislation in the future once the UK has left the EU.
S0032 Responses to this consultation will help inform our final proposals before we make changes to domestic legislation.
S0033 It will also assist us in refining the assessment of the impacts.
S0034 I encourage you to reflect on the proposals set out here and to respond to the consultations questions in full.

Executive summary
S0036 1.1 This document sets out the Government’s proposals for implementing new rules around the testing of vehicles of historical interest (VHIs), in accordance with the Directive 2014/45/EU (“the Directive”).
S0037 The Directive sets new rules on periodic roadworthiness testing for vehicles and their trailers and repeals the old EU Directive 2009/40/EC.
S0038 During our considerations relating to the implementation of the Directive, we have taken advantage of the opportunity to identify whether we can de-regulate current legislation, to increase the number of individuals who may be able to benefit from exemptions from the MOT regime.
S0039 1.2
S0040 The proposals in this consultation apply to Great Britain (given as vehicle testing is a devolved matter in Northern Ireland).
S0041 Northern Ireland will develop its own proposals for implementation of the Directive.
S0042 There is a separate consultation being published in parallel, which focuses on the testing of “fast tractors” (i.e. T5 category vehicles).
S0043 1.3 Under existing GB and EU law all vehicles manufactured before 1960 are exempted from compulsory annual roadworthiness testing.
S0044 The Directive also exempts vehicles from regular testing, but now vehicles must be manufactured or registered at least 30 years ago, must no longer be in production and must not be substantially changed.
S0045 This ensures that the vehicles benefiting from MOT exemptions are actually in the original condition they purport to be.
S0046 This is a useful amendment which aims to ensure that people who drive vehicles which have been significantly changed cannot profit from an exemption.
S0047 1.4
S0048 In 2014, the Department for Transport (“the Department”) conducted an informal web based consultation, which sought opinions and ideas on which we could base our proposals for formal consultation.
S0049 A wide range of views were expressed in the responses, which assisted us in narrowing the options to those being considered here.
S0050 1.5 Although the Directive allows Member States to exempt from testing 30 year old vehicles, our preferred option is to exempt 40 year old vehicles.
S0051 This is mainly for safety reasons as the Impact Assessment shows that vehicles first registered between 1978 and 1987 (broadly 30-40 years old when the regulatory changes need to be made) fail 33.7% of MOT tests.
S0052 For vehicles registered between 1960 and 1977 the failure rate is 25.3% and for pre-1960 vehicles is 14.8%.
S0053 There are also approximately twice as many vehicles in the 1978-87 age group involved in personal injury road accidents compared with 1960-1977 vehicles.
S0054 The 40 year old vehicle option is also in line with the current rolling 40 year exemptions from VED.
S0055 1.6
S0056 We will also have to introduce a definition for ‘substantial change’ to allow exemptions from testing as there is no definition in the Directive.
S0057 One option is to use the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency’s 8-point system for registering radically altered vehicles.
S0058 This system can be found on the Gov.uk website.
S0059 1.7 Another option being considered is for VHIs to undertake a basic ‘VHI’ roadworthiness ‘safety’ test, where existing MOT garages would check vehicle identity, brakes, steering, tyres and lights as a minimum.
S0060 1.8
S0061 We anticipate that the majority of VHIs used for business purposes would be sent for an annual MOT even if they were exempted to help ensure they are in a roadworthy condition and to keep insurance premiums at a minimum.
S0062 1.9 We don’t believe that the proposals we are putting forward will place excessive burdens on businesses and overall will reduce regulation in Great Britain.

The proposals
S0076 1.1 The Directive sets out minimum requirements for periodic roadworthiness testing of vehicles used on public roads.
S0077 Vehicles are categorised by type and the rules differ for each category.
S0078 Cars and vans must be tested at least biennially from 4 years old, unless exempted.
S0079 Heavy goods vehicles, buses and coaches that are not exempted must be tested annually from new.
S0080 1.2 On 23 June, the EU referendum took place and the people of the United Kingdom voted to leave the European Union.
S0081 Until exit negotiations are concluded, the UK remains a full member of the European Union and all the rights and obligations of EU membership remain in force.
S0082 During this period the Government will continue to negotiate, implement and apply EU legislation.
S0083 The outcome of these negotiations will determine what arrangements apply in relation to EU legislation in the future once the UK has left the EU.
S0084 1.3 Under current GB and EU law all vehicles that were manufactured before 1960 are exempted from compulsory annual roadworthiness testing.
S0085 The new EU Directive still enables us to allow exemptions from regular testing, but now vehicles must be manufactured at least 30 years ago, must no longer be in production and must not been substantially changed.
S0086 1.4 If we wish to continue to exempt VHIs we will need to amend domestic law to implement the requirements of the Directive.
S0087 We can also take advantage of the opportunity to exempt some post- 1960 vehicles from testing.
S0088 1.5 Under the Directive, VHIs (described in paragraph 1.3 above) can be exempted from regular testing in Great Britain if we opt to do so.
S0089 The deadline for implementing the requirements of the Directive is 20 May 2018.
S0090 1.6
S0091 Although motorcycles and 3-wheeled vehicles do not come into the scope of the Directive until 1 January 2022 we are including these categories of vehicles in this consultation.
S0092 (Any changes affecting these vehicles will not come into force until 1 January 2022.)
S0093 1.7 If we wish to continue to exempt VHIs, Great Britain can decide how old vehicles should be before they are exempted from testing and how to define ‘substantial change’, provided the vehicles are at least 30 years of age.
S0094 1.8 The Directive also refers to VHIs as being ‘hardly used on public roads’, and we wish to consider whether we should restrict the annual mileage for VHIs exempted from testing.
S0095 Many insurers include a mileage limit in policies for VHIs as a way of reducing premiums.

Evidence Base (for summary sheets) 1) Problem under consideration
S0350 New EU Directive 2014/45 (“the Directive”) sets out minimum requirements for periodic road-worthiness testing of vehicles used on public roads.
S0351 Vehicles are categorised by type and the rules differ for each category.
S0352 Cars and vans must be tested at least biennially, unless exempted.
S0353 Heavy goods vehicles, buses and coaches that are not exempted must be tested annually.
S0354 On 23 June, the EU referendum took place and the people of the United Kingdom voted to leave the European Union.
S0355 Until exit negotiations are concluded, the UK remains a full member of the European Union and all the rights and obligations of EU membership remain in force.
S0356 During this period the Government will continue to negotiate, implement and apply EU legislation.
S0357 The outcome of these negotiations will determine what arrangements apply in relation to EU legislation in the future once the UK has left the EU.
S0358 Under current GB and EU law all vehicles that were manufactured or registered before 1960 can be exempted from periodic testing.
S0359 The new EU directive still enables exemptions from regular testing, but vehicles must be at least 30 years old, no longer in production and should not be substantially changed.
S0360 If we wish to continue to exempt VHIs we will need to implement EU requirements and amend GB law.
S0361 The Road Traffic Act 1988 provides the legislative basis for MOT testing of cars, other light vehicles (including some light goods vehicles), private buses/coaches, and motorcycles.
S0362 Heavy Goods Vehicles are required to have a statutory roadworthiness test under the Goods Vehicles (Plating and Testing) Regulations 1988.
S0363 If we continue to exempt VHIs from testing, we can decide how old they should be (once they are at least 30 years old and no longer in production) before they’re exempted from testing.
S0364 We will also have to decide how to define ‘substantial change’.
S0365 The consultation will cover how “substantial change” is defined.
S0366 The Directive does not contain a definition.
S0367 We suggest one option is to use DVLA’s 8-point rule for registering radically altered vehicles.
S0368 We have no evidence available to indicate how many vehicles would be in scope for this option but will ask for comments in the consultation document and for any suggestions on the number of vehicles that might be affected.
S0369 In 2014 the Department for Transport (“the Department”) held an informal web based consultation seeking opinions and ideas on which we could base our proposals for formal consultation.
S0370 The informal consultation generated 650 comments and over 2800 survey responses.
S0371 There was a wide range of views expressed in the responses, which assisted in narrowing numerous options to those being considered here.
S0372 2) Rationale for intervention At the moment GB legislation simply allows all vehicles manufactured before 1960 to be exempt from roadworthiness testing.
S0373 There are no additional requirements or criteria to be met.
S0374 The ‘substantial change’ provision in the Directive means we will need to amend GB legislation to implement the requirements of the Directive, to continue to exempt VHIs from MOT testing.
S0375 Our preferred option goes beyond the EU minimum requirement, which would be to exempt vehicles over 30 years old from MOT testing so there is an element of gold plating.
S0376 Primarily, the MOT test failure rate is higher for vehicles which were manufactured or registered between 30 to 40 years ago, than for vehicles which were manufactured or registered 40 years ago.
S0377 Our modelling is limited by available data linking accident rates to vehicle vintage, but we think the safety risks of exempting these older vehicles are higher than our modelling suggests.
S0378 We will seek further information about this at consultation.
S0379 Secondly, 40 years is the threshold for exempting vehicles for Vehicle Excise Duty.
S0380 3) Policy objective To make changes to the VHI exemption in domestic legislation to implement the requirements of the Directive, whilst minimising burdens on owners of VHIs and ensuring any changes do not have the potential to adversely impact on road safety.

S0518 The main cost of the preferred option is the extent to which untested vehicles will cause a rise in accidents.
S0519 This is very hard to estimate with any certainty.
S0520 Relatively few people are killed or seriously injured by classic vehicles over the course of a year which makes a meaningful link between mechanical defects and the number of accidents affected hard to quantify with high levels of analytical assurance.
S0521 It is also hard to know how people will behave without the requirement to test their vehicles in a counterfactual scenario.
