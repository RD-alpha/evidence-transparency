1. Summary of proposals
S0043 1.1 Summary
S0044 1.1.1 This chapter provides an overview of the wider reforms of local government finance which will help provide context to the proposals for the 2017/18 settlement:
S0045  it provides background information regarding the ongoing reforms to business rates retention and
S0046  it outlines the key themes that the Government is proposing for the 2017/18 local government finance settlement.
S0047 1.2 Background
S0048 1.2.1 The funding available to councils to deliver their core services for the rest of this Parliament is broadly flat, in cash terms.
S0049 By then, local government will retain 100% of taxes raised locally.
S0050 This will give local government additional business rates receipts of around £12.5bn to spend on local services.
S0051 The system will have stronger incentives to boost growth, and areas that take bold decisions to boost growth will see the benefits.
S0052 In order to ensure that the reforms are fiscally neutral, councils will gain new responsibilities, and some Whitehall grants will be phased out.
S0053 1.2.2
S0054 These reforms represent a unique opportunity to fundamentally change the role of local government and the way it is funded.
S0055 The move towards self-sufficiency and away from dependence on central government is something that councils have been calling for over a number of decades.
S0056 The historic 2016/17 local government finance settlement was a first step along this road.
S0057 It offers those local authorities who are committed to reform far greater certainty over their future funding.

S0108 2.3 Distribution of the improved Better Care Fund 2.3. ** (Reference: 1)
S0109 The Spending Review 2015 announced the introduction of the improved Better Care Fund worth £105 million in 2017/18, £800 million in 2018/19 and £1.5 billion in 2019/20.
S0110 2.3.2
S0111 The Government set out its proposed approach to allocating the improved Better Care Fund allocations alongside the provisional Local Government Finance Settlement 2016/17 and committed to consult on the distribution of the fund in due course.
S0112 2.3.3 Having carefully considered its approach and the views received in response to the consultation on the settlement, the Government proposes to maintain the approach for 2017/18 set out in chapter 5 of the consultation on the provisional 2016/17 local government finance settlement published on 17 December 2015. This approach recognises that authorities have varying capacity to raise council tax, and will ** (Reference: 3)
S0113 allocate the funding through a separate grant to local government, using a methodology which provides more funding to those authorities which benefit less from the adult social care council tax precept.
S0114 2.3.4 The proposed methodology for each financial year is as follows:
S0115 i. We calculate the additional funding available to spend on adult social care at a national level, combining the 2% council tax flexibility for adult social care and the additional funding for the improved Better Care Fund.
S0116 ii.
S0117 We then calculate the share of that national amount which each authority with responsibility for social care would receive if it were distributed according to the 2013 adult social care relative needs formula.
S0118 iii.
S0119 We then calculate how much each authority with responsibility for social care could raise from the additional 2% council tax flexibility for adult social care.
S0120 iv.
S0121 The additional funding for the improved Better Care Fund is then allocated in such a way that, when combined with the money which could be raised from the council tax flexibility, each council would receive its share of the combined national amount as calculated in step (ii) above.
S0122 v. These allocations are adjusted so that, where an authority could raise more from the additional council tax flexibility for social care than its share of the national amount calculated in step (ii), its allocation for the improved Better Care Fund is set to zero rather than a notional negative figure.
S0123 vi.
S0124 The remainder of the allocations are then reduced proportionately, so that the combined totals sum to the national total for additional funding available to spend on adult social care, as calculated in step (i).
S0125 2.3.5 The resulting illustrative proposed allocations of the improved Better Care Fund by local authority can be found at https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/core- **(Link: 9) spending-power-final-local-government-finance-settlement-2016-to-2017 **(Link: 10)

S0178 3.4
S0179 The business rates revaluation adjustment
S0180 3.4.1 The next business rates revaluation takes effect from 1 April 2017.
S0181 Revaluation is a revenue neutral exercise so the total rates bill will stay the same at the England level in real terms, after allowing for appeals.
S0182 At the local authority level, overall bills will increase or fall depending upon whether rateable values in that area have performed above or below the average for England, after allowing for appeals.
S0183 3.4.2 This creates change in the system outside the control of local authorities.
S0184 When the Government introduced the 50% business rate retention scheme it signalled that it would adjust each authority’s tariff or top up following a revaluation to ensure, as far as is practicable, that their retained income is the same after revaluation as immediately before.
S0185 This will ensure that the growth incentive created by the rates retention scheme and the delivery of public services will not be weakened by losses of income outside the control of authorities.
S0186 The following section describes how we will implement this commitment.
S0187 The adjustment for the revaluation
S0188 3.4.3 For a local authority, the measure of rates income in the rates retention scheme is its share of “non-domestic rating income” as defined in regulations and captured on the NNDR3 form.
S0189 Therefore, the objective of the revaluation adjustment is to identify and isolate the amount by which non-domestic rating income in the authority will change purely due to the revaluation.
S0190 Once identified, the relevant shares of these amounts can then be deducted or added to the tariffs or top ups to cancel out the impact of revaluation.
S0191 3.4.4 For example:
S0192 An authority sees its local share of non-domestic rating income grow due to revaluation from £100m to £122m.
S0193 It has a £20m top up.
S0194 With no adjustment, the £22m increase would feed through into extra revenue for the authority.
S0195 To compensate we need to deduct £22m from the £20m top up giving a tariff of £2m.
S0196 3.4.5
S0197 In practice we do not believe it will be possible to directly measure the changes in non-domestic rating income arising from the revaluation alone.
S0198 Instead we propose to estimate the change using a proxy (derived from gross rates payable).
S0199 The proxy will be calculated by comparing the local authority’s rateable value before and after the revaluation to quantify the impact the revaluation has on the tax base.
S0200 This effect will then be applied to the authority’s gross business rates income before the revaluation to produce a figure for the council’s income post-revaluation.
S0201 Subtracting this post-revaluation income figure from the pre-revaluation income and apportioning it according to the authority’s share of business rates income under the scheme will produce the change that needs to be made to its tariff or top up in order to ensure that it has, as far as practicable, the same income after the revaluation as it had before.
S0202 3.4.6 We propose to make this adjustment in three stages: on a provisional basis in the 2017-18 settlement; on a final basis with a reconciliation in the 2018-19 settlement; and finally, by cancelling out the reconciliation in the 2019-20 settlement.
S0203 3.4.7
S0204 Annex A provides more detailed information about the calculations involved in the proposed methodology for the revaluation adjustment.
S0205 Appeals against the 2017 rating list
S0206 3.4.8 The adjustment for revaluation is for those impacts discernable at the time of the revaluation only (and captured in the rateable values on 31/3/17 and 1/4/17 as we look at them on that day).
S0207 Changes to the revaluation which occur after 1 April
S0208 2017 by virtue of backdated amendments or appeals (including those backdated to 1/4/17) fall within the operation of the rates retention system in the normal way.

S0210 3.5 Adjustments to business rates in areas piloting 100% business rates retention
S0211 3.5.1
S0212 At the 2015 Autumn Statement the Government committed to piloting approaches to 100% business rates retention in London, Manchester and Liverpool from 1 April 2017.
S0213 3.5.2 The Government also committed that the pilots’ offer would be available to other areas with ratified devolution deals and that as part of the pilots, the “local share” of business rates could be increased from as early as 2017-18.
S0214 3.5.3 To ensure that an increase in the “local share” of business rates is fiscally neutral at the point of change, the Government and pilot areas are exploring:
S0215  ending entitlement to certain grants and other funding streams  devolving additional responsibilities to pilot areas and  adjusting existing business rate tariffs and top ups.
S0216 3.5.4 The Government intends to use the pilots to test mechanisms for full rollout of the 100% retention scheme.
S0217 Any cost to the system from elements of the pilots will not impact on non-pilot authorities.
S0218 Methodology for calculating the additional local share in pilot areas 3.5.5
S0219 The calculation of the value of an additional local share will be based on the pilot areas’ Baseline Funding Levels and notional Business Rates Baselines (i.e. the 2013/14 Business Rates Baseline, uprated by subsequent changes to the small business rates multiplier).
S0220 This will preserve the integrity of the existing scheme by ensuring that the value of the additional share is exclusive of any growth (or decline) in business rates achieved by pilot authorities since 2013-14.
S0221 3.5.6 For each pilot authority, the value of funding streams and the new responsibilities rolled in to the business rates retention system (hereinafter referred to as “Grant”) will be added to the existing Baseline Funding Level to create a “new” Baseline Funding Level for the authority.
S0222 3.5.7 There are two different options on offer to pilot authorities in 2017-18.
S0223 Either:
S0224 a) the local percentage share of business rates is increased only by the value of the “Grant” rolled-in or
S0225 b) the local percentage share of business rates is increased to 100% and, to the extent that the “Grant” rolled in is not equivalent to 100% of local business rates, tariffs and top ups are adjusted appropriately.
S0226 3.5.8 Under option a) above, the additional percentage share of business rates will be equal to:
S0227 Grant / notional Business Rates Baseline x existing % share of business rates
S0228 3.5.9 This additional percentage share will be added to the existing percentage share to give the percentage to be retained in 2017/18.
S0229 3.5.10 Under option b) above, the notional Business Rates Baseline of each authority will be adjusted to reflect the increase in the local share to 100%. ** (Reference: 4)
S0230 The adjustment to the notional Business Rates Baseline is equal to:
S0231 Business Rates Baseline / existing % share of business rates x 100%
S0232 3.5.11 For option B, the difference between the new Baseline Funding Level and the new notional Business Rates Baseline will be the tariff or top up for 2017-18.
S0233 Calculation of Baseline Funding Levels and Tariffs and Top ups 3.5.12 Baseline Funding Level and business rate tariff and top up figures were set as part of the Local Government Finance Settlement in February 2016 using OBR estimates of the Retail Price Index (RPI) as a proxy for the changes in the small business rating multiplier.
S0234 The actual multiplier for 2017-18 will be set once September 2016’s RPI is published.
S0235 At the 2017-18 Settlement, Government will also update Baseline Funding Levels and tariffs/top ups for later years based on up- to-date estimates of RPI.
S0236 3.5.13 If it is necessary to make any agreed changes to amounts of Revenue Support Grant, the retained local share or tariffs and top ups for 2017-18, these will be made after the change in the small business rating multiplier are known.
S0237 Whilst we will publish indicative figures for later years, it is the Government’s intention to recalculate the value of the local share (and the possible knock-on consequences for tariffs and top ups) for future Local Government Finance Settlements based on the actual change to the small business rating multiplier for those years.

Annex A: 50% Business Rates Retention: Methodology for adjusting for the 2017 revaluation
S0259 B.1.
S0260 In order to ensure, as far as is practicable, the impact of the 2017 revaluation is neutralised in the rates retention scheme, DCLG propose to make the following adjustments to tariffs and top ups.
S0261 This will be in addition to the normal inflation adjustments made to tariffs and top ups.
S0262 B.2.
S0263 Ideally, we would directly measure the change in non-domestic rating income between 31/3/17 and 1/4/17 (the 2 days either side of the revaluation).
S0264 However:
S0265  being the last day of the financial year and a Friday there could be many alterations on 31 March 2017 with retrospective effect (typically appeals credited on that day).
S0266 The authority would, therefore, need to take a view as to how much of their provision they have released to fund those appeals and how much actually reduced non-domestic rates income for that day
S0267  we would need to consider whether changes to the provisions for the year should also be allocated to the income for the day and if so how
S0268  there will be other accounting adjustments in income which would need to be reflected for the day – such as bad debt.
S0269 B.3.
S0270 In practice we believe this would be too complicated and too sensitive to refunds, backdated alterations and subjective allocations of accounting adjustments.
S0271 Instead the Government will use a proxy to measure the change in rates income due to the revaluation.
S0272 We will then apply that proxy (as a factor) to the non- domestic rating income for 2016/17.
S0273 That would then give an estimate of the change in in non-domestic rating income between 31/3/17 and 1/4/17.
S0274 Choice of a proxy for revaluation change B.4.
S0275 As a proxy, we propose to adopt the change in gross rates payable before all reliefs and accounting adjustments between 31 March 2017 and 1 April 2017.
S0276 Essentially, this is just the rateable value x small business multiplier for those 2 days.
S0277 B.5.
S0278 This proxy will not itself reflect changes in reliefs such as Small Business Rate Relief (SBRR).
S0279 However, the proxy will be applied to non-domestic rating income which will already reflect the degree to which local income is affected by reliefs.
S0280 So the adjustment will, by adopting non-domestic rating income, reflect the position of those authorities with a lot of SBRR.
S0281 B.6.
S0282 Where the proxy could be less accurate is where reliefs in a local area change due to the revaluation out of line with the proxy for all properties in the area.
S0283 Many percentage awards of reliefs will not change due to the revaluation (e.g. charitable
S0284 relief) as their eligibility and percentage is not driven by rateable value - so for them the proxy should work as well as it does for any property.
S0285 And some have little impact (e.g. the empty property relief lower threshold) and some reliefs are insignificant (rural rate relief).
S0286 B.7.
S0287 In principle, there is more scope for the revaluation factor for small properties receiving SBRR to be different to all properties.
S0288 However:
S0289  it is not possible to say what that revaluation factor for SBRR properties would have been.
S0290 The Government has made large changes to the eligibility of SBRR for 1 April 2017.
S0291 Had the Government not done anything to SBRR we would in any case have adjusted the thresholds for the relief and the Small Business (SB) multiplier.
S0292 In the absence of the need for that decision we cannot isolate the effect of the Budget change to SBRR from the revaluation change  we believe the impact would still be marginal.
S0293 B.8.
S0294 Therefore, we believe adopting a proxy based on the gross change in rateable value using the Small Business multipliers is a practical and proportionate method.
S0295 However, we will keep this under review for the final adjustment in the 2018/19 settlement (see below).
S0296 We will also separately pay section 31 for the SBRR changes in the 2016 Budget including the increase in the threshold for the SB multiplier and will consider that payment in the context of the revaluation adjustment.
S0297 Provisional and Final adjustments B.9.
S0298 We will not have actual 2016/17 non-domestic rates income or rateable values at 1 April 2017 in time for the 2017/18 settlement.
S0299 Therefore, we propose to make the revaluation adjustment in 3 stages:
S0300  provisional 2017/18 top ups and tariffs will be calculated in the autumn of 2016 based on forecasts.
S0301 This will be based on NNDR3 non-domestic rates income for 2015/16 increased in line with inflation and rateable values for the 2010 and 2017 lists available at the draft list stage (30 September 2016)
S0302  final 2017/18 top ups and tariffs will be calculated in the autumn of 2017.
S0303 The 2018/19 settlement will then include a reconciliation of the 2017/18 adjustment
S0304  in 2019/20 we will cancel the one off reconciliation adjustment for 2018/19.
S0305 Appeals and the multiplier B.10.
S0306 At the revaluation the Secretary of State is allowed, in setting the multiplier, to anticipate future appeals on both the old and new rating list.
S0307 This has the effect of increasing the multiplier so in effect we over-collect in the early years of the rating list and then under-collect in later years as the appeals start to come through with retrospective effect.
S0308 B.11.
S0309 Local government is responsible for accounting for appeals through their forecast of business rates income using proper accounting practice.
S0310 Therefore they will make a provision at the start of the 2017 rating list to reflect all expected future appeals.
S0311 To offset the effect of this provision we propose to remove from the revaluation adjustment the impact of future appeals.
S0312 We will do this by calculating the revaluation adjustment using a notional small business multiplier for 2017/18 which has not been adjusted for appeals.
S0313 B.12.
S0314 This approach will give local authorities funds to deal with volatility and ensure retained rates income and spending does not fall from 1 April 2017 due to large new provisions for the revaluation.

Annex C: Glossary of technical terms
S0368 Baseline funding level
S0369 The amount of an individual local authority’s Start-Up Funding Assessment for 2013/14 provided through the local share of the Estimated Business Rates Aggregate uprated each year by the change to the small business multiplier (in line with RPI).
S0370 Business rates baseline
S0371 Determined for individual authorities at the outset of the business rates retention scheme by dividing the local share of the Estimated Business Rates Aggregate (England) between billing authorities on the basis of their proportionate shares, before the payment of any major precepting authority share.
S0372 Central share
S0373 The percentage share of locally collected business rates that is paid to central government by billing authorities.
S0374 This is set at 50%.
S0375 The central share is re-distributed to local government through grants including the Revenue Support Grant.
S0376 This replaces the previous ’set-aside’ policy.
S0377 Local government spending control total
S0378 The total amount of expenditure for Revenue Support Grant in the Department for Communities and Local Government’s Local Government Departmental Expenditure Limit (LG DEL) plus the local share of the Estimated Business Rates Aggregate that is allocated to the local government sector by Government for each year of a Spending Review.
S0379 It does not include the resources identified in the 2013 Spending Round for social care and Troubled Families.
S0380 Local share
S0381 The percentage share of locally collected business rates that is retained by local government.
S0382 This is set at 50%.
S0383 Revenue Support Grant
S0384 Billing and most major precepting authorities receive Revenue Support Grant from central government in addition to their local share of business rates Aggregate.
S0385 An authority’s Revenue Support Grant amount plus the local share of the Estimated Business Rates Aggregate will together comprise its Settlement Funding Assessment.
S0386 Safety net
S0387 Mechanism to protect any authority which sees its business rates income drop, in any year, by more than 7.5% below their baseline funding level (with baseline funding levels being uprated by the small business rates multiplier for the purposes of assessing eligibility for support).
S0388 Start-up funding assessment
S0389 A local authority’s share of the local government spending control total which will comprise its Revenue Support Grant for the year in question and its baseline funding level.
S0390 Tariffs and top ups
S0391 Calculated by comparing at the outset of the business rate retention scheme an individual authority’s business rates baseline against its baseline funding level.
S0392 Tariffs and top ups are self-funding, fixed at the start of the scheme and index linked to RPI in future years.
S0393 Tariff authority
S0394 An authority with, at the outset of the scheme, a higher individual authority business rates baseline than its baseline funding level, and which therefore pays a tariff.
S0395 Top-up authority
S0396 An authority with, at the outset of the scheme, a lower individual authority business rates baseline than its baseline funding level, and which therefore receives a top up.
