Section 1: Introduction
Scope of the consultation
S0008 Topic of this consultation This consultation sets out proposed changes to the existing fisheries management Concordat between Defra, DAERA, Marine Scotland and the Welsh Government (“the Administrations”).
S0009 Scope of this consultation The purpose of this consultation is to seek your views on the proposed changes to existing Concordat (2012) which aim to simplify the existing Concordat and provide clarity to both fishermen and government.
S0010 Geographical scope These proposals apply to fishing businesses in the UK.
S0011 This includes fishermen, Producer Organisations and those involved in trading and leasing licences and quota.
S0012 The proposals also affect the UK Fisheries Administrations, who implement the arrangements contained in the Concordat.

Section 2: Questions and answers
S0038 What is the UK Fisheries Concordat and why are changes being proposed?
S0039 The Concordat is an agreement between all four fisheries administrations in the UK that sets out the broad principles that apply for the management of their own commercial fishing fleets.
S0040 The Concordat provides the flexibility for fisheries administrations to adopt their own management rules, whilst respecting certain overarching UK principles on quota, effort control and licencing regimes.
S0041 The four Administrations agreed, when the 2012 Concordat was adopted, that there would be a review of its operation to ensure that the Concordat remains relevant; that review has now been completed.
S0042 The review concluded that the existing Concordat contained elements which are no longer relevant in UK fisheries management and proposed changes to simplify and provide clarity to fishermen and government.
S0043 What is the impact of the UK decision to leave the EU on the Concordat?
S0044 Until exit negotiations are concluded, the UK remains a full member of the European Union and all the rights and obligations of EU membership remain in force.
S0045 During this period the Government will continue to negotiate, implement and apply EU legislation.
Details of the proposed changes
S0046 We are proposing to revise the Concordat in three main areas, and to make some additional minor changes.
S0047 The changes are about:
S0048 • Licensing and vessel nationality;
S0049 • The transfer of FQA units;
S0050 •
S0051 The economic link licence condition; and,
S0052 • A number of minor and consequential changes, to reflect changes in EU regulations and also changes in administrative procedure made since 2012.
S0053 Licensing and vessel nationality
S0054 The four UK Fisheries Administrations are agreed that fishing vessels should be able to move between Administrations within the UK.
S0055 Whether for reasons of change of ownership or simply because of a business decision to pursue different fisheries, there will be reasons why vessels may wish to operate in a different constituent part of the UK.
S0056 The revised Concordat aims to simplify the licencing process with the individual administrations being responsible for the issuing of licences for operation under its own jurisdiction e.g. England for English vessels
S0057 Vessels holding a fishing licence from one of the UK Administrations may fish from any UK port.
S0058 As a result of this, we propose to consider applications from vessels owners to change country of administration taking into account changes of fishing operations that have already taken place.
S0059 Where a vessel has moved its normal operation and is making the majority of its landings into a different Administration in the UK, we will consider an application to change country of administration.
S0060 We propose that such changes should have been sustained for a period of 12 months prior to any application to change administration and we welcome your views on this.
S0061 Where a change of fishing operations has been well established, such that the majority of landings are made into a different Administration in the UK, the Fisheries Administrations will consider applications with an assumption that applications will be approved.
S0062 We propose that the Fisheries Administrations should be permitted to insert conditions that require such changes to fishing operations to be maintained for a specified period into the future.
S0063 We propose that where such conditions are breached, the Fisheries Administrations will reserve the right to suspend the licence of the vessel in question until such time as it re- registers to the Administration where its fishing operations are in fact predominantly taking place.

S0067 3 Do you agree that operators should be required to maintain changes to their operations, in order to remain administered by the new Administration?
S0068 Do you have a view on what the appropriate period of time should be?
S0069 Economic link licence condition
S0070 The 2012 Concordat contains an economic link condition which allows for foreign access by other Member States to UK waters, where the holder of the quota has an economic link to the UK e.g. quota held by an operator based in another member state but who employs a UK crew.
S0071 As part of the development of a new fisheries policy post leaving the EU, we will be reviewing this link.
S0072 We propose removing the existing paragraph from the Concordat to allow Administrations to undertake further discussions about the effectiveness and development of the licence condition.
S0073 Administrations are agreed that it is essential that the economic link licence condition contribute to meaningful benefits for coastal communities across the UK.
S0074 Transfer of FQA units
S0075 The UK Fisheries Administrations are individually responsible for the management of UK quota, which is divided between Administrations according to the UK quota management rules.
S0076 We propose that each Administration should have power to exercise discretion about whether to approve applications for the transfer of FQA units from their licences to licences issued by other Fisheries Administrations.
S0077 This will give individual fisheries administrations greater scope to manage quota according to each administrations needs and priorities.
S0078 It will be for individual Administrations to confirm whether or not they intend to exercise such discretion, and in what circumstances.
S0079 Administrations will confirm these arrangements in their own quota management rules.
S0080 The Concordat provides that there will be assumption that transfer of units between countries will be permitted where a vessel is granted permission to move Administrations, in relation to any units on that licence and subject to controls during a transitional period.

S0083 Minor and consequential changes
S0084 In order to simplify the Concordat and ensure it reflects current realities, we propose a number of minor and consequential changes:
S0085 • References to paragraphs in the 2012 Concordat that were not relevant to domestic fisheries management;
S0086 • To reflect the agreement between the Administrations to apportion the 10 metre and under vessel allocations based on the recorded landings of vessels in the period 2008-2012.
S0087 As a consequence of this agreement, paragraphs 7d, 7e and 8 in the 2012 Concordat are no longer required and have been deleted;
S0088 •
S0089 Paragraph 7b has been revised slightly to take into account the inclusion of rules about over-fishing in UK and their impact on Producer Organisations (POs) by clarifying the process for accounting for the uptake of quota.
S0090 These rules have been agreed post 2012 and are just formalised in the Concordat;
S0091 • Paragraph 9, which is concerned with the FQA register, has been amended to reflect the establishment and operation of the register; and,
S0092 • Paragraph 13 has been amended to refer to the new CMO regulation.

S0114 Licensing In the United Kingdom
S0115 1.
S0116 This Concordat contains a framework for enabling the four UK Fisheries Administrations to be given a greater degree of control over the management of their own commercial fishing fleets, within a UK wide quota and effort management and licensing system.
S0117 It replaces any licensing and quota management arrangements contained in existing Concordats, in particular paragraphs 4 to 8 of the 1999 MAFF/Scottish Executive Fisheries Concordat and paragraph 55 of the Main MAFF/National Assembly for Wales Concordat.
S0118 The arrangements will be subject to formal annual review between the Administrations as well as in the light of any changes arising from changes to the CFP rules and regulations.
S0119 2.
S0120 As part of this Concordat all Administrations agree to the following broad principles:
S0121 a. The UK remains a member of the EU until our withdrawal is completed.
S0122 All Administrations have a duty to respect any existing EU quantitative obligations accordingly and if they fail to do so will be responsible for any penalties payable in accordance with the provisions of the Control Regulation;
S0123 b. Allocations of fishing opportunities to Administrations do not constitute
S0124 a permanent split of UK opportunities; c. Administrations may impose their own licence conditions, subject to
S0125 these not discriminating unfairly against vessels from the other Administrations; d. Vessels, Fixed Quota Allocation (FQA) units and licences will remain
S0126 free to move between Administrations, subject to the arrangements described in paragraphs 10 and 11; e. Administrations will only issue licences to vessels registered in and
S0127 administered from ports in their territory; and f. There will be no new restrictions on PO membership.
S0128 Licensing and vessel nationality
S0129 3.
S0130 The nationality of a vessel is determined by the port at which it is registered, however the allocation of a PLN by the RSS is not a guarantee that the vessel will be granted a licence to fish by the Administration in which that port is located, or by any Administration.
S0131 Administrations agree that the issue of fishing vessel licences will be carried out in the manner described below:
S0132 a. Administrations will, in general, only issue licences to vessels that
S0133 make the majority of their landings, measured by volume, into ports in their territory; b. Vessels may, in general, apply to change country of Administration
S0134 only where there has been a change in fishing pattern such that the majority of landings, measured by volume, over a period of 12 months prior to the application, have been made into the Administration the vessel wishes to change to.
S0135 On approval of a change of Administration, the vessel will have to reregister to a port in the area of the new Administration; c. Where an Administration (“the original Administration”) grants approval
S0136 for a vessel to move to another Administration (“the receiving
S0137 Administration”), in accordance with sub paragraph 3(b) the receiving Administration will, on receipt of a request from the original Administration, add to the licence of the vessel that has moved a condition that requires the vessel to make the majority of its landings into the UK, measured by volume, outside the territory of the original Administration.
S0138 This condition will apply for the remainder of the calendar year during which the vessel changes country of Administration and in the three calendar years that follow.
S0139 Where a vessel subject to such a condition is found to have breached the condition, the receiving Administration will suspend the licence of the vessel until it re-registers to a port in the original Administration and will thereafter transfer Administration of the vessel to the original Administration; d.
S0140 The port of Administration of vessels that do not make the majority of
S0141 their landings into the UK will be considered on a case by case basis; and, e. Administrations may agree not to apply the arrangements in this
S0142 paragraph in relation to the moves of vessels 10 of metres and under.
S0143 EU fishing authorisations
S0144 4. Where there are quantitative restrictions in EU law on the number of fishing authorisations that the UK may issue for example to fish for Albacore Tuna, Administrations will agree on a case by case basis the way in which such authorisations will be divided between their vessels.
S0145 There will be no change to the distribution of existing authorisations without the prior agreement of all four Administrations.
S0146 National fishing authorisations or other restrictions.
S0147 5. Administrations may introduce bespoke licensing conditions and restrictions for their own fleets.
S0148 Where licences containing such devolved conditions/restrictions are transferred to vessels in another Administration, or vessels with a restricted licence move to another Administration, the receiving Administration may decide (including in response to a request from the transferring Administration) whether to continue to apply the licence restriction imposed by the previous Administration.
S0149 Whether it does or not, if the licence subsequently returns to the original Administration that Administration may re- impose the original restriction.
S0150 Quota
S0151 6.
S0152 The Administrations note that this Concordat involves an agreement to allocate amounts of quota to each Administration.
S0153 Such allocations do not constitute a permanent split of UK fishing opportunities.
S0154 From January 2013, the basis of allocation to Administrations of quotas allocated to the UK will, unless exceptionally agreed otherwise, be as set out below:
S0155 a. For vessels whose licences are assigned FQA units, allocations will be
S0156 calculated each year based on the FQAs attached to licences issued by each Administration at 1 January in the year of the allocation; b.
S0157 For under 10 metre vessels, allocations will be split between
S0158 Administrations on the basis of recorded landings by 10mu fishing vessels of each nationality in the period 2008-2012; c. Calculations will be based on the allocations granted to the UK after
S0159 any post-Council swaps and any subsequent UK adjustments are carried out; d.
S0160 The quota allocations resulting from the above calculations will be
S0161 attributed to each Administration based on the nationality of licences; and, e. Each Administration will then be responsible for allocating and
S0162 managing the resultant allocations and distributing them to groups for which it has nominally been given quota allocations, either in full or subject to any adjustments that it wishes first to make.
S0163 7.
S0164 The general principles in the preceding paragraph are subject to the following additional rules, which apply to the “sector” – those vessels in the membership of a PO:
S0165 a. There will be no new restrictions on PO membership.
S0166 POs may
S0167 therefore receive quota from more than one Administration and there will be no restriction on vessels licensed by one Administration fishing against quota received by a PO from another; b. POs may account separately for quota uptake purposes to each
S0168 Administration which issues it with quota allocation.
S0169 Alternatively, POs receiving allocations from more than one Administration may opt to manage these allocations collectively; Where POs elect to manage quota collectively, Administrations will act in concert when necessary to 'close' fisheries to prevent over-fishing; and, c. Quota, once allocated to a PO or other management group, is within its
S0170 control and it will be free to utilise it in any way authorised by Administrations' quota management rules, including in swaps with another PO of quota awarded by another Administration.

S0172 FQA Units
S0173 8.
S0174 The UK FQA register shows holdings of FQA units as they relate to fishing licences (including PO dummy licences) and enables transfers of FQA units between licences within the terms of Administrations’ rules.
S0175 9.
S0176 The Administrations reiterate that FQAs do not provide any right to a share of UK quota.
S0177 Administrations acknowledge nonetheless that FQA holdings involve at present a general expectation of receiving a share of UK annual quotas.
S0178 Administrations also acknowledge that there is a trade in FQA units.
S0179 This Concordat provides for Administrations to make annual allocations either in full, or by first making adjustments once it has received its allocated amount of
S0180 quota.
S0181 In providing for this possibility, each Administration acknowledges that any contemplation of allocation systems with different bases must take into account (in addition to other relevant factors which may be of equal significance) the interests of persons that have made investments in FQAs.
S0182 10.
S0183 Administrations may give notice of their intention to alter the basis on which the quotas allocated to them by the UK Government are distributed.
S0184 In such circumstances the Administration in question may temporarily prohibit the transfer of some or all of the FQA units from those licences under its jurisdiction until the expiry of the notice period, which will be agreed by the four Administrations.
S0185 Unless agreed otherwise, this prohibition will only apply to the number and type of FQA units affected by the proposed alteration.
S0186 The other Administrations will honour such prohibitions.
S0187 Any FQA units withheld from individual licences as a result of such action will for the purposes of the annual allocation exercise be treated as belonging to the relevant Administration and quota deriving from those units will be attributed to that Administration in accordance with paragraph 6.
S0188 11.
S0189 Administrations may also exercise discretion in general about whether to approve applications for the transfer of FQA units from their licences to licences issued by other Fisheries Administrations.
S0190 Such discretion may be exercised in relation to the transfer of units from a licence or a licence entitlement but where vessels are permitted to move administration under paragraph 3, FQA units attached to such vessel licences may also be transferred.
S0191 Where a condition is in place under paragraph 3(c), the receiving Administration shall not allow the vessel to transfer any FQA units from its licence until the expiry of any such condition.
S0192 12.
S0193 These rules are without prejudice to and must be read alongside Administrations' quota management rules.
